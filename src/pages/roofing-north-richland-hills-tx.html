December 13, 2022, along Sayers Lane

Roofing in North Richland Hills, TX got a hard lesson before sunrise on a Tuesday in December 2022. A tornado crossed the city that morning, and the National Weather Service later rated it EF-1, with peak winds near 90 mph and a damage path of about a mile and a half.

News reports put its start near Sayers Lane just west of Davis Boulevard. North Richland Hills police described the damage running from around Smithfield and Starnes roads northeast toward Precinct Line and North Tarrant Parkway, and said about 20 homes and businesses were hit. It was one of many tornadoes counted across North Texas that morning.

An EF-1 does not flatten neighborhoods, but it peels shingles, lifts ridge caps and drops limbs, and a roof that looks fine from the driveway can leak for months afterward. What North Richland Hills expects when the old roof comes off

The city's Building Inspections FAQ goes further than most about the work itself. These are the points a homeowner can hold a roofer to.

  1. A registered contractor. Anyone doing work that needs permits and inspections must be registered with the city through the NRH E-Portal. Owners whose home carries a homestead exemption with the appraisal district may do their own work; every other roofing permit goes only to a registered contractor.
  2. A permit when decking is involved. The current FAQ says replacing shingles alone on a one or two family home needs no permit, while a re-roof that repairs or replaces decking does. Because an older FAQ said otherwise, confirm with the city.
  3. Two layers means a full tear-off. A roof carrying two or more layers of shingles must be stripped completely before new shingles go on.
  4. New underlayment. The underlayment must be replaced whenever the roof is re-shingled.
  5. Surprises get reported. If damaged decking or rafters appear after tear-off, the contractor must contact the city to modify the permit, and the field inspector has the final say.

Reading an NRH roofing quote

Because the city ties so much to decking and layers, a good quote here answers four things in writing. How many layers are on the roof today. What underlayment goes on. What one sheet of replacement decking costs if bad wood is found. And who will update the permit if that happens.

If a quote is silent on any of those, ask before you sign. A roofer who already works in North Richland Hills should not be surprised by the question, and the answers make it much easier to compare two bids line by line.

Questions for a roofer working in North Richland Hills

  • What business name is your NRH registration under, and does it match this contract?
  • How many layers of shingles are on my roof right now, and will you strip all of them?
  • Which underlayment are you putting down, by product name?
  • If you find soft decking, how will you show it to me, and will you update the city permit?
  • For a commercial roof: what insulation goes back on, and is it listed as its own line?
  • How do I get a copy of your liability insurance certificate from your agent?
