April 11, 2016

Anyone who talks about roofing in Wylie, TX eventually gets to the same evening. On April 11, 2016, a storm dropped hail that FOX 4 described as baseball-sized across the city. The National Weather Service says about 80% of Wylie's homes were affected and damage passed $300 million.

CBS Texas quoted Mayor Eric Hogue estimating that roughly 12,000 homes were damaged. Hail went through roofs and soaked the rooms below. Blue tarps covered house after house. The city closed its Public Safety Building because of hail damage, and Wylie ISD called off classes the next day. CoreLogic later put losses from the Wylie storm at $246.8 million.

Recovery was slow. NBC 5 found a Wylie homeowner still waiting on repairs nearly nine months after the storm.

What the storm taught about Wylie roofs

Decking can be punctured

Roofing investigators who studied the damage told NBC 5 that stones the size of baseballs or larger went through the wood decking on many Wylie roofs, not just the shingles.

Overlays did worse

The same team said that overlays, a new layer of shingles nailed over the old one to save the tear-off cost, weaken the new layer's resistance to hail, and that new shingles do best on a solid deck.

Rush jobs age fast

With thousands of roofs to replace at once, work stretched across months. A roof installed in that crunch deserves a closer look at its flashing, vents and nailing.

Ten years on: where Wylie roofs stand

  1. 2016 onward. Damaged homes were re-roofed over the following months and years, many through insurance claims.
  2. Since then. Each spring adds its own hail and wind. Small bruises do not always leak right away.
  3. 2026. Those post-storm roofs are about ten years old. Asphalt shingles in North Texas heat often show wear well before the number on the package, so this is a sensible point to have one inspected.
  4. Before the next storm. Know your roof's age, whether it was a full tear-off, and what your policy says about wind and hail deductibles. Having those answers ready makes the next claim simpler.

Between two lakes, and growing

Wylie sits about 5 miles south of Lavon Lake, between it and Lake Ray Hubbard, and the city spreads across Collin, Dallas and Rockwall counties. Lavon Dam and the choice of Wylie for the North Texas Municipal Water District's offices helped the town grow by 1960, but the big wave came later. The census counted 15,132 residents in 2000, 41,427 in 2010 and 57,526 in 2020.

So most Wylie houses were built in the 2000s, and many were already on their second roof within ten years because of the 2016 hail. The older blocks near downtown have their own history: a tornado struck downtown on Mother's Day 1993, and the district was later renovated around its surviving century-old buildings.

Wylie permits and the roofer's paperwork

  • Permit required. The City of Wylie lists roofing replacement, residential and commercial, among projects that need a building permit.
  • Turnaround. The city puts residential permit review at 5 to 10 business days, so a roofer should file before it schedules your tear-off.
  • Where to ask. Building Inspection takes registration and permit questions at permits@wylietexas.gov and 972-516-6100, option 3. Ask there whether the roofer you are considering has filed with the city.
  • No state license. Texas does not license roofers, so the city's records and your own checks are what you have.

Questions to ask a Wylie roofer

  • Will you tear my roof down to the deck, and can you tell whether the last roof was an overlay?
  • If you find hail punctures or soft spots in the decking, how will you document and price them?
  • Will you file the Wylie permit before scheduling, and who requests the inspection?
  • Would an impact-rated shingle make sense here, and what would it change on my insurance? (Ask your agent too.)
  • Have your liability insurer send me the certificate directly.
