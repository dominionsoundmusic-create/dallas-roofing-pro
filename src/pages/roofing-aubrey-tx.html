Horse Country, U.S.A., with new rooftops

Roofing in Aubrey, TX covers two very different kinds of property. The town calls itself Horse Country, U.S.A., and Aubrey ISD's history explains why: sandy, fertile land and a mild climate drew horse ranchers here long before the subdivisions came.

Then the subdivisions came. Aubrey counted 2,595 residents in the 2010 census and 5,006 in 2020, nearly double. Ray Roberts Dam, finished in 1986 on the Elm Fork of the Trinity River, created the lake to the north, and large master-planned communities have filled in the land around town. Sandbrock Ranch alone was planned for about 2,600 homes on roughly 800 acres.

How Aubrey handles roof permits

  1. Roofing is a trade permit. Aubrey's building permit list groups roofing with electrical, HVAC and plumbing under one trade permit category.
  2. Everything goes by email. The city asks for the application and supporting documents as a single complete PDF sent to permitting@aubreytx.gov.
  3. Allow time for review. The city says plan reviews are typically finished in 10 to 14 business days, so a roofer who promises to start tomorrow should explain how the permit fits.
  4. The office has moved. In August 2026 the city said its Building Permits office is now at 510 Rockhill Road. Older forms still show the Main Street City Hall address.

The residential application says contractor registration is required at submittal for general, plumbing, electrical and mechanical contractors. It does not name roofers, so ask the city whether your roofer must register and ask the roofer to confirm it in writing.

Two kinds of Aubrey roof calls

Newer subdivision homes

Most roofs in the newer neighborhoods are architectural shingles that are only a few years old. The usual call is hail or wind damage, and the first questions are about warranties: the builder's, the shingle maker's and the installer's. Keep the closing paperwork handy, and check HOA rules on shingle colors before any replacement.

Ranch houses, barns and arenas

Horse properties bring metal roofs on barns and shelters, plus older homes with their own roof history. Tell the roofing company about every building you want looked at, and ask your insurance agent which structures your policy covers before you file.

What the storm record shows

Aubrey's storm history in public records is thin, and this page does not stretch it. Storm report listings show hail about 1.75 inches across, golf ball size, near Aubrey on April 8, 2024, and reports from 2025 describe heavy tree damage in town. Hail that size is large enough to bruise shingles and dent vents and gutters, so it is worth a look after any storm like it.
