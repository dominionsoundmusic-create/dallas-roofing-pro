Southlake's roof replacement rules in plain terms

A roofer in Southlake, TX works under one of the clearest re-roof rulebooks in northeast Tarrant County. In a short guide titled "Roof Replacement: What You Need to Know," the city lays out the whole process from registration to final inspection.

StepWhat Southlake requiresWhat it means for you
RegistrationThe contractor registers online as a general contractor, and general contractor registration renews each year.Ask whether the registration is current for this year.
PermitA building permit is required for a roof replacement and is applied for online.No permit, no tear-off. Ask for the permit number.
Permit cardThe card must be posted at the property in plain view from the road.You and your neighbors can see the job is permitted.
Final inspectionWhen the work is done, the contractor requests a final inspection in the city portal, in person or by photos.Ask how the final went and keep the record.
Photo finalPhotos of the house address, exterior roof photos from ground level, and vent photos.Ask for copies; they document the finished roof.
ExpirationA permit expires after 180 days without an inspection or activity.A job that stalls can leave you with no valid permit.

The city also posts monthly permit reports with the permit number, address, job value and contractor. Questions go to Building Inspections at 817-748-8236 or through Southlake Serves.

A town named for a lake

People farmed this stretch of northeast Tarrant County from the 1840s, but Southlake as a city is younger than the lake beside it. The Army Corps of Engineers finished Grapevine Lake in 1952. Four years later, on September 25, 1956, residents met at the 1919 Carroll School and voted to incorporate, in part to stay out of reach of annexation by Hurst. The new town covered just 1.62 square miles, and its name came from the lake to the north.

Voters created Carroll ISD in 1959, and the area stayed largely rural until DFW Airport opened in the 1970s. The houses that came afterward are mostly large, and many sit in neighborhoods with homeowners associations and architectural review.

Trees, power lines and storm days in Southlake

Many Southlake homes get power from Tri-County Electric Cooperative. In August 2026, Mayor Shawn McCaskill filed a complaint with the Public Utility Commission over repeated outages, and utility officials blamed tree branches touching overhead lines in the Southlake and Keller service areas.

Know your provider

Your bill shows who delivers your power. Tri-County members can report an outage at 817-444-3201 or by texting OUTAGE to 25069. Oncor's outage line is 888-313-4747.

Limbs and roofs

The same mature trees that brush power lines hang over roofs. A limb that scrapes shingles in every wind wears them down, and one that falls in a storm can break decking. Ask a tree service, not a roofer, to trim near lines.

Southlake's hail record in the Storm Prediction Center logs is lighter than some neighbors': quarter-size hail a mile north of town on April 14, 2011, and again on May 27, 2024, the same night baseball-size stones were reported around Euless and Bedford. On December 13, 2022, an EF-1 tornado that tore through Grapevine lifted at the Grapevine Lake shoreline.

Before you hire a Southlake roofer

  • Ask whether its Southlake general contractor registration is current this year.
  • Get the permit number before tear-off and look for the card facing the street.
  • Ask whether the final will be in person or by photos, and request copies.
  • If your HOA reviews roofs, have the roofer supply the product sheet and color before ordering.
  • Ask the roofer's insurance agent to send you its liability certificate.
  • On a claim job, find the deductible notice Texas requires in the contract.

Texas Property Code Section 202.011 keeps an HOA from banning shingles built mainly to stand up to wind and hail, or energy-saving and solar shingles, as long as they look like ones already used or allowed in the subdivision.
