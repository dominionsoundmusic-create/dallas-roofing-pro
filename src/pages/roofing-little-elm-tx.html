A lake town that grew in one generation

Choosing a roofing company in Little Elm usually means dealing with a roof that is younger than you might think. The town counted 3,646 people in 2000, 25,898 in 2010 and 46,453 in 2020, and the Census Bureau estimated 58,496 by mid-2023.

Little Elm sits on the northern and eastern shores of Lewisville Lake, with 66 miles of shoreline by the town's economic development count. KERA reported in 2019 that the town's population took off after it invested in lake living. That growth means most houses here are from the last 25 years, many of them two-story homes with architectural shingle roofs that are now reaching the age of a first replacement or a first big hail claim.

The Little Elm roofing permit question

People search for a Little Elm roofing permit because the answer is not posted on one simple page. Here is what the town's own material says.

The rule in the code

The building code Little Elm applies says any owner, agent or contractor who wants to alter or repair a building must first apply to the code official and get the required permit for the work.

Who issues it

Building Safety, part of the Development Services Department, reviews, issues and inspects permits. Its office is at 100 West Eldorado Parkway.

Where it is filed

Applications, contractor registrations and yearly registration renewals all run through MyGov, the town's online request center.

How to get a straight answer

Call Building Safety at 214-975-0456 or email permits@littleelm.org with your address and the work planned. Ask before tear-off, not after.

The town's 2020 residential packet listed a $100 contractor registration fee. Fees change, so treat that as a guide and confirm the current amount with the town.

Four reasons Little Elm homeowners call about roofs

The first replacement

Houses built during the 2000s boom are now old enough that their original shingles are wearing out, especially on south and west slopes that take the most afternoon sun.

Hail on March 14, 2024

Reader photos of hail in Little Elm ran on NBC DFW that night, and nearby reports logged stones up to about 2.5 inches. Damage from a storm like that may not show from the street.

Open-water wind

Homes near the shoreline can catch wind that has crossed open water with little to slow it. Wind lifts shingle edges and ridge caps, which later lets rain in.

HOA approval

Many newer neighborhoods have design rules on shingle color and style. Ask the HOA what is approved before signing, so the roofer orders the right product.
