Typing "roofing company in Dallas" into a search bar returns hundreds of names, ads that look alike, and a lot of door knockers who showed up after the last storm. DFW Expert Roofers does no roof work itself. It does not inspect, quote, repair or replace anything, and it does not handle insurance claims. Why roofs wear out fast around Dallas-Fort Worth

North Texas throws nearly every kind of roof weather at a house within a single year. Knowing which one hit yours tells a roofer where to look first.

Spring hail

The worst storms usually arrive between March and June. The May 5, 1995 Mayfest storm dropped softball-size hail on the festival crowd in Fort Worth and its damage across Tarrant and Dallas counties is commonly put at about $2 billion, though the earliest estimates were lower. On June 13, 2012, the National Weather Service in Fort Worth logged hail as large as baseballs and softballs across Dallas and Collin counties, smashing car windows and skylights in North Dallas. Hail bruises shingles and knocks off granules in ways that are hard to see from the yard.

Straight-line wind and tornadoes

Thunderstorm winds can peel ridge caps and crease shingles over whole neighborhoods, and tornadoes have struck the metro many times, including the December 26, 2015 tornado that crossed Sunnyvale, Garland and Rowlett and the EF3 tornado that tore through North Dallas on October 20, 2019. Wind damage often shows up as lifted tabs and missing shingles along edges and ridges.

Summer heat

Long stretches of triple-digit days bake asphalt shingles and dry out sealants around pipes and vents. Attic ventilation matters as much as the shingle itself, and a poorly vented attic can shorten a roof's life.

Ice and freezes

Winter storms are rare but rough. In February 2021 snow, sleet and freezing rain coated the region, and DFW Airport fell to minus 2 degrees on February 16. Ice can back water up under shingles at the eaves, and ice-laden limbs can fall on the roof.

Texas also leads the country in large hail. Citing the Insurance Information Institute, Texas Public Radio reported 902 major hail events (stones an inch across or larger) in Texas in 2025, more than any other state. If a storm has passed over your street, it is worth having a roofer look before the next one does.

After the roofer looks at the roof

You should get a written estimate that lists what will be removed and installed, the shingle or panel product, how the decking and flashing will be handled, cleanup, the permit if your city needs one, and the warranty terms. With a claim, you deal with your insurer directly. The roofer may point out damage to the adjuster, yet it has no standing to bargain over the claim.

Take the time you need. A good roofer will not pressure you to sign on the spot, especially after a hailstorm when crews from out of town are knocking on doors.

Five things to settle before you sign with any roofer

  1. Insurance you can confirm. Have the roofer's insurance agent send you a general liability certificate directly, and call the agent if anything looks off.
  2. The permit question. Many DFW cities, Plano and Dallas among them, require a permit for a re-roof, and some require roofers to register with the city. Ask who pulls the permit and get the number.
  3. A complete written contract. Scope, materials by brand and line, decking price per sheet, start date, cleanup, payment schedule and warranty, with no blank spaces.
  4. No deductible games. State law bars any contractor from waiving, refunding or absorbing a deductible. An offer to do so is a reason to call someone else.
  5. Payment tied to progress. Keep most of the money back at the start, and release the last payment only for a finished job with invoice and warranty in hand.
