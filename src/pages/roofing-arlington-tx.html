Arlington writes down who works on its roofs

Arlington is one of the few big cities in Tarrant County where you can look up a roofer before it ever climbs your ladder. The city requires a building permit for most roof replacements and requires every contractor hired to do construction to register with it, and both records sit in a public search on ArlingtonPermits.com.

That paper trail matters in a city that has taken an EF-2 tornado and some of the costliest hail in recent North Texas memory. After every big storm, trucks from all over show up on Arlington streets. A registration lookup and a permit history are the quickest way to tell a company that works here from one that just arrived.

Check an Arlington roofer before you sign

The City of Arlington publishes the records that answer most of the questions you would ask a stranger at your door. These steps use them.

  1. Look up the registration. Arlington says roofing contractors must be registered with the city to get a building permit, and anyone you hire under a verbal or written contract to do construction on your property must be registered. Search the company's business registration on ArlingtonPermits.com.
  2. Look at its permit history. The same public search shows permits. A company that says it roofs all over Arlington should have pulled permits here. If it has none, ask why.
  3. Ask whether your job needs a permit. Since November 1, 2011, Arlington has required a permit to replace roofing. The city lists replacing 10 percent or less of the roof covering as work that does not need one. A full re-roof is well past that line.
  4. Get the permit in the roofer's name. The registered contractor applies, pays and schedules inspections through the portal. Ask for the permit number in writing.
  5. Hold the last payment. Pay the balance after the work is finished and inspected, and ask for lien releases from suppliers and subcontractors so an unpaid bill does not land on your house.

Planning and Development Services answers permit questions at 817-459-6502, and the One Start Center is on the second floor of City Hall, 101 W. Abram Street. Rules change, so confirm with the city before work starts.

Why Arlington homeowners call the line

Roofs from the 1980s building boom

A housing profile published through the city's economic development office puts the median year built for an Arlington single-family home at 1984, and says more than 65 percent of the housing stock was built before 1990. Many of those roofs are on their second or third covering, and owners want to know whether a repair or a new roof makes sense.

Older streets near Downtown and East Arlington

The same profile notes the oldest homes sit near Downtown and in East Arlington. Older houses can hide tired decking, past layers and flashing that was patched more than replaced, which is worth knowing before a quote.

Newer homes in Viridian and south Arlington

Newer construction is concentrated in Viridian and parts of south Arlington. Owners there often call after their first big hail claim, or to ask about impact-resistant shingles for the next roof.

Wanting a local company, not a storm crew

After a big storm, the knocks on the door start fast. Callers want to be put through to a company that works in Arlington year round, then check that company on the city's registration search before they sign anything.

What a solid Arlington roofing contract shows

Arlington's 2016 guidance listed what a contract should include, and it lines up with what any careful homeowner should ask for. Compare each bid against this list:

  • The contractor's name and a permanent street address, not just a cell number.
  • A detailed scope: tear-off, decking replaced and at what price per sheet, underlayment, flashing, vents and drip edge.
  • A payment schedule, with no large cash payment up front. Paying for materials once they are delivered is normal.
  • A completion date and who hauls away the debris.
  • The workmanship warranty in writing, and the shingle maker's warranty registered in your name.
  • The Arlington permit number, and lien releases at the end.

If the roof is part of an insurance claim, talk to your insurer before you sign, and let the adjuster see the damage first.
