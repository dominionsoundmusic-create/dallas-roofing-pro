Small town, big hail

Roofing in Argyle, TX comes up most after hail. On March 26, 2017, a storm that lasted about five minutes left roofs across Argyle damaged badly enough that many had to be replaced outright, according to the Argyle High student newspaper, The Talon News. Car windows were smashed all over town.

Argyle is a small town about six miles southwest of Denton. It counted 4,403 residents in the 2020 census, and the Census Bureau estimated 5,859 by mid-2023, so it is growing quickly. Homes range from horse properties with pasture, stalls and arenas to newer houses in master-planned communities such as Harvest and Canyon Falls that carry Argyle addresses.

Argyle's contractor rule: register and show $500,000 in coverage

Most towns ask for a contractor's insurance certificate. Argyle puts a number on it.

Who must register

All contractors, before performing services within town limits. Registration is yearly. A homeowner doing work on their own home does not register but must still follow the codes.

What they file

A registration application, a valid trade license or a driver's license where there is no state trade license (the case for roofers), and a certificate of insurance with at least $500,000 in coverage.

What it costs the roofer

$125 for the first registration, then $50 for each yearly renewal, per the town's fee schedule.

Where to ask

The Permits Department at 308 Denton Street, open weekdays 8 a.m. to 4:30 p.m., phone 940-464-7273. Applications run through the town's iWorQ portal.

On permits, the town's rule is broad: a permit is needed before new construction, an addition, or the alteration or repair of an existing building. Its FAQ list of common permit projects does not mention roofs, so call the permits office before a re-roof and get a clear answer for your address.

Acreage roofs need a wider look

On a property with a barn, a shop building and a house, a hailstorm rarely hits only one roof. When you call, mention every structure you want checked so the roofing company can plan its visit. Ask whether it handles both shingle and metal roofs, how it documents damage to outbuildings, and whether your insurance policy lists those buildings at all. Some policies treat barns and other structures differently from the home, so read yours or ask your agent.

In a master-planned neighborhood the questions shift. HOA design rules can limit shingle colors and styles, so ask the HOA what is approved before signing. Under section 202.011 of the Texas Property Code, an HOA has limited power to block certain impact-resistant or energy-efficient shingles, a point worth raising with the roofer and the HOA together.

Ask an Argyle roofer

  • Are you registered with the Town of Argyle this year, and can your insurer send me the certificate you filed?
  • Is my property inside town limits, and what permit, if any, will you pull?
  • Will you inspect the barn and outbuildings on the same visit?
  • Which storm date do your photos and notes tie the damage to?
  • Do you install metal as well as shingles, and how do the warranties differ?
