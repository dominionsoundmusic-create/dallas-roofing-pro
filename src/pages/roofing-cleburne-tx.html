Cleburne after 9:30 p.m. on May 15, 2013

Ask anyone in Cleburne about roofs and the 2013 tornado comes up quickly. At about 9:30 that night an EF3 tornado moved through the Johnson County seat. The National Weather Service put its peak winds near 140 mph, its path at about 8.5 miles and its width at roughly 1,060 yards, more than half a mile.

The worst of it fell just east of Lake Pat Cleburne, the reservoir that supplies the city's water. CBS Texas reported that hundreds of homes lost their roofs, debris covered whole blocks, fences were flattened, and about 600 families were affected. The mayor declared a local disaster. It was one of 16 tornadoes the weather service confirmed across North Texas from that outbreak.

Storms have kept coming since. Coverage of a later storm, written around the 13th anniversary of the tornado, reported hail up to 4 inches falling across most of Johnson County and more than 1,000 homes losing power, about half of them in the Cleburne area.

Older roofs in the county seat

Cleburne started in 1862 as Camp Henderson near Buffalo Creek and took its current name, for General Patrick Cleburne, when it incorporated in 1871. The Santa Fe railroad shops later became its largest employer for about a century, and that history is still visible in the buildings. The Cleburne Downtown Historic District, listed on the National Register, counts 89 contributing buildings from 1877 to 1968. A National Register nomination describes Prairie Avenue as still lined with substantial homes from the late 1800s and early 1900s.

Roofs on houses that old raise questions that newer suburbs rarely face: several layers of old shingles, original board decking, brick chimneys with worn flashing, and porch roofs at a lower pitch than the main roof. A careful roofer looks at all of it before quoting. Cleburne had 31,352 residents at the 2020 census, and plenty of them live in newer neighborhoods too, where the questions are about hail and a first replacement.

What Cleburne requires of a roofer

From the City of Cleburne's Quick Guide to Residential Construction (October 2025) and its Building Inspection FAQ.

  • Every contractor registers. All contractors must be registered with the City of Cleburne; the guide lists the fee as $100.
  • Re-decking needs a permit. A roofing permit is required for re-decking an existing roof, and only a registered contractor can pull it. The guide lists the roofing (decking only) fee as $30 plus a $20 administrative fee.
  • Every name on the permit. The general contractor and all subcontractors must be registered, current and listed before the permit is issued.
  • 180 days to the first inspection. After that the permit expires and fees must be paid again.
  • Online requests. Permits and inspections run through the city's online portal; inspection requests made before 4 p.m. are scheduled for the next day.

Building Inspections is on the ground floor of City Hall, 10 N. Robinson Street, open Monday through Friday during business hours. Fees can change, so confirm them there. Addresses outside city limits in Johnson County are not under this office.

Before you hire in Cleburne

  1. Get the registered name. Ask what name the company registered under with the City of Cleburne, then confirm it at Building Inspections.
  2. Ask about layers and decking. On an older house, find out how many roofs are already on it and what replacing board or plywood decking will cost per sheet.
  3. Ask who pulls the permit. If decking is replaced, a registered contractor must. Make the fee part of the written price.
  4. Get insurance proof from the source. A certificate sent straight from the insurer is worth more than a photo of one.
  5. Keep your own record. Photos from the ground and inside, dated, help if you file a claim after the next storm.
