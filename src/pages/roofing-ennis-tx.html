Ennis, from the polka halls to the I-45 corridor

Ennis sits in eastern Ellis County along Interstate 45 and counted 20,159 residents in the 2020 census. It is one of the largest Czech communities in Texas, home of the National Polka Festival every Memorial Day weekend and the Bluebonnet Trails each spring. Its downtown historic district holds dozens of buildings from the railroad era.

A small city means fewer roofing companies based in town, and after a big storm many of the trucks in driveways come from somewhere else. Ennis has a rule that helps sort them: the city wants work done by a contractor that is registered with it.

What the 2013 tornado taught Ennis

On the night of Wednesday, May 15, 2013, a tornado formed west of Interstate 45, crossed the highway south of Ennis Avenue and stayed on the ground for about six miles. The National Weather Service rated it EF-1, with peak winds near 90 mph. Downtown took the worst of it: buildings in the historic district lost roofs and walls, and city officials counted 25 homes and 40 commercial buildings severely damaged. No one died, and the only reported injury came from flying glass.

Some of the damaged downtown buildings drew demolition requests in the months that followed. The city instead looked for buyers who would restore them and offered incentives to do it, and the historic district came back.

It was not the first time. In March 2004 the Fort Worth weather office surveyed an F1 tornado that unroofed a mobile home, overturned semi trailers in southern Ennis and tore most of the roof off a warehouse and office building along Interstate 45.

The lesson for homeowners is simple. Even a tornado at the low end of the scale can strip roofs, and the weeks after a storm are when hurried, unpermitted work happens. A registered contractor and a permit on file give you a record if something goes wrong.

Getting an Ennis roof job on the books

  1. Call the city before work begins. Planning, Development and Inspections asks owners to call 972-875-6442 to find out what a project requires.
  2. Confirm the roofer is registered. The city keeps a contractor registration application and says work must be done by a registered contractor, or by the owner under a homestead permit.
  3. Apply through the portal. Since June 5, 2024, Ennis takes every building permit application through its online portal.
  4. Schedule the inspections. The permit holder books inspections, again only through the portal. If the roofer holds the permit, ask when each inspection is set.
  5. Pay the balance after it passes. Keep the last payment until the work is finished and any inspection is done.

Before you sign with a roofer in Ennis

  • Proof the company is registered with the City of Ennis.
  • Who will apply for the permit, and the permit number once issued.
  • A liability insurance certificate mailed or emailed to you by the insurer.
  • A written scope that names the shingle or membrane, the underlayment and the flashing.
  • For a flat roof on a downtown building, how water will drain and what happens at the parapet walls.
  • A payment schedule with no large cash deposit.
