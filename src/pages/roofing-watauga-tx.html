A town built in a hurry, re-roofed in waves

Roofing in Watauga, TX follows the town's growth curve. Watauga was incorporated in 1958, and the city's own budget history says it grew rapidly in the late 1970s and early 1980s.

  • 1958: incorporation, with a lot deeded that April for a fire station and City Hall.
  • 1976: a population of about 7,050.
  • 1990: about 20,000 residents.

The name is older than the town: it comes from a Cherokee word, though its exact meaning has been lost.

When most of a town is built within a decade, its roofs age on the same clock. Houses that went up around 1980 are now past forty years old, and most are on a second or third roof. Each of those re-roofs may or may not have stripped the old layers, replaced tired decking or fixed the attic ventilation, and that history decides what the next roof needs.

Permits and registration in Watauga

Watauga handles building paperwork through its Public Works Department rather than a separate building office.

  1. Registration. The Public Works action center takes contractor registrations and renewals. Ask the roofer whether its registration with Watauga is current.
  2. Permit. Permit submittals go in online or in person at Public Works, 7800 Virgil Anthony Sr. Boulevard, inside the water tower. The city's monthly permit reports show residential roof permits being issued to roofing contractors.
  3. Inspection. Inspection requests go through the same action center. Ask your roofer to tell you when the inspection is booked and how it went.
  4. Your record. Keep the permit number, the contract, the final invoice and the shingle warranty registration together. They show the next buyer, or the next roofer, what was done.

The city says a homeowner who hires a contractor for permitted work should make sure that contractor holds a permit with Watauga. Requirements change, so a quick call to Public Works before the tear-off date is the surest check.

After a storm crosses Watauga

Spring storms move across northeast Tarrant County most years, and an older roof gives up shingles and granules more easily than a new one. Brittle tabs crack where a newer shingle would bend, and sealant strips that have baked for fifteen or twenty summers let go in a gust. None of that is always visible from the street, which is why a second look after a big storm is common here. Stay on the ground and check from the yard, photograph anything that came down and any new ceiling stain, and call your insurer to ask about your wind and hail deductible before deciding on a claim.
