Haltom Village grew up next door to Fort Worth

Roofing in Haltom City, TX means working on one of the older suburbs in northeast Tarrant County. The city's own history traces Haltom Village to 1932, when it was founded on ranch and farm land and named for G. W. Haltom, a Fort Worth jeweler.

Growth came from the Meadow Oaks development and from new highways that made the drive to Fort Worth, the rest of northeast Tarrant County and Dallas easier. Those state highways were routed past the old Birdville business district in 1932. The result today is a mix of long-settled residential streets and busy commercial corridors, with plenty of roofs that have been replaced more than once.

What the Haltom City roofing permit asks for

Haltom City keeps a dedicated Residential Roofing Permit application, plus a contractor registration form. Between them, they tell you what the city wants to see.

The materials

The permit form asks for the type of shingle, the type of felt and the thickness of the decking. Ask your roofer to put the same three items in your contract so the two match.

A registered roofer

All contractors must register with the city to receive permits. The registration form asks for a driver's license and a certificate of insurance naming Haltom City as the certificate holder.

A start date

The permit becomes invalid if the work it covers does not start within 180 days. The fee notes also list a higher charge when work begins without a permit.

Applications go through the city's online portal. Planning and Inspections is at City Hall, 4801 Haltom Road, and the department has listed weekday hours Monday through Thursday. Confirm current requirements with the city before work starts.

When the city sends a storm warning

Haltom City passes National Weather Service warnings along to residents through its Nixle alerts. A warning relayed in late August 2026 called for gusts to 60 mph and hail the size of quarters, and it told people to expect wind damage to roofs, siding and trees. That is a typical North Texas warm-season storm, and it is enough to loosen tabs on an older roof.

After a storm passes, stay on the ground. Walk the yard for pieces of shingle or vent caps, look at ceilings inside for new stains, and take dated photos of both. Then call your insurance company to ask how wind and hail claims work on your policy, and call a roofer.
