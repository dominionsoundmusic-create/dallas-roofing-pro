A city full of roofs from the same few years

Census counts tell the story of roofing in Allen. The city had roughly 19,000 people in 1990, about 43,600 in 2000 and more than 84,000 in 2010. By the 2020 census it had passed 104,000. Twin Creeks alone, the city's largest master-planned community, began development in 1993 and covers 2,400 acres.

Houses built in a rush get roofed in a rush, and those roofs age together. A shingle installed in 1998 or 2005 has had two decades of Collin County sun and storms. Many Allen homes are already on their second roof because hail forced the issue early. Others are still on the first and nearing the end.

Either way, the decision usually lands in the same week for a whole street, often right after a storm, and that is when the trouble starts: trucks, yard signs and contracts handed over at the door. A little preparation makes the choice of a roofing company in Allen much calmer.

Three rules to know before anyone knocks

Three business days

The FTC's Cooling-Off Rule lets you cancel most sales of $25 or more made at your home within three business days. The seller must tell you about that right. Send the cancellation in writing.

The deductible stays with you

State law forbids a roofer from forgiving, refunding or swallowing your deductible. An offer to "take care of it" is a reason to shut the door politely.

Nobody negotiates for you but you

Under Texas law, only a licensed public adjuster may negotiate a claim on your behalf, and a contractor cannot be the public adjuster on a house it may work on.

Many storm crews also hand over a "contingency agreement" that says they will do the job if the insurer approves the claim. It is still a contract. It can commit you to that company at whatever the claim pays before you have seen another estimate.

Allen's permit and contractor paperwork

  1. The contractor registers. Allen requires contractors to register with the city, at $50 a year, through its Citizen Self Service portal.
  2. The permit goes in. Roofing is on the city's list of improvements that need a permit. Applications are filed through the same portal, and only a registered contractor can be added to one.
  3. You confirm it. The city's residential page tells homeowners to make sure the contractor has applied for and received the permit before work begins.
  4. Inspections close it out. The city requires inspections on permitted work. Keep the permit number with your closing papers and roof warranty.

Allen Building Services takes questions at 214-509-4130 and permits@allentx.gov. City Hall is at 305 Century Parkway.

What usually prompts an Allen call

A yard sign war after hail

Half the street has signed with someone. The caller wants a local roofer to look before deciding, not the crew that happened to knock first.

A first roof from the 1990s

Homes from Allen's early growth years that dodged the worst storms are now showing curled tabs and bare granules. Owners want to know if replacement can wait.

A second roof that looks wrong

Some roofs replaced quickly after one storm have loose ridge caps or vents that leak. A fresh set of eyes can tell workmanship problems from new damage.

An HOA approval to sort out

Master-planned neighborhoods often have color and material rules. Callers want a roofer who will put the shingle line in writing so the HOA can approve it.

Questions that sort Allen roofers quickly

  • Does Allen's portal show your company as registered this year, and will your name be on the permit?
  • Where is your office, and which Allen or Collin County roofs from the past year can I drive by?
  • Will you put the shingle brand, line and color in the contract so my HOA can approve it?
  • How do you price decking that turns out to be damaged, and will I see photos first?
  • What do you collect up front, and what is due only after the final inspection?
  • Can your insurer email me a certificate of liability coverage?
