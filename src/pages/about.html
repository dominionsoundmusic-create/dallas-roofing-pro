What this is

DFW Expert Roofers is a phone number and a website. The number is answered at every hour by an automated assistant, which connects callers across Dallas-Fort Worth with an independent roofing company that is locally owned and works in their area. The website explains roofing in plain terms so callers know what to ask once a roofer arrives.

The line is a d/b/a of Dominion Digital Group. It has no crews, sells no materials, and does not inspect, quote or repair roofs. It also does not file or negotiate insurance claims; in Texas, only a licensed public adjuster may negotiate a claim on a homeowner's behalf, and a contractor may not act as one on a property it works on.

Why it exists

Roofing in North Texas is driven by weather. When a hailstorm crosses Collin County or a line of storms flattens fences in Tarrant County, thousands of homeowners need a roofer in the same week, and the yard signs and door knockers arrive fast. Some of those crews are local and careful. Some are gone before the first leak. Texas has no state roofing license to sort them out.

The line offers a simpler first step: one free call that reaches a company based in the area, plus guides that help you check that company, understand your insurance policy, and read an estimate.

Free to call

No charge to call or to be connected, and no bill from the line, ever. Roofing companies may pay a referral fee.

Answered around the clock

The assistant picks up nights, weekends and holidays, which matters in a region where many storms arrive in the evening.

Local first

Calls go to independent, locally owned roofing companies that work in Dallas-Fort Worth, not to a national call center.

How the guides and town pages are written

Every page on this site was researched and written for its own topic. The 67 town pages each draw on that town's own records: its permit office, the storms that hit it, and the neighborhoods people there know.

  • Sourced. Facts come from the National Weather Service, city websites, the Texas Department of Insurance, Texas statutes and established news outlets, and each page lists them.
  • Left out when unverified. If a claim could not be confirmed, it is not on the page.
  • No invented trust signals. No made-up reviews, licenses, awards, warranties of service or arrival times.
  • Safety first. No page tells you to climb on a roof or do roof work yourself.

What the line will not claim

{{ img('roofer-ladder-at-gutter.jpg', 'Roofer standing at the foot of an aluminum ladder set against the gutter of a brick home', 1200, 800) }}

You will not find promises here about how fast a roofer will show up, about a company holding a manufacturer credential, or about what your insurer will decide. Those depend on the roofing company, the shingle maker and your insurance company, and you should hear them from each of them, in writing.

What you will find is a straight answer about how the call works, and the questions worth asking before you sign anything.
