Between the interstate and the lake

Roofing in Corinth, TX is mostly about newer suburban homes in a city that grew fast. The 2010 census counted 19,935 people, the 2020 count was about 22,600, and the Census Bureau estimated 23,707 by 2023.

Corinth sits in southern Denton County along Interstate 35E, between Denton to the north and Lake Dallas and Hickory Creek to the east. Being a Denton County suburb has a practical side for roofs: the same spring storm lines that cross the county cross Corinth, and wind has been as much of a problem here as hail.

DFW Expert Roofers is a free phone line, not a contractor. A call is answered by an automated assistant and passed to an independent, locally owned roofing company that serves Corinth. That company looks at the roof and deals with the city.

Corinth's permit steps, in order

  1. The roofer registers in Civic Access. Corinth switched all new permits and plans to its Civic Access portal on March 27, 2025. Contractors register there, send the permit office a color copy of a driver's license plus an insurance certificate where applicable, and pay no registration fee. Even companies registered in the old system had to sign up again.
  2. The roof permit is applied for. The city lists a Roof Permit Application among its building forms. A 2023 fee ordinance prices residential re-roofs by square foot, while another city page lists a flat residential permit fee, so confirm the current number with Building Services.
  3. Work starts after the permit is issued. Ask for the permit number before the old shingles come off.
  4. Inspections are scheduled. Through Civic Access, or by phone at (940) 498-3273. General inspection questions go to (940) 498-3270.

Using Corinth's home repair grant program? Its rules require the contractor to give a written warranty of at least five years on roof repairs, and all permits must come from Building Inspections before work begins.

Wind and hail on the record in and around Corinth

An EF-1 just over the line in Hickory Creek

NBC DFW and Fox 4 reported a tornado that the National Weather Service rated EF-1, with winds of 85 to 95 mph on a half-mile path. It heavily damaged a car wash near I-35 and Swisher Road in Hickory Creek, Corinth's neighbor, and tore into the Kensington Square shopping center. Winds like that crease shingle tabs and pull loose ridge caps.

Golf ball hail, March 14, 2024

When supercells crossed Denton County that evening, Corinth residents sent NBC DFW photos of golf ball size hail, and the National Weather Service in Fort Worth listed Corinth among the places with stones of roughly 1.5 to 3 inches.

After a storm passes over Corinth

Wind damage is often visible from the yard: shingles on the lawn, a dark strip where tabs blew off, a gutter pulled away from the fascia, a limb resting on the roof. Hail is harder to judge from the ground, even when the stones were as big as the March 2024 ones. Either way, take dated photos from the yard and from inside, write down what time the storm came through, and call your insurer to ask how its claim process works before anyone starts repairs.

Before you sign with a Corinth roofer

  • Is your company registered in Corinth's Civic Access portal under the same name as this contract?
  • Who files for the roof permit, and can I have a copy of it before tear-off?
  • Does the estimate list replacement shingles, starter strip, ridge cap and any decking separately?
  • What warranty do you give on your own work, in writing, and how is the shingle warranty registered?
  • For a storm claim, will you give me your photos and measurements to send my insurer?
