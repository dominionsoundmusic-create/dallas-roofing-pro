A small county with a lot of new roofs

Roofing in Rockwall happens in a place that is short on land and long on growth. Rockwall County covers only about 147 square miles, the smallest county in Texas, and the City of Rockwall is its seat.

Lake Ray Hubbard bounds the county on the west, and that lakeside setting has drawn steady building for decades. The 2020 census counted 107,819 people in the county. In the year after that count, the county grew by nearly 8 percent, one of the fastest rates in the state at the time.

Growth like that means two very different roofing jobs side by side. Newer subdivisions are full of first roofs, some reaching the age where hail and summer heat start to show, while older houses have been re-roofed before and bring questions about layers and decking. Both kinds of homeowner call for the same reasons: a storm went through, a stain appeared, or a sale or insurance renewal is coming up.

Looking up a Rockwall roof permit from home

The City of Rockwall is unusually open about its permits. Here is how a homeowner can use that before, during and after a roof job.

  1. Know the rule. Rockwall Building Inspections says a new roof needs a city building permit first, whether the owner or the contractor applies.
  2. Ask about registration. General contractors register with the city, and the fee schedule says those registrations expire every year. A roofer that registered two seasons ago may not be current.
  3. Watch where it is filed. Permits and contractor registrations go through the city's CityWorks public portal, which also handles inspection scheduling and results.
  4. Look it up afterward. The city publishes permits-issued reports by month on its website. A roofing permit for your address should appear in one.
  5. Know what happens to shortcuts. Rockwall's permit application says contractors caught working without a permit can have their registration revoked and must reapply.

Questions about a particular job go to the permit technicians at 972-771-7709. Fees and procedures change, so confirm before you rely on any detail here.

Impact-resistant shingles and your HOA

If your Rockwall home is in a neighborhood with a homeowners association, its approval can be the slowest step in a re-roof. Texas law helps here. Section 202.011 of the Property Code says an association may not prohibit or restrict shingles that are designed mainly to resist wind and hail, to provide heating and cooling efficiency beyond customary composite shingles, or to produce solar energy.

There is a condition: once installed, the shingles have to resemble the shingles used or authorized in the subdivision. So the HOA can still weigh in on color and profile, and you should still submit the product information it asks for. What it cannot do is ban the category.

Whether a sturdier shingle is worth it for your house is a question for the roofer and your insurer. Ask the insurer whether it changes your premium or your coverage, and ask the roofer what the product's wind and hail ratings are.

Storms on the record in Rockwall County

The National Weather Service office in Fort Worth keeps a tornado history for Rockwall County going back to 1880. Two recent entries stand out.

April 3, 2012

During the North Texas outbreak that day, a tornado touched down in Rockwall County near Royse City and moved northeast into Hunt County. The NWS rated it EF-2, with a path about 3.1 miles long and up to about 400 yards wide.

October 20, 2019

The night an EF-3 tornado tore through north Dallas, NWS surveyors also found straight-line wind damage in Rockwall, and EF-1 tornado damage just across the lake in Rowlett and Sachse.

Most roof claims here come from hail and wind that never make a named list. After any hard storm, look from the yard for missing or creased shingles, dents on vents and gutters, and granules collecting at downspouts, then call your insurer before permanent repairs.

Questions for any roofer you are considering in Rockwall
