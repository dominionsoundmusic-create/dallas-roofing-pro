A young city with its first round of re-roofs

Mansfield began as a mill town on Walnut Creek and stayed small for most of its history. According to the Handbook of Texas, it lies mainly in southeastern Tarrant County, with sections reaching into northeastern Johnson County and northwestern Ellis County. The growth came late and fast: by the 2020 census the city had 72,602 residents.

That timing shapes roofing here. NeighborhoodScout's housing data puts roughly two of every three Mansfield homes in the "built since 2000" column. A shingle roof on a house finished in the mid 2000s has been through two decades of North Texas sun and spring storms, and a lot of those owners are now meeting a roofing salesperson for the first time.

The older part of town is different. NeighborhoodScout describes the Walnut Creek Valley area as mostly built between 1970 and 1999, with newer homes mixed in, so some roofs there are on their second or third covering.

When hail the size of baseballs hit Mansfield

On June 12 and 13, 2023, two days of storms dropped large hail across the southern half of the Metroplex. Fox 4 reported baseball-size hail in Mansfield and neighborhood streets covered in broken windshields and rear windows. WFAA described it as large hail falling on North Texas for a second straight day, and NBC DFW collected viewer photos of the damaging hail from both nights.

Hail that size does more than knock granules loose. It can crack shingles, split vents and skylights, and dent gutters and metal flashing. Damage on a roof is often hard to judge from the street, which is why a hailstorm is followed by a flood of offers to "take a look." A few Mansfield-specific points before you let anyone on your roof:

  • Call your insurer before signing a contract, and ask how your policy handles wind and hail, including any separate deductible.
  • Hold off on permanent repairs until the insurance adjuster has had a look.
  • Ask each roofing company how long it has worked in Mansfield, the same question the city itself suggests.
  • Keep photos you took from the ground and from inside, with dates.

Mansfield's permit line falls at the decking

Mansfield draws the line in a different place than many of its neighbors. Its Building Safety FAQ separates jobs by what the roofer touches.

The jobCity permit?What still applies
Shingles only, one or two-family homeNo permit requiredCode still applies: flashing and drip edge required, old shingles fully removed
Re-roof that involves decking or structural membersResidential permit requiredApplied for online; the city inspects
Any commercial re-roofBuilding permit requiredIf insulation is removed, an energy code calculation, which usually means more insulation

Because a shingle-only job can skip the permit, no city inspector may ever look at it. The written contract becomes your main protection, so make it say that the old roof comes off completely, that new drip edge and flashing go on, and how damaged decking will be handled and priced if the crew finds it. Building Safety is at 1200 E. Broad Street and answers at 817-276-4220.

Four reasons Mansfield residents pick up the phone

The original roof is wearing out

On a house built during the 2000s boom, the original shingles may be near the end of their life even without a storm. Owners want someone to tell them honestly whether to repair or replace.

Hail came through

Homes from the 1970s through 1990s can have tired flashing around chimneys and vents, and sometimes older layers that hide decking problems.

Questions to put to any Mansfield roofing company

  1. How long have you worked in Mansfield? The city suggests this question itself. Ask for addresses of nearby jobs.
  2. Will this job need a city permit? If decking will be replaced, the answer should be yes. Ask who applies for it.
  3. Does the price include a full tear-off, new drip edge and flashing? The city's FAQ calls for all three.
  4. What happens if you find bad decking? Get the price per sheet in writing before work starts.
  5. Can I see your liability insurance certificate? Have the insurance company send it to you, not the salesperson.
  6. Who registers the shingle warranty? Ask for proof it was registered in your name.
