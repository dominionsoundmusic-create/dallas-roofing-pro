When the roof cannot wait until Monday

Most roof problems give you time to collect quotes. A few do not. If rain is reaching the inside of the house, if something heavy came through the decking, or if wind peeled back enough roofing to expose the wood, every hour of weather adds to the damage. That is what emergency roof repair in Dallas is for: closing the roof quickly and safely so the real repair can be planned in daylight.

DFW Expert Roofers is a phone number, not a roofing company. Nobody from the line comes to your house. When you call, an automated assistant answers at any hour, asks what happened and where you are, and transfers you to an independent, locally owned roofing company that takes emergency work in your part of Dallas-Fort Worth. If that company cannot pick up, your name, number, town and how urgent it is are passed along so it can call you back.

How soon a crew can arrive is up to that company and the weather. Nobody can safely set a tarp during lightning or in a downpour, and after a large hailstorm every roofer in the metro is busy. Say clearly on the call that water is coming in.

Your part: safe steps from inside and from the yard

Everything useful you can do during a roof emergency happens indoors or from the ground. Trying to cover a hole yourself in wind and rain is how homeowners get hurt, and a tarp that is set wrong can catch the wind, tear the roof further or channel water somewhere new.

{{ m.safety_box() }}
  • Downed or sagging lines: Oncor's advice for a downed line is to leave the area and call 911, which contacts Oncor for you. Never touch the line or anything it is resting on, including a wet fence or a fallen limb. Outages can be reported to Oncor at 888-313-4747.
  • Water near electricity: if water is dripping into a light fixture or running down a wall with outlets, keep people out of that room and call 911 or an electrician. Do not reach into a breaker panel in standing water.
  • Protect what you can move: pull rugs, electronics, papers and furniture away from the leak, cover what cannot move with plastic, and set buckets or towels under the drip.
  • A bulging ceiling: a ceiling that is sagging with water can drop without warning. Clear the room and keep children and pets out until the roofer sees it.
  • Photos and video: take them from the yard and inside, with the date, before anything is covered or cleaned up. Wide shots of the house, then close shots of the damage.

An emergency roof timeline, from the first drip to the final repair

  1. First minutes. Get people away from wires, sagging ceilings and water near outlets. Call 911 if anyone is hurt, a line is down or you smell gas.
  2. First hour. Move belongings, catch the water, take photos, then call the line and say it is an emergency. Report the damage to your insurance company and write down the claim number.
  3. When conditions allow. The roofing company you were connected with sends someone to cover the opening, often with a heavy tarp fastened over the damaged area and past it, or boards over a hole first. Ask for a written price before they start.
  4. The next few days. The adjuster inspects. The roofer documents what it found and writes a repair or replacement estimate. Keep every receipt, including the tarp invoice and anything you bought to dry out the house.
  5. Within weeks. The permanent repair is scheduled. A tarp is a stopgap: HomeGuide reports that a temporary roof tarp lasts about 60 to 90 days on average, so do not let it become the roof.

What a roofer's emergency tarp job involves

{{ img('oak-limb-on-roof-after-storm.jpg', 'Large oak limb lying across the roof of a brick ranch home after a storm, with wet pavement in front', 1200, 800) }}

A proper emergency cover is more than a sheet of plastic. A careful roofer usually checks whether the decking around the opening can hold weight before walking near it, removes a limb or debris that is still on the roof (sometimes with a tree service if it is large), and covers holes with plywood when the decking is broken. The tarp should extend well past the damage and over the ridge where possible so water sheds off it rather than under it, and it is secured with wood strips or a method that does not add a row of new holes in good shingles.

Expect the roofer to photograph the damage before covering it. Those photos help both you and the adjuster, and you should ask for copies.

In Dallas, the city's storm damage guidance says no permit is needed for temporary measures such as tarps, while replacing large areas of the roof or the whole roof takes a re-roof permit and broken rafters or joists need a building permit. Other cities set their own rules; each town page covers its permit office.

Keeping the insurance side clean

Texas homeowner policies generally expect you to take reasonable steps to keep damage from getting worse. The Texas Department of Insurance tells storm victims to make temporary repairs, cover holes to keep rain out, keep a list of what was done and save the receipts. It also says to photograph damage before those repairs, not to throw out damaged items until the adjuster says so, and not to start permanent repairs before the adjuster has looked.

Records worth keeping

  • Date-stamped photos and video, outside and in
  • The claim number and the adjuster's name
  • The tarp invoice with what was covered and why
  • Receipts for buckets, fans, plastic and any drying service

Questions for your insurer

  • Does my policy cover temporary repairs, and up to what amount?
  • Do you need photos before or after the tarp goes on?
  • What is my deductible for wind and hail damage?
  • When can the adjuster come out?

What emergency roof repair costs, and why it varies

National cost sites give a rough sense of the tarp portion. Angi reports that tarping a roof averages about $450 nationwide, with jobs from $150 to $3,300 depending on how big the damaged area is and how hard the roof is to reach. HomeAdvisor says emergency tarping usually runs 50 to 100 percent above the price of scheduled work. Those numbers are not DFW quotes and not anyone's price list; a two-story roof with a steep pitch, a limb that has to come off first, or a call in the middle of a storm all push the price up, and real quotes vary.

The permanent repair is a separate job with its own estimate. Ask the roofer to write the tarp charge and the repair estimate as two items, so your insurer can see each one.

Help paying for roof repairs in Dallas-Fort Worth

People ask how they can get help to fix a roof they cannot afford. A few public programs exist. They take weeks or months and have income rules, so they are for the permanent repair, not tonight's tarp. Check each program's current terms before you count on it.

City of Dallas

The Home Improvement and Preservation Program moved to Volunteers of America Texas in February 2026. A city bulletin from October 2026 describes forgivable loans of up to $24,000 for necessary repairs, roofing included, for low- and moderate-income homeowners, with an application window that ran October 5 to 16, 2026. Watch the city's repair programs page for the next round.

City of Fort Worth

The Priority Repair Program, run by Neighborhood Services, covers leaking roofs for very low and low-income owners living in the home. Its June 2025 policy lists repairs at no charge up to $25,000, one whole roof replacement in the life of a home except after major weather events, and repairs no more than once every 12 months. Call 817-392-7548; the city warns waits can be long.

Rural areas: USDA Section 504

In USDA-eligible rural areas around the edges of the metro, Section 504 lends up to $40,000 at a fixed 1 percent over 20 years to low-income owners who cannot get affordable credit elsewhere. Owners 62 and older with very low income can get grants up to $10,000 to remove health and safety hazards, repayable if the home is sold within three years. Eligibility is checked by address.

Suburbs often have their own programs; ask your city's housing or neighborhood services office. The line does not process applications for any of these.

Questions to ask on an emergency call

  • When can someone get here, and what happens if the storm is still going?
  • What will the tarp or board-up cost, and will you put that in writing before you start?
  • Will you photograph the damage before covering it and send me the pictures?
  • How long should this cover hold, and when would the permanent repair happen?
  • Can you show me a certificate of liability insurance sent from your agent?
  • Is the repair estimate separate from the emergency charge?

Be wary of anyone who asks you to sign a full replacement contract to get a tarp, wants most of the money up front, or offers to deal with your insurer for you. Texas law reserves claim negotiation for you and licensed public adjusters.
