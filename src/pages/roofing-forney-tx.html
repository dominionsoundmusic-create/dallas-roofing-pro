Diamond Creek, April 3, 2012

Many Forney homeowners moved in after 2012 and never heard about the afternoon a tornado crossed the edge of town. Roofing in Forney, TX is worth looking at with that day in mind.

During the North Texas outbreak of April 3, 2012, a tornado touched down near Forney, southwest of US 80, and stayed inside Kaufman County for its whole life. The National Weather Service office in Fort Worth rated it EF-3, with winds estimated at 150 mph, a path about 8 miles long and up to about 150 yards wide.

The heaviest damage was in the Diamond Creek subdivision northeast of Forney, where two houses were destroyed and several others were heavily damaged. The tornado also damaged the roof of Crosby Elementary School, and a car from the school's parking lot was carried about 300 yards into a field. Seven people were hurt; no one died.

The fastest-growing city in the country, for a while

Since that storm, Forney has been transformed. Using census estimates, the Dallas Business Journal found that Forney led the nation in population growth from 2020 to 2023, at 51.2 percent, reaching about 35,470 residents by July 2023. The Census Bureau's own list put Forney tenth among fast-growing U.S. cities for the year ending July 2023, at 10.4 percent.

For roofs, that means a city full of first roofs on new houses. Young roofs still need attention. Hail does not care how old the shingles are, a builder's roof can have flashing or ventilation mistakes that show up only after a few seasons, and many new owners are unsure whether the builder's warranty or their homeowners policy is the first call. A roofer can tell you what it finds; your builder and insurer can tell you what they cover.

Forney's registration and permit rules, step by step

  1. The roofer registers with the city. Forney requires every contractor working inside city limits to register, and no permit is issued until it does. The fee is $30 per license.
  2. Registration expires on December 31. Forney registrations cover the current calendar year only. Early in a new year, ask whether the roofer has renewed.
  3. The permit comes before the work. The city says permits must be obtained before improvements or alterations, and its fee schedule lists re-roofing.
  4. Everything runs through MyGov. Registration and permit applications are filed online in the city's MyGov portal.
  5. Start within 180 days. The residential application says a permit becomes void if work has not begun within 180 days of issue.

Rules and fees change. Check the details for your job with Forney's building inspection office before work begins.

Why Forney homeowners call

Hail on a nearly new roof

Owners of houses only a few years old are often surprised by hail damage and unsure whether it is worth a claim. A roofer's written findings help with that conversation.

A leak in a new house

Stains around a vent or skylight in a young home can point to an installation detail. Ask the roofer to photograph it so you can raise it with the builder.

Homeowners association approval

Many newer subdivisions have an HOA. Texas Property Code 202.011 limits an association's power to ban wind and hail resistant shingles that look like the approved ones.

Paperwork for a fast-changing city

With registration renewing every January, homeowners want a roofer that keeps its Forney paperwork current and files permits in MyGov.

Questions worth asking a Forney roofer

  • Is your Forney registration renewed for this calendar year?
  • Will the re-roof permit be in MyGov before you start, and can I have the number?
  • On a newer house, will you photograph anything that looks like an installation problem so I can show the builder?
  • What wind and hail ratings does the shingle you recommend carry, and will it satisfy my HOA?
  • Will your liability carrier send the certificate to me directly?
  • What exactly does your workmanship warranty promise, and is it written into the contract?
