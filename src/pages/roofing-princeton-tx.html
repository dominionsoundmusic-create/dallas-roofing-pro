Princeton's population has more than doubled since 2020

Princeton, in eastern Collin County, counted 17,027 people in the 2020 census. The Census Bureau estimated about 28,000 by mid-2023, Wikipedia cites a 2025 estimate of 43,524, and the bureau's 2024 to 2025 estimates put Princeton among the five fastest-growing U.S. cities of 20,000 or more.

How a Princeton re-roof gets permitted

Registration

General contractors are among the trades that must register with the City of Princeton. Confirm the roofer's registration is current before you sign.

A roof permit

The city's FAQ says any roof repair or replacement needs a permit, priced at $90. Its 2025 monthly reports list many residential re-roof permits at that fee.

A clear scope

Permit entries describe the work, from shingle line to new felt, ice and water shield and vents. Expect your contract to be at least that specific.

Development Services is at 2000 E. Princeton Drive; the city lists 972-736-6169 for permitting questions. Fees and steps change, so let the roofer confirm them for your job.

April 3, 2014: the tornado near Princeton

{{ img('older-frame-home-shingle-roof-hero.jpg', 'Older one-story frame house with a gray shingle roof and a front porch beside a quiet road', 1920, 1080) }}

On the evening of April 3, 2014, a storm crossing Collin County produced a brief tornado near Princeton. The National Weather Service rated it EF-0, with estimated peak winds of 85 mph and a path of about 1.2 miles. Some structures in the path lost shingles and roof decking.

The same storm dropped hail 2 inches across in Allen and up to golf ball size around McKinney. An EF-0 is the lowest rating on the scale, yet it was still enough to tear the deck off roofs. Strong straight-line winds can do similar damage without any tornado at all.

If a storm like that passes over, assume the roof needs a look even if the house next door seems fine, and stay off it yourself.
