Two kinds of roofs in one county seat

McKinney, the Collin County seat, has roofs that span more than a century. Close to the square, the McKinney Residential Historic District holds houses from 1880 to 1937. Out past US 75 and along SH 121, whole villages went up in the last few decades with matching composition shingle roofs.

Those two halves of town hire roofers differently. An owner on North Benge may need a historic preservation review before switching roofing materials. An owner in Stonebridge Ranch needs a shingle from the association's approved list. Both need a city permit, because McKinney requires one for every roofing project.

DFW Expert Roofers is a free phone line, not one of the roofing companies in McKinney. You call, describe the roof, and the call goes to an independent, owner-run roofing company that takes McKinney work and handles the inspection, price, permit and job.

What McKinney's roof permit packet spells out

Building Inspections publishes a roof permit packet. These are the parts worth reading before you sign a contract.

Every roofing project

The packet says permits are required for all roofing projects, with a flat fee of $200. Ask before assuming a small job is exempt.

Drip edge is mandatory

Asphalt shingle roofs must have drip edge, the metal strip that carries water off the edge and into the gutter. Look for it as a line item in the estimate.

Old layers and worn metal

Damaged or deteriorated vents and flashing get replaced, and existing roof layers have to come off under conditions the packet lists, such as a soaked or failing base.

Accounts in the city portal

Permits and inspections run through Citizen Self Service. The applicant, the contractor and every subcontractor attached to a permit each need their own account.

A signature form

The city requires a Signature Verification Form for each project, so ask the roofer who signs it and when.

A building final

The final checks that shingles and flashing follow the manufacturer's instructions, that plumbing and gas vents are reattached and flashed, and that debris is gone.

Registration, and why the permit is your best check

{{ img('roof-05-card.jpg', 'Roofer sealing around a roof vent pipe with a caulk gun on an asphalt shingle roof at sunrise', 1200, 675) }}

McKinney's contractor registration is written for licensed trades, which must register with the city before a permit is issued. The roof packet itself points out that Texas does not license roofers, so there is no state license number for a roofing company to give you.

That makes the permit the most useful public record you have. Ask the roofer to apply in its own company name, to send you the permit number, and to tell you when the final is scheduled. If an electrician or plumber is needed for the job, that trade is attached to the same permit with its own account.

Building Inspections can answer questions about a specific address. Keep the permit and the final inspection result with your roof warranty papers, since a buyer or insurer may ask for both later.

Storms the Weather Service logged in McKinney

  • May 2006. During a tornado warning for north-central Collin County, trained spotters reported tornadoes north and northeast of McKinney, near Anna and Westminster.
  • April 2008. An EF-1 tornado touched down near Custer Road and Hedgcoxe Road in Allen and lifted near US 75 and SH 121 in McKinney. The Weather Service said damage at Collin County Regional Airport looked like downburst wind.
  • April 3, 2014. Hail 1.75 inches across fell at the sheriff's office near Bloomdale Road and US 75, and ping pong to golf ball size hail hit Craig Ranch. The same storm produced a brief tornado near Princeton.

Hail does not hit every street evenly. CBS News Texas once profiled a neighborhood near SH 121 and Stacy Road where late-March hail broke windows and damaged roofs, and patched roofs and open claims were still easy to find weeks later. If a storm passes over and you are unsure, photograph the roof from the yard and have a roofer look before the claim deadline on your policy.

Reasons McKinney homeowners pick up the phone

A storm crew at the door

The city requires a solicitor's permit for door-to-door sales. Callers want a second opinion from a roofer they reached on their own terms.

An old house with an old roof

Owners near the square want a roofer who understands the historic preservation review and can document materials for the city.

An HOA form due before work

Stonebridge Ranch owners need the exact shingle name and color for the Modification Application before a crew can start.

A small repair on a newer roof

Lifted shingles or a leak at a vent pipe after a windy night. The permit rule covers roofing projects, so ask how the roofer handles it.

Questions to put to any McKinney roofer

  • Whose name goes on the city permit, and when do I get the number?
  • Is drip edge in the price, and will worn vents and flashing be replaced rather than reused?
  • Can you give me the shingle line and color in writing for my HOA or the Historic Preservation Office?
  • How will you charge for decking that turns out to be rotten, and will I see photos first?
  • Can your insurance agent email me a current liability certificate?
