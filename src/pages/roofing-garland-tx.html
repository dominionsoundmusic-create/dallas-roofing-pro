A big postwar city with its own rules

Roofing in Garland, TX is shaped by three things that are particular to this city: a building department with a written re-roof rule, a city-owned electric utility, and housing that grew in a rush after World War II and again in the far north decades later.

The rush is easy to see in the numbers. Garland had roughly 38,500 residents by 1960 and about 81,500 by 1970, and the city limits pushed east toward Lake Ray Hubbard as farmland was annexed. Many streets in south and central Garland were built in that run. A house from 1962 has been through several roofs since, and some of those jobs stacked new shingles over old ones.

North Garland tells a different story. The Firewheel area takes its name from Firewheel Golf Park, the city course built in 1983, and most of its neighborhoods went up from the late 1990s on. Firewheel Town Center, the open-air shopping center at the President George Bush Turnpike and State Highway 78, opened in 2005. Those homes are on their first or second roof.

Garland re-roof permits, layer limits and roofer registration

The City of Garland puts its roofing rules in writing on a dedicated re-roofing page and in its contractor registration form. Here is what they say, in the order a job runs into them.

  1. The $2,000 line. Garland requires a permit for roof repairs valued at $2,000 or more. A full replacement is above that line, and so is a large storm repair. Small fixes below it may not need one; ask Building Inspections if you are unsure.
  2. The roofer registers first. Roofers register with the city as general contractors. The form asks for the owner's or principal's driver's license and a certificate of insurance with general liability of no less than $300,000, with the City of Garland listed as certificate holder.
  3. Paperwork for the permit. The application needs a copy of the signed contract for the roof work or the insurance adjuster's scope. That makes it worth reading your contract closely before you sign it, since the city will see it too.
  4. Two layers, then a tear-off. Citing the residential code, the city allows up to two layers of shingles. A roof that already has two must be stripped to the deck before new shingles go on.
  5. Material changes count. Switching from shingles to another material needs a permit as well, and the page sets metal roofing at 26 gauge or heavier, installed to the maker's specifications.

The city's re-roofing page listed the permit fee at $175 when this page was written. Fees and rules change, so confirm the current version with Garland Building Inspections, 800 Main Street, 972-205-2300.

December 26, 2015, and what Garland learned about roofs

On the evening after Christmas in 2015, a tornado formed in Sunnyvale and moved northeast through Garland and into Rowlett. The National Weather Service office in Fort Worth rated it EF-4 in Garland, estimated peak winds of 170 to 180 mph, and mapped a path about 13 miles long. The worst of it struck where Interstate 30 meets the President George Bush Turnpike. NWS Fort Worth calls it the highest rated and deadliest December tornado on record in Dallas County, and its records list ten deaths: nine in Garland and one in Rowlett.

No roof survives a direct hit from a storm like that, and no installer should claim one will. The lesson Garland owners took from it is smaller and more useful. Houses at the edge of a tornado's path, and houses hit by ordinary spring thunderstorms, lose shingles along the eaves, rakes and ridge, where wind gets underneath first. How the edges are fastened and sealed matters more than most people realize until a storm tests it.

After a strong storm, look from the ground first. Shingle pieces on the lawn, bare strips at the roof edge, a bent vent cap or a fresh stain on a ceiling are all reasons to call. Take dated photos, then call your insurer before any permanent repair, so an adjuster can see the damage as it was.

Four Garland situations that lead to a call

A third layer is not an option

On a 1950s or 1960s house, the roof may already carry two layers. Under Garland's two-layer limit, the next job is a full tear-off, and that is when soft or rotted decking finally shows up.

The adjuster's scope goes to the city

Garland accepts the insurance adjuster's scope with the permit application. Homeowners with an open claim often want a roofer who will work from that scope and explain any difference between it and the estimate.

A newer house, a first replacement

Homes built in the Firewheel area from the late 1990s on are reaching the age when a first roof wears out. If your street has a homeowners association that reviews shingle color, a roofer can tell you what to submit before the job is booked.

Wind from the east

Garland reaches east to Lake Ray Hubbard, and open water gives a storm nothing to slow it down. After a hard blow, lifted tabs and loose ridge caps are worth a look from the yard, wherever in the city you live.

What to ask a roofer working in Garland

  • Are you registered with the City of Garland as a general contractor, and is the city listed on your insurance certificate?
  • Counting from the deck up, how many shingle layers does this house carry, and is removing every one of them in the price?
  • Whose name goes on the Garland permit, and can I have the permit number before tear-off day?
  • If this is an insurance job, are you working from the adjuster's scope, and where does your estimate differ from it?
  • If rotten decking turns up under the old roof, what does each replaced sheet cost, and do I see photos before you swap it?
  • How will you fasten the shingles at the eaves, rakes and ridge, and what wind rating does the product carry?
  • Put your labor warranty on paper: which defects, how many years? And who files the manufacturer's warranty registration?
