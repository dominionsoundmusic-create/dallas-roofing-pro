A lake city with a contractor register

Benbrook sits southwest of Fort Worth in Tarrant County, a city of 24,520 people at the 2020 census, wrapped around the north end of Benbrook Lake. The lake is a flood control reservoir on the Clear Fork of the Trinity River. The Army Corps of Engineers began the dam in May 1947, finished it in December 1950, and started holding water in September 1952.

For roofing in Benbrook, the city's contractor rules are the most useful fact. Benbrook's FAQ says work in the city must be performed by a contractor registered with Benbrook (it lists one exception, for owners with a homestead exemption on file). Registration asks for the owner's driver's license, any state trade license, and proof of at least $300,000 in liability insurance, and it lapses one year after the application.

Checking a roofer against Benbrook's records

Based on the City of Benbrook's permit FAQ, its registration requirements sheet and its building code amendments.

  1. Ask for the registered business name. Use it, not a sales rep's name, when you check with the city.
  2. Confirm it is current. Registration runs one year from the application date, so a company registered two years ago may have lapsed.
  3. Ask for the insurance certificate directly. The city wants proof of at least $300,000 in liability coverage; have the insurer send you a copy too.
  4. Ask whether your job needs a permit. Re-roofs are not named in the permit FAQ, but the city logs roof repair and replacement permits. Find out before work starts.
  5. Ask how the roof will meet code. Benbrook's code amendments say materials and methods for recovering or replacing a roof covering must comply with the building code's roofing chapter.

Permits and Inspections is in City Hall at 911 Winscott Road, phone 817-249-6064. Inspections are requested at 817-249-6000 a day ahead.

March 2016: a tornado on the water

In March 2016 the National Weather Service confirmed a brief EF-0 tornado near the Benbrook Lake marina, with winds estimated around 85 mph. KERA reported that it tore apart the dry dock area and shoved boats on top of one another; it was one of five tornadoes the weather service confirmed across North Texas over a single day. NBC DFW noted the damage stayed light.

An EF-0 is the weakest rating, but 85 mph is more than enough to lift shingle tabs, peel ridge caps and drop limbs onto a roof. Houses near open water or on higher ground catch gusts that sheltered streets do not, which is worth remembering when you check the roof after a spring storm.
