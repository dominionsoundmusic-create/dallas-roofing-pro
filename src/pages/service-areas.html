How coverage works across the metroplex

Roofing in DFW means very different houses from one town to the next: 1950s ranch homes under old pecans in parts of Dallas and Fort Worth, brick two-stories from the 1980s and 1990s in Plano and Arlington, brand-new subdivisions on former farmland around Prosper and Celina, and stone ranch houses on acreage west of Weatherford.

The free line takes calls from all of it. Each town page below was researched for that town: which office handles roof permits, whether the city registers roofers, what storms have hit it, and what is particular about its neighborhoods. The roofing company you reach works in your area, though DFW Expert Roofers itself has no local offices.

Where to start in your town

Before you hire anyone, check three things on your town's page: whether a permit is required for the work, whether the city asks roofers to register, and which office to call with questions. Then confirm with the city, because fees and forms change.

If your address is outside city limits, the city's permit rules may not apply, but your HOA's may. Your county appraisal district record shows which city, if any, your property is in.
