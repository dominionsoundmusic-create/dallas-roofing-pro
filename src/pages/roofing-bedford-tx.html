How a Bedford roof job moves through City Hall

Roofing in Bedford, TX comes with more city oversight than in some neighboring towns. The City of Bedford requires a permit for all roofing replacement or repairs, not just full tear-offs, and it sends an inspector out when the job is done.

  1. The roofer registers. Contractors register with the city through its online permit portal, and the registration is renewed each year. A homeowner working on their own homestead is the one exemption.
  2. The permit is issued first. The city says the permit must be issued before work starts. A remodel permit is added if the framing or roof pitch changes.
  3. The permit stays on site. It is kept at the job until the final inspection, so you should be able to see it.
  4. The city inspects the finished roof. Bedford performs a final roof inspection. Ask the roofer to tell you the result, and keep a copy for your records and any future sale.

Building Permits and Inspections is at 1805 L. Don Dodson Drive, Building C, and answers at 817-952-2140. The city's roofing page also mentions state licensing, yet no such state license for roofers exists in Texas, so ask the office what it actually checks when a roofer registers.

From a ranch town of 475 to a busy Mid-Cities suburb

The city's own budget history says settlers reached the Bedford area around 1860, the Bedford Boys Ranch was set up on 253 acres in 1949, and the town incorporated on January 22, 1953, with about 475 people on roughly two square miles.

Then it filled in. By 1970 Bedford had passed 10,000 residents, and the decade that followed brought an industrial park, shopping centers and the opening of DFW Airport next door. The population reached about 20,800 by 1980 and about 44,000 by 1990. The Boys Ranch, meanwhile, became the city's largest park.

A look back by the Bedford Citizen notes that ranch and Cape styles were the favorites in the 1950s. Much of the town's housing followed in the 1970s and 1980s, so many Bedford roofs have been replaced at least once, and a roofer should be ready to talk about layers, decking and attic airflow on an older house.

Hail reports logged in and near Bedford

The Storm Prediction Center keeps a daily log of spotter and public reports. These are preliminary, but they show what Bedford roofs have taken.

August 1, 2021

Golf-ball hail reported near Forest Ridge Drive and Highway 183 on the southwest side of Bedford, with quarter-size hail near Bedford Road and Highway 121.

May 27, 2024

Late that night, baseball-size hail was reported a mile north of Bedford and hen-egg hail, two inches across, a mile southwest, along with golf-ball reports in town.

After a storm like either one, walk the yard rather than the roof. Note dents on gutters, vents and the air conditioning unit, look for shingle bits on the lawn, and photograph everything with the date before you call your insurer.

Roof work Bedford homeowners most often need

On a town of 1950s through 1980s houses, the calls tend to be repairs on aging roofs, full replacements once a roof wears out, and storm damage after a spring squall line.

Six things to ask a roofer about a Bedford job

  • Is your City of Bedford registration current for this year?
  • Will you pull the permit before tear-off and leave the card on site?
  • Who meets the city inspector for the final, and will I get the result?
  • Counting the old layers, how many are up there, and what does a replaced sheet of decking add to the bill?
  • Will your insurance agent send me your liability certificate directly?
  • If this is a claim, where is the Texas deductible notice in the contract?
