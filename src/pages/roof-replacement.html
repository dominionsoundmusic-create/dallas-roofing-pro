What a roof replacement in Dallas really involves

Replacing a roof is not just new shingles. Done right, it means stripping the old roof to the wood, fixing any rotten decking, laying new underlayment and metal, setting new shingles to the maker's instructions, getting the attic to breathe, and passing a city inspection where one is required.

Two bids that look close in price can describe very different jobs. One might include drip edge, new pipe boots and a ridge vent; the other might reuse old flashing and say nothing about decking. This page lays out each part so you can read a bid the way a building inspector would.

Tear-off or overlay?

An overlay (a "recover") lays new shingles over the old ones. A tear-off strips everything to the deck first. The International Residential Code, the model code cities build their house rules from, treats a roof replacement as removal down to the deck and allows a recover only in limited cases.

If a bid proposes an overlay, ask how many layers are on the house now and whether the shingle maker's warranty allows installation over an existing roof. Then confirm with your city that the permit covers it.

Layer by layer: what goes on a new roof

Read your bid against this list from the wood up. Anything missing should be explained in writing.

  1. Decking. Plywood or OSB sheets nailed to the rafters. Since no one sees the deck until the old roof is off, a fair bid sets a price per sheet for any that must be replaced and says how the count will be shown to you, for example in photos.
  2. Drip edge. L-shaped metal along the eaves and rakes. Code section R905.2.8.5 makes it mandatory for shingles: its lower lip hangs a minimum of 1/4 inch past the sheathing, nails go in at 12-inch maximum spacing, and the underlayment runs on top of it along eaves but beneath it along rakes.
  3. Underlayment. Felt or synthetic sheet over the whole deck, often with a self-adhering membrane in valleys and around penetrations. The bid should name the product.
  4. Flashing. New step flashing at walls, new valley metal if valleys are open, new chimney and skylight flashing, and new pipe boots. Reusing old metal to save a few dollars is a common shortcut. See where flashing goes on a roof.
  5. Shingles. Named by brand, product line and color, installed with the nail count and pattern the maker requires for its warranty.
  6. Ventilation. Intake at the eaves and exhaust near the ridge. The IRC (R806.2) sets net free vent area at 1/150 of the attic floor area, with a reduced 1/300 ratio only under conditions the code lists. A hot, stuffy attic ages shingles from below, so ask how the roofer calculated yours.
  7. Ridge cap, cleanup and final inspection. Cap shingles over the ridge, a magnet sweep for nails, and the city inspection where your city requires one.

The Dallas re-roof permit, step by step

Inside Dallas city limits, the city's Development Services department draws a clear line. Minor repairs to roofing or roof vents need no permit, and temporary tarps are exempt. Replacing large areas of a roof, or the entire roof, requires a re-roof permit.

  1. A registered contractor applies. An owner or agent may submit, but the city will not issue the permit unless a registered contractor is on the application. Registered contractors can apply online through the city's permit portal.
  2. Structural work is separate. If rafters, roof joists or wall sections need replacing, that calls for a building permit, not just a re-roof permit.
  3. Doing it yourself has its own form. Owners who do their own work file a Homeowner's Exemption from Contractor Registration rather than registering.
  4. Ask to see the permit. It should be in the roofer's name, posted at the job, and closed out after the final inspection.

The city takes questions at 214-948-4480. Suburbs differ: Plano, for example, requires a permit for every re-roof and does a final inspection. Your town page has the local rule.

Class 4 shingles, insurance credits and HOA rules

Impact-resistant shingles

Hail resistance is graded by the UL 2218 impact test on a four-step scale, and the top grade, Class 4, survives the largest steel balls. The Texas Department of Insurance says insurers that offer roofing credits give the largest one to Class 4 coverings, but each insurer sets its own amount and paperwork. To qualify, the packaging or label has to show the class, the maker, the year made and the brand. Once the roof is on, your roofer completes the state form PC068, which you pass along to the insurance company.

Before choosing, call your insurance agent and ask what credit applies, what proof it wants, and whether the credit is worth the higher material price over the years you plan to stay.

What an HOA can and cannot block

A 2011 state law, now section 202.011 of the Property Code, limits HOA covenants here. If your deed restrictions already permit a shingle roof, the association cannot enforce a ban on shingles built for wind and hail protection, energy savings or solar generation. The shingles must resemble the ones used or allowed in the neighborhood, be more durable than and of equal or better quality than those, and match the look of the surrounding homes.

Most HOAs still want a color approval before work starts. Submit the shingle sample and the roofer's product sheet, and wait for the written approval.

How to compare roof replacement bids

Lay two or three bids side by side and check each line. A bid that is missing a line is not cheaper, it is vaguer. Here is what a complete estimate should spell out.

Scope

  • Full tear-off, with the number of layers removed
  • Decking price per sheet, and how replacements are documented
  • Drip edge, underlayment and valley treatment named

Materials

  • Shingle brand, line, color and impact class
  • New flashing, pipe boots and ridge vent listed
  • Ventilation plan, intake and exhaust

Paperwork

  • Permit pulled in the roofer's name
  • Workmanship warranty length, in writing
  • Maker's warranty registration after the job

Money

  • Deposit amount and when the balance is due
  • The deductible notice Texas requires on insurance jobs of $1,000 or more
  • Change orders priced only in writing

Site

  • Where the dumpster and materials will sit
  • How landscaping, the pool and the AC unit are protected
  • Magnet sweep for nails at the end of each day

The company

  • Liability certificate sent by its insurer
  • City contractor registration where required
  • Addresses of nearby roofs it finished

Prices for whole roofs vary widely with size, pitch and material. The roof replacement cost guide for DFW explains the drivers and cites published ranges; the only real number is the written bid for your roof.

When a storm claim is paying for the roof

After hail or wind, your insurer may cover some or all of a replacement, depending on the policy, the damage the adjuster documents and your deductible. The line does not file claims, and a roofer may not negotiate your claim for you; only a licensed public adjuster may do that in Texas. Ask your insurer whether the policy pays replacement cost or actual cash value, what your wind and hail deductible is, and whether part of the payment is held back until the work is finished. The Texas roof insurance claim guide explains each term.
