A railroad town turned fast-growing suburb

Anna began beside a north-south rail line that came through in 1873. The town was platted in 1883 with about twenty residents and incorporated in 1913. By the 2020 census it had 16,896 people, and Census Bureau estimates for 2024 to 2025 placed it among the five fastest-growing U.S. cities of 20,000 or more.

Much of that growth is new subdivisions in northern Collin County, which means a lot of roofs of similar age and, when hail comes through, a lot of roof claims at once. Anna's city rules for roofing are clear and fairly strict, and knowing them helps you sort careful roofers from fast ones.

DFW Expert Roofers is a free phone line, not a contractor. It connects your call with an independent, locally owned roofing company that serves Anna.

The City of Anna's roofing requirements

The 3-square line

New roofs and re-roofing need a permit unless the repair is 3 squares or less, which is about 300 square feet of roof.

Who can pull it

Only a registered general contractor, or a homeowner working on a homestead that shows a homestead exemption with the appraisal district.

Tear-off and new underlayment

The old roof comes off and new underlayment goes down. The application has to describe all work, including decking or rafter replacement.

Surprises after tear-off

If damaged decking appears once the shingles are off, the city asks to be contacted so the permit can be modified. Gas and plumbing vents must be reconnected by the contractor.

Contractor registration costs $100, lasts one year and requires liability insurance of $500,000 with the City of Anna as certificate holder. The fee schedule says no construction permits are issued without it. The Building Department is at 120 W. 7th Street, Suite 142.

When the doorbell will not stop

{{ img('roofer-ladder-at-gutter.jpg', 'Roofer standing at the foot of an aluminum ladder set against the gutter of a brick home', 1200, 800) }}

After one hailstorm, NBC DFW talked with an Anna homeowner who had been approached by roofers at least 30 times. Anna police told the station they usually see about one door-to-door solicitor application a month, and they had received 70 in two days, every one from a roof or hail repair company. Police said they were checking permits and shutting down sellers who did not comply.

Anna requires a permit for door-to-door sales, so asking for it is fair. Even with a permit, a solicitor is just one option. Take a card, close the door, and decide after you have a second estimate from a company you contacted yourself.

Northern Collin County has a tornado record too. During a May 2006 warning, the National Weather Service received spotter reports of tornadoes near Anna and Westminster.

Ask an Anna roofer

  • Are you registered with the City of Anna this year, and is your insurance certificate on file with the city?
  • Does the job go over 3 squares, and if so, who files the permit?
  • Does your price include tear-off and new underlayment?
  • How will you handle and price bad decking, and will you update the permit if you find it?
  • Who reconnects the gas and plumbing vents, and when is the final inspection?
