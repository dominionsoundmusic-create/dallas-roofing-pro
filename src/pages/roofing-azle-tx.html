A dairy town on the edge of Eagle Mountain Lake

Azle straddles the Parker and Tarrant county line northwest of Fort Worth. It traces its start to 1846, when Dr. James Azle Steward settled here, and the town later took his middle name. Farms and dairies sold milk to Fort Worth creameries in the early 1900s, and the 2020 census counted 13,369 residents.

Just east of town, a dam on the West Fork of the Trinity River holds back Eagle Mountain Lake. The Tarrant Regional Water District owns and runs it. Construction ran from 1930 to 1932, and a 2018 state survey measured the lake at roughly 9,246 acres. Homes along its shore and on open acreage around Azle face storms with fewer trees and buildings to break the wind.

Azle leaves most roof checking to you

From the City of Azle's roofing page and its contractors and registration page.

Shingles on a house: no permit

The city says replacing residential shingles does not require a permit. That also means no city inspector looks at the finished roof.

New decking: Miscellaneous Permit

If the whole roof deck is replaced, the roofer files a Miscellaneous Permit application with the city.

No roofer registry

Azle has no general contractor registration. Only trades such as mechanical, electrical, plumbing and backflow register.

Commercial roofing projects begin with an email to permits@cityofazle.org giving the address and a short description. The permit desk answers at 817-444-2541, extension 102.

Making the contract do the inspector's job

With no permit and no registry on a typical shingle job, the written agreement is your main protection in Azle. Before you sign, make sure it says:

  • The old shingles come off completely, down to the deck.
  • What underlayment goes on, and where ice and water membrane is used, if any.
  • That drip edge, valley and wall flashing, pipe boots and vents are replaced or named as reused.
  • The price per sheet for bad decking, and that you get photos of any replaced.
  • Who files the Miscellaneous Permit if the whole deck has to be redone.
  • The shingle's wind rating, and who registers the manufacturer warranty in your name.
