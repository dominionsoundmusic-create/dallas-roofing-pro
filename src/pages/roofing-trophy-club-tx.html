A planned town built around golf

Trophy Club roofing questions start in a town with an unusual origin. It was laid out in the 1970s as one of the first planned communities in Texas, centered on the only golf course Ben Hogan ever designed, and it incorporated as a town on January 19, 1985.

Today Trophy Club is home to 13,688 people (2020 census). It sits along State Highway 114 west of DFW Airport and south of the western reach of Grapevine Lake. Nearly all of the town is in Denton County, with a very small part in Tarrant County. Its oldest streets date from those early golf-course years, and later neighborhoods filled in around them, so roof ages vary widely from one block to the next.

DFW Expert Roofers is a free phone line, not a contractor. It connects you with an independent, locally owned roofing company that takes Trophy Club calls. That company sees the roof, sets the price and pulls the town permit.

Trophy Club's re-roof permit, step by step

  1. The roofer registers with the town. Free, good for one year, with a driver's license copy and proof of insurance naming the Town of Trophy Club. The town's pages describe both an email form and the online portal, so the roofer should confirm the current method.
  2. The re-roof goes on the trade permit form. Trophy Club uses one form for re-roofs and several other trades, submitted to permitting@trophyclub.org.
  3. The fee is paid. The town's fee schedule lists a re-roofing permit at $85.
  4. Work starts once the permit is issued. Ask for the permit number before tear-off.
  5. Questions go to Community Development. Call 682-237-2917. Town Hall is at 1 Trophy Wood Drive.

Storms that hit Trophy Club roofs

March 14, 2024: hail up to about 3 inches

The Texas State Climatologist's summary for the month records the largest hail of that day, 3 inches, in Denton County. A USA Today Network storm report citing the National Weather Service in Fort Worth put stones of roughly 1.5 to 3 inches in Trophy Club, Roanoke, Argyle, Corinth and Highland Village, and NBC DFW ran reader photos of hail in Trophy Club. Stones that size can crack shingles, split ridge caps and dent metal vents.

Lightning and roof fires

CBS News Texas reported a Sunday storm in which lightning set five Trophy Club homes on fire. No one was hurt, but two of the houses lost their roofs and second floors. A lightning strike is a fire and insurance matter first: call 911, then your insurer, before anyone talks about the roof.
