A city that answers the roofing questions in writing

Roofing in Fort Worth, TX starts with a document most homeowners never open. The city's Development Services department publishes a residential roofing FAQ, and it settles several arguments that come up between homeowners, roofers and adjusters: when a permit is needed, whether old decking must go, and which edge details every new roof gets.

Fort Worth also has the storm record to explain why those questions come up so often. The May 1995 Mayfest supercell and the March 2000 downtown tornado both hit this city, and spring hail still rolls in from the west most years. Two storms Fort Worth still measures others against

Ask anyone who has lived in Tarrant County for a while about hail, and the Mayfest storm usually comes up. Here is what the National Weather Service records about the two big ones.

  • May 5, 1995, the Mayfest storm. A high-precipitation supercell moved in from west of the city and struck the Mayfest festival in Fort Worth around 7:10 p.m. The Weather Service lists hail to softball size, gusts near 70 mph and rain falling at up to 2.25 inches in 15 minutes. Its case study puts damage at about two billion dollars across Tarrant and Dallas counties, which still ranks it among the costliest hailstorms on record.
  • March 28, 2000, the downtown tornado. A tornado touched down about four miles west of downtown, followed West 7th Street, badly damaged the Cash America building and broke thousands of windows in downtown towers. Two people died. The same storms dropped hail that severely damaged roofs and cars in Lake Worth and Saginaw, according to the Weather Service review.

Neither storm is likely to repeat exactly, but they explain the pattern. Fort Worth sits on the western side of the metroplex, so spring storms building over the plains often reach it before they reach Dallas. A roof here can take hail that bruises shingles without leaving a mark you can see from the yard, which is why a careful roofer looks at vents, gutters and soft metal as well as the shingles themselves.

After a Fort Worth hailstorm

  1. Stay on the ground. Look from the yard and the street, and note any shingles, ridge caps or vent covers that came down.
  2. Photograph everything. Dented gutters, hail on the lawn, ceiling stains inside. Date the photos.
  3. Call your insurer. Ask how wind and hail claims work on your policy, how large the deductible is, and whether an older roof is paid out at full replacement value or depreciated value.
  4. Have one roofer look. Plenty of roofers look at storm damage without charging; ask about that when you are connected. Ask for photos of what it finds.

Historic districts add a step

Some Fort Worth homes sit in locally designated historic districts, and there the roof is part of the look the city protects. The design standards for the Fairmount/Southside Historic District, for example, say original tile and slate roofs are to be maintained, that a metal roof needs a Certificate of Appropriateness, and that standing seam metal may go on secondary slopes while R-panel and rib-panel profiles are not allowed. The city's historic roofing guidance ties a Certificate of Appropriateness to exterior work that requires a building permit. Carver Heights has its own published design guidelines.

If your house is in a historic district, call the city's historic preservation staff before you pick a material, and ask the roofer whether it has worked under these guidelines before. A shingle that matches what is there now is usually the simplest path; anything different should be cleared first.
