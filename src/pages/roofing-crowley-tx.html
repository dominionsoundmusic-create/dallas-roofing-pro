A railroad town that filled in fast

Crowley lies in southern Tarrant County, with a sliver of about 5.8 acres reaching into Johnson County. Farmers worked the land along Deer Creek from around 1848. When the Gulf, Colorado and Santa Fe Railway came through, the settlement shifted about a mile west and took the name of S. H. Crowley, the railroad's master of transportation.

For most of the 20th century it stayed a small town. Then the suburbs arrived. The 2020 census counted 18,070 residents, up 40.8 percent from 12,838 in 2010, and the Census Bureau estimated 20,483 by mid 2024. Much of the housing is newer, which means many owners are now dealing with the first hail claim or the first full replacement on their house.

After a hailstorm, look before anyone climbs. Dented gutters, downspouts and window screens, granules collecting at the bottom of a downspout, and hail marks on a mailbox or fence can all be seen from the ground. Photograph them with the date, then let a roofer judge the shingles in person.

Call your insurer early to ask how wind and hail are covered on your policy and what the deductible is. A roofer can document damage for you, but only a licensed public adjuster may negotiate a claim on your behalf.
