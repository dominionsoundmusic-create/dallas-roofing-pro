From Possum Trot to a commuter city

Red Oak was called Possum Trot when settlers arrived in the 1840s. It took the name Red Oak in 1849, and the Missouri, Kansas and Texas Railroad reached it in 1884. For a long time after that it stayed small, a town in northern Ellis County about twenty miles south of Dallas.

Then it grew fast. The census counted 4,301 people in Red Oak in 2000, 10,769 in 2010 and 14,222 in 2020, and the Census Bureau's estimate for mid-2023 was 18,624.

That growth is the main thing to understand about roofing in Red Oak, TX. When thousands of new residents arrive in two decades, many homes go up within a short span, and their roofs age on roughly the same schedule. Houses built in the early 2000s are now past twenty years old, and many may still carry the shingles they were built with.

Signs a boom-era Red Oak roof needs a look

You can spot most of these from the yard or from inside. None of them requires a ladder.

Granules in the gutters

Older asphalt shingles shed their surface grit. Piles of it at the bottom of downspouts point to wear.

Curling or cracked edges

Shingle edges that curl, cup or crack in the summer sun show up from the street, especially in low morning or evening light.

Hail history

A roof that has been through several spring hail seasons may have bruises you cannot see from the ground. A roofer's inspection, with photos, tells you.

Stains on the ceiling

A brown ring near a vent pipe, chimney or wall is often the first sign that flashing or shingles are failing.

A hot attic

Builder-grade vents may not move enough air. Ask the roofer to check ventilation when it looks at the roof.

Neighbors re-roofing

When several houses on your street built the same year are getting new roofs, it is a fair moment to have yours checked.

What the City of Red Oak asks for

Red Oak's Permits and Inspections Division takes applications and issues permits for work that requires one, and the city inspects all permitted work using its own and contracted inspectors. Its adopted codes include the 2024 International Residential Code.

Registration before any permit

Any firm, business, individual or organization obtaining a permit in Red Oak must have a valid contractor registration on file with Development Services, signed by an official of the business. There is no fee, and it is done through the city's online portal.

Ask about the re-roof itself

The city's posted pages do not state a rule for a re-roof alone. Before work starts, the roofer should call the permit technician at (469) 218-7725 and tell you, in writing, whether your job needs a permit.

180 days to start

Red Oak's residential permit application says a permit becomes void if work does not begin within 180 days, and that all permits need a final inspection.

Where to go

The building department is at 101 S. Live Oak St., (972) 576-3414. Rules change, so confirm before you sign.

Storms in this corner of Ellis County

Red Oak shares the spring hail and wind season of the whole Dallas-Fort Worth area. Research for this page did not find a National Weather Service damage survey of a tornado inside Red Oak, so none is claimed. Next door, though, the weather service surveyed an EF-1 tornado that began on the north side of Midlothian on October 20, 2019, with peak winds near 100 mph. Storms of that size are well within the range that strips shingles.

After any storm with hail or strong wind, work from the ground and from inside:

  • Look for shingles, ridge caps or vent covers in the yard.
  • Check gutters, downspouts, mailboxes and car hoods for fresh dents.
  • Check ceilings and the tops of exterior walls for new water marks.
  • Take dated photos, then call your insurer and ask how your policy treats hail and wind.

Questions to ask a Red Oak roofer

  1. Registration. Do you have a contractor registration on file with Red Oak Development Services?
  2. Permit. Did you ask the city whether my re-roof needs a permit, and what did it say?
  3. Age and condition. Is this the original roof, and what in your inspection photos tells you it needs repair rather than replacement, or the reverse?
  4. Ventilation. Does the attic move enough air, and does your plan change it?
  5. Coverage. Can I get proof of your liability coverage straight from the carrier that writes it?
