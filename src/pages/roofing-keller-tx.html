A railroad stop that became a commuter city

Roofing in Keller follows the arc of a town that stayed small for a century and then grew all at once. Settlers came in the early 1850s, and the stop on the Texas and Pacific Railway, first called Athol, took the name of John C. Keller, a railroad foreman.

The city incorporated on November 16, 1955, and marked its 70th anniversary in 2025. U.S. Highway 377 tied it to Fort Worth and Denton, but the real change came with DFW Airport. When the airport opened in 1974, Keller had fewer than 4,000 residents. By 1990 it had 13,683, and the 2020 census counted 45,776.

Growth that fast means many Keller houses were built within a few decades of each other, and their roofs age on similar clocks. Streets of homes from the 1980s and 1990s have often been re-roofed at least once, while newer ones are reaching their first replacement. Wooded lots and 26-plus miles of city trails add another factor: trees.

What Keller asks of contractors

Building Services at Keller Town Hall, 1100 Bear Creek Parkway, registers contractors and issues permits through the city's online portal. Its forms set a few useful bars for a homeowner to check.

Yearly registration

A contractor's Keller registration expires one year from the date it was issued, and the city charges a yearly fee per trade. Ask whether it is current.

$300,000 in coverage

The registration form asks for proof of liability insurance with a minimum of $300,000. That gives you a number to look for on the certificate.

Permits for repairs and changes

The city's FAQ says a permit is needed to alter, repair or replace a building, and doing it yourself does not exempt you. Ask how that applies to your roof.

Keller's construction tips say contractors should be the ones to obtain permits. For a firm answer on your job, call Building Services at 817-743-4110 or email buildingservices@cityofkeller.com.

Roof work Keller homeowners call about most

With so many houses of similar age, the calls cluster around three jobs: repairs on roofs that still have life, leaks that show up in ceilings, and full replacements when a roof is done.

Hail, wind and branches in Keller

March 30, 2009: baseball hail

The Storm Prediction Center's log for that day lists 2.75-inch hail at Keller, relayed by an NWS meteorologist who saw baseball hail at home and noted damage to a skylight. Stones that size crack shingles and break skylights and vents.

March 8, 2016: wind

The SPC reports for that day include wind damage in Keller, among them fence damage on Bradley Court. Straight-line wind can lift and crease shingles without tearing them off.

Trees over the lines

In 2026, Tri-County Electric Cooperative officials blamed tree branches touching overhead lines for repeated outages in its Southlake and Keller service areas. The same limbs often hang over roofs.

Tree damage to roofs

Branches that scrape a roof wear off granules, and a limb that drops in a storm can puncture decking. Have a tree service trim limbs near power lines, and a roofer check the shingles under them.

Questions for a Keller roofer before you sign

  • Is your City of Keller registration current, and when does it expire?
  • Does your liability certificate meet the city's $300,000 minimum, and will your agent send it to me?
  • Does my job need a city permit, and who will pull it?
  • Are there branches over my roof I should have trimmed first?
  • If my HOA reviews roofs, will you supply the shingle sample and product sheet?
  • For a claim, where is the deductible notice in the contract?

On HOAs: Texas Property Code Section 202.011 keeps an association from banning impact-resistant, energy-efficient or solar shingles outright, provided they look like shingles already used or approved in the neighborhood.
