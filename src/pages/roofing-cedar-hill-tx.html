The high ground of Dallas County

Most of North Texas is flat. Cedar Hill is not. The city sits on the escarpment that rises along the southwest side of Dallas County; the city's own nature page gives its elevation as 880 feet, the highest point in Dallas County, and city planning documents place Cedar Hill at the northern end of the Balcones Escarpment.

Below the ridge lies Joe Pool Lake, with Cedar Hill State Park on its shore. Texas Parks and Wildlife says the state acquired the park in 1982 and opened it in 1991. Next to it, Dallas County's Cedar Mountain Preserve holds some of the highest points in the county, and Dogwood Canyon Audubon Center sits alongside. Most of Cedar Hill is in Dallas County, with a small southern piece in Ellis County. The 2020 census counted 49,148 residents.

Roofing in Cedar Hill, TX is shaped by that setting: wooded slopes, homes tucked among trees, and a town that has been in the path of North Texas storms since before the Civil War.

Four reasons Cedar Hill homeowners call about roofs

Limbs over the roof

The hills and preserves that make Cedar Hill green also put branches over a lot of roofs. Rubbing limbs wear shingles, and a heavy one can come down in a storm. A roofer can point out where limbs touch; a tree service cuts them.

Leaves and valleys

Leaves and needles settle in roof valleys and gutters, hold water against the roof and slow drainage. Ask a roofer to check valleys and gutters on any inspection, not just the open field of shingles.

Spring hail

Cedar Hill shares the spring storm season of the rest of Dallas County, when hail comes through most years. Once the ice melts, dents on the gutters, the vent caps or the car hood, seen from the driveway, hint at what the shingles took.

Wind on exposed slopes

Homes on a ridge or an open slope do not have neighbors and trees blocking every gust. Ask the roofer how the shingles on your elevation will be fastened and what wind rating the product carries.

Permits and registration in Cedar Hill

Cedar Hill Building Inspections works out of the Government Center at 285 Uptown Boulevard, open weekdays from 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. Here is what the city's pages show about a roof job.

  1. Registration. The city says a person or company acting as general contractor must register with Cedar Hill, at a $100 fee for general contractors. Trades with a state license, such as electricians and plumbers, register each year with proof of that license. Roofing has no state license, so ask the roofer what it has on file with the city.
  2. The reroof permit. Residential reroof permits appear by name in the city's monthly reports of permits issued, month after month. Expect the roofer to take one out for your house and to give you the number.
  3. Inspection requests. Inspections can be requested through the online portal, by email or by phone at 972-291-5100 ext. 1093, before 4 p.m. on the day before.
  4. Questions. Building Inspections answers at 972-291-5100 ext. 1090. Fees and requirements change, so check before the job starts.

1856: the storm Cedar Hill still remembers

Cedar Hill's best known storm came early in its history. On April 29, 1856, a tornado struck the town, killing at least nine people and leaving only a building or two standing. The Dallas Herald reported on it that May, and the Dallas Pioneer history site has reprinted the account; local historians still debate whether the toll was nine or eleven.

Buildings and roofs have changed completely since then, but the season has not. Spring still brings the hail and wind that do most of the damage to Cedar Hill roofs. Searches for recent tornado damage surveys inside Cedar Hill did not turn up one, so none is claimed here.

After a storm on the hill

  • Look for limbs on the roof or hanging broken over it, and keep people and cars out from under them.
  • Check ceilings, especially near outside walls and skylights, and catch any drip.
  • Photograph the roof, gutters and yard from the ground, with the date.
  • Call your insurer to ask how the policy treats wind, hail and fallen trees, and what the deductible is.
  • Then call a roofer, and a tree service if a limb needs to come off first.

What to ask a Cedar Hill roofer

  1. "Are you registered with Cedar Hill?" Then confirm it with Building Inspections.
  2. "Will you pull the reroof permit and send me the number?" Before tear-off, not after.
  3. "Which limbs touch my roof, and what damage have they done?" Ask for photos.
  4. "How will you fasten shingles on this slope, and what wind rating do they carry?"
  5. "Will your insurance carrier send me a certificate directly?"
