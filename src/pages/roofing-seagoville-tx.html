A small city with a long storm memory

Seagoville began as Seago, laid out in 1876 by T. K. Seago. In 1910 the Post Office renamed it Seagoville so mail would stop going to a different Texas town called Sego. Today it is a city of 18,446 people, according to the 2020 census, on the southeast edge of Dallas County, with a small piece reaching into Kaufman County.

The city is also home to a federal prison. The Federal Correctional Institution in Seagoville opened on October 10, 1940, as a reformatory for women, held foreign nationals during World War II, and is now a low-security men's prison that remains one of the city's largest employers.

For homeowners, though, the date that matters most is September 8, 2010. That day a tornado came through, and roofing in Seagoville, TX has been a practical topic ever since.

September 8, 2010, in Seagoville

The Storm Prediction Center's log of storm reports for that day lists a tornado southwest of Seagoville, caught on live video, followed by a 65 mph wind report in the city itself. The next morning, Seagoville police told reporters the storm had left a path of damage about four and a half miles long in town.

  • An apartment complex in the city reported about 10 damaged units.
  • One resident's garage roof was torn off and landed on his gazebo.
  • A man made news for outrunning a tornado headed toward a Seagoville house.

A storm like that rarely leaves just one kind of damage. Torn shingles, lifted flashing, punctured decking and fallen limbs can all come from the same night, and some of it does not show until the next hard rain.

Permits and contractor registration in Seagoville

Seagoville's Permitting Department handles building permits and contractor registration, and its inspectors handle the inspections. The city moved the whole process online, so a roofer can create an account, register as a contractor, pull a permit and request inspections without visiting City Hall.

  1. Ask whether your job needs a permit. The city's FAQ says permits are needed when a building is constructed, remodeled or enlarged, and that cosmetic work does not need one. It also says to check with Building Inspections when in doubt. Have the roofer make that call, at 972-287-6829, and tell you the answer.
  2. Confirm the roofer is registered. Contractor registration runs through the same online portal. Ask the company for the name it is registered under.
  3. Keep the paperwork. If a permit is issued, save the number and the inspection result with your house records. A future buyer or adjuster may ask.

Rules and fees change; the city keeps its current fees in its master fee schedule. Confirm before work begins.

If a storm just came through Seagoville

  • Stay off the roof, and keep children and pets away from fallen limbs and debris.
  • Move furniture and electronics away from any drip and catch the water.
  • Photograph damage from the yard and from inside, and note the date and time.
  • If a line is down, stay well clear and report it. Seagoville spans two counties, so check your electric bill for the right utility to call, or call 911.
  • Call your insurer and ask how your policy covers wind and hail, what the deductible is, and how soon to report.
  • Then call a roofer to inspect, document and, if the roof is open, cover it until repairs.

Questions for a Seagoville roofer

  • Are you registered as a contractor with the City of Seagoville?

  • Can your insurance carrier send me a certificate of liability coverage directly?
  • Will the written estimate list tear-off, decking, underlayment, flashing and cleanup separately?
