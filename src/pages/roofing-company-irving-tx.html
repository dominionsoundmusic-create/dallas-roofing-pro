Irving puts reroofing on its permit list

Roofing in Irving, TX runs through the city's Inspections Department. When the City of Irving lists the everyday projects that need a building permit, reroofing is on it, and the city adds that the permit and inspections are required no matter who does the work: a contractor or the owner.

That second part catches people. A relative with a nail gun, a handyman who offers a weekend job, or a roofer who suggests skipping the paperwork to save a few days all land in the same place under Irving's rule. The permit is the city's record that a re-roof happened and was inspected, and it is the first thing a buyer's inspector or an insurance adjuster may ask about later.

  1. The application. Applications for Irving permits are filed online, on the city's portal. On a contracted job, the roofer normally files it.
  2. The fee. The city fee schedule in our research, a 2022 version, set re-roofing and roofing overlay permits at $100 per structure for single-family homes, townhouses and duplexes, and $150 for multifamily and commercial buildings. Confirm the current fee before you rely on it.
  3. The decking. If sheets of roof decking are replaced, Irving's answer is that they must meet the same requirement as new construction under the International Residential Code. The city does not add its own local thickness rule on top.
  4. The inspection. The work is inspected before the permit closes. Ask the roofer who schedules it and how you will know it passed.

Questions go to the Inspections Department at (972) 721-2371 or Irving-Permits@cityofirving.org. City Hall is at 825 W. Irving Blvd.

What an Irving permit tells you about the roofer

Texas does not license roofing contractors, and Irving's own guidance on contractor licenses lists state-licensed trades such as electrical, mechanical, plumbing and irrigation, not roofing. So the permit is one of the few official paper trails a roof job leaves.

A roofer that files the permit in its own name is putting its company on a city record for your address. One that tells you a permit is not needed for a full re-roof in Irving is telling you something the city's own list contradicts. Ask the Inspections Department whether the company you are considering must file anything else with the city before it starts.

The rest of the checking is yours: proof of liability coverage mailed or emailed to you by the roofer's insurance carrier, a signed contract that spells out every part of the job, a few nearby addresses where the company has worked, and proof that the shingle warranty was registered when the job ends.

October 20, 2019: the tornado that started next door

The tornado that tore through North Dallas on the night of October 20, 2019, began just east of Irving. The National Weather Service office in Fort Worth placed its start point about three miles east-northeast of Irving, in northwest Dallas near State Highway 348 and Luna Road. From there it moved east-northeast across Interstate 35E toward Richardson.

The survey numbers show how strong it was: peak winds estimated at 140 mph, a path of 15.76 miles and a width of up to 1,300 yards. It ended at 9:30 that night.

For Irving owners the takeaway is simple. Severe storms in this part of Dallas County do not respect city lines, and the edge of a storm's track can strip shingles and drop limbs on houses miles from the worst damage. After any hard storm, a look from the yard costs nothing.

What to look for from the ground

  • Pieces of shingle, ridge cap or vent cap in the yard or the street.
  • Bare or darker strips along the eaves and rakes, where wind lifts shingles first.
  • A limb resting on the roof, or one missing from a tree that hangs over it.
  • A new stain on a ceiling, or water at the top of a window or around a light fixture.
  • Dented gutters or downspouts, which can point to hail on the roof above them.

Renting in Irving? Who calls the roofer

If you rent and the roof is leaking, the call usually belongs to whoever owns or manages the property, so tell them first and do it in writing. Take dated photos of the water and the ceiling, move your things away from it, and keep a copy of what you sent. Do not hire a roofer for a rental yourself unless the lease or the owner says you may.

If you own a rental in Irving, the same permit rule applies to your property as to a house you live in. A tenant's photos and the roofer's inspection photos, shared with both sides, make it easier to agree on what needs fixing.

Questions to put to an Irving roofer

  • Will you pull the City of Irving permit in your company's name, and when will I get the number?
  • Who books the city inspection, and will you send me the result?
  • Is the permit fee in your price, or billed separately?
  • How many layers of shingles are on the house now, and is removing all of them in the quote?
  • If decking has to be replaced, what is the price per sheet, and will you photograph each sheet before you swap it?
  • Can I have your certificate of liability insurance sent directly from your insurer?
  • What does your labor warranty cover in writing, and who registers the manufacturer's warranty?
