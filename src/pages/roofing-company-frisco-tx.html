A city that grew six times over in twenty years

Frisco counted about 33,700 residents in the 2000 census, about 117,000 in 2010 and 200,509 in 2020. It sits in two counties, Collin and Denton, and much of its housing went up during those two decades.

For roofing in Frisco TX, that timeline matters more than any single storm. Homes finished in the early 2000s have now carried their shingles through twenty or more North Texas summers. Homes from the 2010s may already have a second roof because hail forced it. Either way, a large share of Frisco owners are buying a roof for the first time, and they are doing it in a city with clear rules about who can do the work.

DFW Expert Roofers is a free line, not a roofer. Call it, explain the problem, and you are transferred to an independent, owner-run roofing company that serves Frisco. That company sees the roof, prices it and does the job.

The Frisco R-Number, and why it is worth asking for

Frisco requires every contractor to register before it gets a permit. The registration carries a detail most cities do not spell out.

  1. The roofer registers online. Registration goes through eTRAKiT, the city's permit system, with company and contact details and the registration fee.
  2. Insurance comes first. The roofer sends a general liability certificate with the City of Frisco listed as holder. Until the documents arrive, the registration stays pending.
  3. The city issues an R-Number. That number lapses on the date the roofer's general liability policy ends. A lapsed policy means a lapsed registration.
  4. The permit follows. The city tells contractors to confirm their registration status before applying, since a permit is not issued to an unregistered contractor.

Ask any roofer for its Frisco R-Number and the expiration date on its liability certificate. If the two do not line up, or the roofer cannot produce either, keep looking.

Same shingle or something new: two permit paths

Self-service permit

The city's residential permit list includes a self-service permit for a roof replaced with the same material. A registered contractor gets it right away online, so it should not hold up a job.

Reviewed permit

Roof work that changes things, such as a different material, goes through the city's electronic plan review. Allow time for it, and ask the roofer to include it in the schedule it gives you.

Many Frisco subdivisions also have an HOA that approves roof colors and materials. Get the association's approval in writing before the permit goes in, so a crew is never waiting on a form. Keep the permit and final inspection records with the roof warranty.

Wind, not just hail

The 2017 storm is a reminder that straight-line wind does real harm here. Wind lifts shingle tabs, loosens ridge caps and works at chimney flashing, and the damage is easy to miss from the street. After a windy night, walk the yard for shingle pieces and look at the ceilings inside.

Check how your homeowners policy treats the roof before the next storm. Ask your agent whether roof damage is paid at replacement cost or actual cash value, what the hail and wind deductible is, and how long you have to report a claim.
