A city that mostly went up in the 1970s

Carrollton roofing starts with the age of the houses. According to the Handbook of Texas, the city grew from 13,855 people in 1970 to 40,595 in 1980, and almost three quarters of its year-round homes were built during that one decade.

A house framed in 1976 has seen close to fifty North Texas summers. Its first shingles are long gone, and many homes are now on a second or third roof. Each of those jobs left something behind: decking that has been nailed through several times, flashing reused from an earlier roof, vents and skylight curbs that were patched instead of replaced. That history matters more than the shingle color when you get a quote.

The city now stretches across parts of Dallas, Denton and Collin counties and passed 133,000 residents in the 2020 census, so newer homes sit north of the older core. Either way, the call works the same. DFW Expert Roofers is a free phone line, not a contractor. It listens, then connects you with an independent, locally owned roofing company that takes Carrollton calls.

Carrollton's line between "no permit" and "permit"

The city's Building Inspection re-roofing page draws the line at the structure. On older Carrollton homes that line gets crossed more often than people expect, because rot under the shingles only shows up after tear-off.

The page also says new decking may match the thickness of the old decking. Before any permit is issued, every contractor on the job must be registered with the city through CityServe. Registration is free and renews each year. Questions go to Building Inspection at 972-466-3225.

June 6, 2018: the morning the west side took the hail

Storms rolled across Dallas-Fort Worth before dawn on June 6, 2018. CBS News reported that the National Weather Service received some of its largest hail reports of the event from Carrollton and Coppell, and that Carrollton police confirmed significant property damage in the western part of the city, along Hebron Parkway and State Highway 121.

  • Across the metro: the Insurance Council of Texas put its first estimate at about 20,000 damaged structures, 25,000 damaged vehicles and roughly $425 million in insured losses.
  • Later estimate: the catastrophe modeler Karen Clark and Co. put insured damage near $1 billion, with most home damage expected to roofs, windows, skylights and solar panels.
  • In Carrollton: the city sent residents a warning about roofers going door to door after the storm, and the Better Business Bureau advised calling the insurance company before agreeing to any deal.

Many of those roofs were replaced that year, which means a lot of west Carrollton shingles are now around eight years old. Hail since then can leave bruises that are hard to see from the yard, so a roof that rode out a newer storm deserves a look before you assume it is fine.

Four Carrollton situations worth a call

The third roof on a 1970s house

Decking that has held several sets of nails can be soft or split. Ask how many layers are up there now and how the roofer will price wood it finds bad.

An old skylight or turbine vent

Curb flashing and worn vent bases are classic leak points on older roofs. A re-roof is the time to decide whether to replace, re-flash or deck over them.

A roof from the 2018 hail wave

Roofs replaced after June 2018 are now well into their life. If later storms passed through, a checkup helps you know where you stand before the next claim season.

A knock on the door after a storm

The city has warned about door-to-door roofers before. Calling a line you chose yourself, and checking the city registration, keeps the decision in your hands.

Before you sign with a Carrollton roofer

  • What company name is on your Carrollton contractor registration?
  • How many layers of shingles are on my roof, and will all of them come off?
  • If you find bad decking, who pulls the permit and what does each sheet cost?
  • What is your plan for my skylight, chimney flashing and vents?
  • Can your insurance agent send me your liability certificate directly?
  • Will the shingle warranty be registered in my name, and when do I get proof?
