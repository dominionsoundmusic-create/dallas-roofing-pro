Coppell's 2018 hail morning

For roofing in Coppell, TX, June 6, 2018 is the date people still mention. CBS News reported that some of the largest hail reports from that early morning storm came from Coppell and Carrollton.

NBC DFW spoke with a Coppell homeowner whose roof was only about three years old and was going to be replaced along with siding and gutters. Across the metro, the Insurance Council of Texas estimated that as many as 20,000 homes and 25,000 vehicles were damaged, with early insured losses around $425 million. A catastrophe modeler later put the total near $1 billion, mostly to roofs, windows, skylights and solar panels.

DFW Expert Roofers is a free phone line, not a roofing company. When you call, an automated assistant takes the details and transfers you to an independent, locally owned roofer that takes Coppell calls.

A suburb built in about twenty years

Coppell was a small farming community until DFW Airport opened next door. The census counted 3,826 residents in 1980, about 16,900 in 1990 and about 36,000 in 2000. According to the Handbook of Texas, by 2000 nearly all of the land zoned for homes had been built on. The 2020 census counted 42,983 people.

That means much of Coppell was framed within the same two decades, and many of those houses have already had at least one new roof. Coppell sits mainly in Dallas County, with a small area in the far north reaching into Denton County.

1980s homes

Built in the first wave. Find out the number of shingle layers, and whether decking has been replaced before.

1990s homes

Likely on a second roof. If that roof went on after 2018, keep its warranty paperwork where you can find it.

Newer infill

Fewer in number, since the city was nearly built out by 2000. Builder warranty terms may still apply.

Read Coppell's permit reports before you hire

Coppell publishes a monthly list of permits issued by work class. Residential reroofs show up every month, often with a short description of the work. That makes it a useful reference when you compare quotes.

  1. Open a recent month. Find the "Permits Issued by Work Class" report in the city's Document Center.
  2. Look at the reroof lines. Typical scopes read like "tear-off and replacement of existing roofing system, new underlayment, drip edge, flashing and architectural asphalt shingles."
  3. Compare with your quote. If your quote is missing underlayment, drip edge or flashing, ask why.
  4. Check the contractor. Anyone pulling a permit must be registered with the city first. Ask for the registered company name.
  5. Call with questions. Building Inspections: 972-304-3500.
