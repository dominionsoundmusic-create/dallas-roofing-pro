A town that has rebuilt its roofs before

Ask anyone who has lived in Lancaster for thirty years about roofing and the conversation tends to start with April 25, 1994. At 9:38 that night, one or more tornadoes went through the heart of town. A state historical marker on the square records three deaths and more than 200 homes and 26 businesses destroyed or badly damaged. The junior high, the library and City Hall all needed major repair.

The square had already burned once and come back in brick after fires in 1877 and 1889; in 1994 the storm hit it hard again. Eighteen years later, on April 3, 2012, another tornado cut across the north side of Lancaster on its way into south Dallas.

Lancaster sits in southern Dallas County, about fifteen minutes south of downtown Dallas, and counted 41,275 residents in 2020. Roofing in Lancaster, TX today runs under city rules that are stricter than many people expect, and they are the place to start.

Lancaster wants roofers on its books before the job

Texas has no state license for roofing, so the state never checks who is on your roof. The City of Lancaster fills part of that gap with its own registration. Its development handbook says builders and subcontractors must be registered with the city, and the contractor registration form names roofing contractors among the trades it covers.

Registration, renewed yearly

A registration is good for one year from the day it is issued. The 2025 to 2026 fee schedule lists building and general contractor registration at $100 a year. If someone other than the company owner registers, or it is done by mail, the form must be notarized.

Liability coverage on record

The form asks for a general liability policy of at least $500,000, with the City of Lancaster listed as certificate holder. That is coverage the city sees; ask the roofer's insurer to send you a certificate too.

A $100 roofing permit

The same fee schedule lists a roofing permit at $100. Registered contractors apply, track the permit and request inspections through the city's online portal.

The permit on the fence

Lancaster's handbook says a copy of the permit must be posted at the job site the whole time work goes on. If tear-off starts and nothing is posted, ask why before the next bundle goes up.

Questions about whether a company is registered go to the Building Division at (972) 218-1200, at City Hall, 211 N. Henry Street. The permit technicians answer at (972) 218-1201 and (972) 218-1205. Fees and forms change, so confirm the current rules before work starts.

Two Lancaster tornadoes, side by side

The two storms were very different, and both are why so many roofs in town are newer than the houses under them.

Before you sign with a Lancaster roofer

Because the city keeps a list, you can check more here than in many towns. Use it, and then ask the questions the city does not.

  1. Registration. Is your registration with Lancaster current for this year, and under what company name?
  2. Permit. When will you file the roofing permit through the portal, and where will the posted copy hang?
  3. Inspection. Who requests the city inspection, and will you send me the result?
  4. Insurance. Will your insurer email me a certificate, the same coverage you filed with the city?
  5. Scope. Does the written quote list tear-off, decking by the sheet, underlayment, flashing, vents and cleanup?
  6. Warranty. How long is your labor warranty, what does the paper say it covers, and who registers the shingle warranty?
