Roofs from the 1950s boom

Farmers Branch roofing is shaped by one fast decade. The census counted 13,441 people here in 1960, an increase of more than 1,300 percent over 1950, and many of its houses date from that era and the years right after it.

D Magazine has written about how midcentury modern design came to Farmers Branch, and houses of that period often have long, low rooflines. Low pitches drain more slowly than steep ones, and flat sections over carports, patios and later additions are the first places water tends to sit. A roofer who knows these houses will look at the transitions between the original roof and anything added later.

The city, in Dallas County, has also encouraged owners of aging homes to rebuild through its Demo/Rebuild incentive. For owners keeping the house they have, the roof is usually the bigger question. Two tornadoes that reached Farmers Branch

May 24, 2015: EF-1 from Dallas

A tornado that began near Walnut Hill Lane and Marsh Lane in Dallas tracked north-northwest, crossed LBJ Freeway at Josey Lane and moved into Farmers Branch, where an apartment complex lost part of its roof. The National Weather Service put winds at 100 to 105 mph along a 5.1-mile path. No one was hurt.

June 16, 2019: EF-0 near Mustang Trails Park

Two days later the Weather Service confirmed a brief EF-0, with winds near 85 mph, that touched down near Mustang Trails Park at about 3:45 p.m. Roofs at the Villa Creek apartments were torn, and 14 families were displaced. It was the fourth tornado confirmed from that Sunday's storms.

Both touched down on Sundays, and both hit apartment roofs hardest. Houses nearby can still lose shingles or ridge caps to the same winds, and that damage is easy to miss until the next heavy rain.

Call City Hall before the tear-off

Some North Texas cities publish exactly when a re-roof needs a permit. Farmers Branch's Building Inspection Division administers the city's building, zoning and property maintenance ordinances and takes permit applications online, but its re-roof rule was not one we could confirm. So make one call before any shingles come off.

  1. Describe the job. Tell Building Inspections whether it is shingles only or new decking as well.
  2. Ask about the permit. Find out whether one is needed and who should apply for it.
  3. Ask about registration. Some neighboring cities make every contractor register. Ask whether Farmers Branch does.
  4. Hold the roofer to it. Put the answer, and who is responsible for it, in the contract.

Questions for a Farmers Branch roofer

  • How many layers of shingles are on my house today?
  • What will you do with the low-slope or flat section over the carport or addition?
  • Did you check with the city about a permit for this job, and who applies?
  • How do you price replacement decking if you find rot?
  • Will your insurance agent email me a current liability certificate?
  • For an insurance job, does the contract state that the deductible is mine, as Texas law requires?
