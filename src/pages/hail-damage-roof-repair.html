Hail damage roof repair starts with knowing what hit the house

Hail damage roof repair in Dallas is a judgment call made one slope at a time. The roofer is asking how big the stones were, how many of them broke the shingle rather than just marking it, and whether the damage is scattered or spread across the whole roof.

From the driveway, most hail-hit roofs look fine. The marks are small: a dark spot where granules were knocked loose, a soft bruise you can feel with a thumb, a split along a shingle edge. The clearer evidence is often on the soft metal around the roof, which dents easily and shows how hard the storm hit.

How big does hail have to be to hurt a roof?

The National Weather Service treats hail one inch across, about the size of a quarter, as the line where a storm becomes severe. Many offices now tag their warnings with damage threat levels as well: golf ball hail of 1.75 inches is labeled considerable, and baseball hail of 2.75 inches is labeled destructive. Those sizes line up roughly with what roofers see on asphalt shingles.

Hail sizeRoughly likeWhat it tends to do to a shingle roof
Under 1 inchPea to nickelOften little lasting harm to a sound roof; may chip granules on older, brittle shingles and dent thin vent caps.
1 inchQuarterThe severe threshold. Bruising becomes possible, especially on roofs past their middle years.
1.75 inchesGolf ballBruises and cracks the mat on many asphalt shingles, dents gutters, and leaves clear marks on soft metal.
2.75 inches and upBaseball or largerCan fracture shingles outright, crack skylights and puncture decking; whole slopes are often written off.

Wind changes the picture. Hail driven sideways strikes one face of the house harder, so the west or north slopes can be badly hit while the opposite slopes look untouched.

Hail history across Dallas-Fort Worth

North Texas has been through some of the most expensive hailstorms in the country. A handful stand out in the record.

  • April 28, 1992, Fort Worth. A spring storm over Fort Worth appears on national lists of the costliest hailstorms on record.
  • May 5, 1995, Fort Worth. The Mayfest storm caught a crowd outdoors and dropped hail up to softball size. Damage across Tarrant and Dallas counties is commonly put at about $2 billion, though the earliest estimates were lower.
  • April 5, 2003, North Texas. A string of supercells crossed the area; the weather service office in Fort Worth wrote that the day may rank among the costliest hail events ever in the United States.
  • June 13, 2012, North Dallas. A severe storm sent large hail across North Dallas neighborhoods, and KERA reported it as one of the region's costly storms.
  • April 11, 2016, Wylie. A supercell hit Wylie hardest of all. The weather service reported about 80 percent of the city's homes affected and more than $300 million in damage, and KERA reported baseball-size stones and closed schools the next day.

Most of these storms arrived in spring, but hail can fall here in any warm month. A roof that has been through even one of them deserves an inspection before it is counted on for another decade.

Repair or a new roof after hail?

Hail repairs make sense when the hits are few and grouped: a handful of bruised shingles on one slope, a dented vent cap, a cracked pipe boot. The roofer replaces those pieces and blends in shingles that match. When bruises run across most slopes, patching becomes a losing game, because every slope that was not replaced keeps its hidden damage and the old and new shingles age at different rates.

Usually a repair

Damage limited to one area, a younger roof, shingles still available in the same color, no soft spots in the decking.

A closer call

Hits on two or three slopes, a roof in its middle years, or a discontinued shingle that no longer matches. Ask for both options priced.

Usually a new roof

Bruising across most slopes, cracked mats, brittle shingles near the end of their life, or decking damaged by very large stones.

If you file a claim, your insurer decides what it will pay for, not the roofer. Ask the insurer how it values the roof and how it treats matching, and keep the roofer's photos next to the adjuster's report so you can compare them.

Class 4 shingles and what the rating really tells you

Shingles sold as impact resistant are usually rated under UL 2218. To earn the top grade, Class 4, a sample takes two hits in one spot from a steel ball two inches across, and the sample must not crack or break through. It is a useful screen, but steel balls are not hailstones.

IBHS has rated impact-resistant shingles since 2019 using lab-made hailstones fired at real roof samples. In its 2025 round, IBHS rated 24 products; most earned Good and none earned Excellent. Its advice is to pick a shingle that carries Class 4 and also rates well on its list.

Some Texas insurers give a premium credit for Class 4 roofs, but it is not automatic and the amount varies. Before you pay extra for one, ask your insurer whether it offers a credit and what paperwork it needs.

Questions to ask the roofer about hail

  • How many hits per slope did you find, and can I see photos of each slope?
  • Which marks are hail and which are blistering, scuffs or ordinary wear?
  • Is this shingle still made in my color, and if not, what do you propose?
  • Will you meet the adjuster, and will you share your photos with me first?
  • If I replace, which shingles are you offering, and what impact rating does each carry?
  • Does my city need a permit for this job, and who will pull it?
