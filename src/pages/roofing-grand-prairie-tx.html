One city, three counties, one permit desk

Grand Prairie is long and narrow on the map. Most of it sits in Dallas County, a sizable slice lies in Tarrant County, and a small southern piece reaches into Ellis County, where part of Joe Pool Lake falls inside the city. The tax bill may come from different counties, but every roof job inside city limits answers to the same office: Grand Prairie Building Inspections.

That office is unusually plain about roofs. Its residential page says a permit is needed for any roof replacement, recover or repair. The contractor who files it must be registered with the city, and the application has to describe the work in detail and include the maker's specifications for the roofing going on.

Read the city's re-roof sheet like a bid checklist

Grand Prairie's re-roof guidelines list what a permit application must say. Ask every roofer you are comparing to put the same items in writing.

A real description of the work

The city's sample description runs through tearing off the existing shingles, checking for rot or damage, and replacing the underlayment, flashing, drip edge and vent boots. That is the level of detail a written quote should match.

Size and value

The application asks for square footage and the job's value. A bid that states the roof size in squares lets you compare one company's price against another's on the same footing.

The product, by name

Applicants attach the manufacturer's specifications for the roofing material. Ask which shingle line is on the quote, and whether its warranty will be registered in your name.

A registered contractor

The applicant needs a General Contractor registration with Grand Prairie and an account on the city's CSS portal. Ask for the company name on that registration and make sure it matches your contract.

Worn metal gets replaced

The guideline sheet repeats the code rule that existing flashings, edgings, outlets and vents that are rusted, damaged or deteriorated must be replaced, not reused. Ask how the roofer will judge yours.

Inspections to the 2021 code

The city inspects re-roofs to the 2021 International Residential Code. Ask who schedules the inspection, and keep the final payment until it has passed.

Tear off or lay over: the layer count decides

Grand Prairie quotes a code rule that ends many recover plans: no recover is allowed where the house already wears two or more applications of any kind of roof covering. Older homes that were re-roofed once by laying shingles over shingles are already at that limit.

Wind has peeled roofs here, too

Hail gets most of the attention in North Texas, but Grand Prairie's documented tornado damage has fallen on its industrial north end and its airport. On the night of March 16, 2023, the National Weather Service office in Fort Worth confirmed an EF-1 tornado that formed in the far northern part of the city, in a distribution facility area near West Trinity Boulevard and Trinity Ridge Drive. Surveyors estimated peak winds near 105 mph and found a section of roofing peeled off a warehouse before the tornado crossed into Irving and damaged an apartment building there.

In January 2017 another confirmed tornado touched down near the Grand Prairie airport and ripped doors off a hangar. No one was hurt in either storm.

Lifted and creased shingles after a strong wind are easy to miss from the street, and a peeled membrane on a low-slope roof can let water in long before anyone sees it. If a storm has passed, look from the ground and from inside, never from the roof.

Before you hire a roofer in Grand Prairie

  • Ask for the company's Grand Prairie General Contractor registration and check it against the name on your contract.
  • Ask whether the job will be a recover or a tear-off, and how many layers are on the roof today.
  • Get the permit number before the old shingles come off.
  • Have the roofer's insurance agent email you its liability certificate directly.
  • Get the written scope to match the city's sample description, line by line.
  • If insurance is involved, let the adjuster see the damage first, and remember the deductible is yours.
