A town that counts its trees

Choosing a Flower Mound roofing company means working in a town that protects its canopy. Flower Mound adopted a tree preservation ordinance with a goal of 35 percent canopy cover, and the native post oaks of the Cross Timbers come up again and again at Town meetings.

Those trees shade a lot of roofs. Branches that brush the shingles wear off granules, leaves pile up in valleys and gutters, and a limb that breaks in a spring storm can punch through decking. The trees also make the work harder: a crew has to protect them, stage materials around them and keep debris out of the beds below.

The town was incorporated on February 25, 1961, sits mainly in Denton County with a smaller piece in Tarrant County, and counted 75,956 residents in 2020. Whatever part of town you are in, DFW Expert Roofers works the same way: it is a free number, not a contractor, and it connects your call to an independent, locally owned roofing company that serves Flower Mound.

Flower Mound's permit and registration rules

Unlike towns that only want a permit when decking is replaced, Flower Mound's residential guidelines require a permit for any roof replacement.

Storm nights Flower Mound remembers

  1. March 23, 2016. Hail of 1.5 to 2 inches fell along a path nearly 50 miles long, from near Lewisville to east of Wylie, according to CoreLogic figures reported by Insurance Journal. Spotters logged hail in Flower Mound that night as well.
  2. June 11, 2023. Sunday evening storms dropped very large hail across southern Denton County. NBC DFW collected readers' photos from Flower Mound and Argyle, one describing 2 to 3 inch hail shortly after 8 p.m., and storm report logs list a social media report of hail over 4 inches near Cross Timbers Road and Shiloh Road.
  3. The days after. Hail that size can crack shingles, split vent caps and dent gutters and metal flashing. Bruises on shingles are often invisible from the ground, which is why a roof inspection after a storm like that is worth asking for.

Four Flower Mound reasons to pick up the phone

Limbs over the shingles

Post oaks and other mature trees rub, shade and drop debris on roofs. Ask the roofer to note every limb touching the roof and to check valleys for buildup.

Hail three inches across

After June 2023, plenty of roofs had damage that only shows up close. A written inspection with photos helps you decide whether to file a claim.

A permit for every replacement

Because the Town wants a permit on each roof replacement, a quote that leaves it out is incomplete. Ask who files it and what it costs.

Two electric utilities

Parts of town are served by Oncor and parts by CoServ. If a storm brings a line down, call the one that serves your address, or 911.

Questions for a Flower Mound roofer

  • Is your company on the Town's registered contractor list right now?
  • Will you file the roof permit in eTRAKiT, and when do I get a copy?
  • Which limbs should be trimmed, and does anything you suggest need a Town tree permit?
  • How will you protect the trees and beds around the house during tear-off?
  • Has my HOA approved the shingle and color, or do I need to submit it?
  • Can your insurance agent send me your liability certificate directly?
