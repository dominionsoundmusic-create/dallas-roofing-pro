Twenty years that built most of Mesquite

To understand roofing in Mesquite, TX, start with a census table. The town counted 1,696 people in 1950. By 1960 it had 27,526, and by 1970 it had 55,131.

That growth came from returning veterans and a housing shortage in Dallas, and it was met with block after block of new single-story homes. The roads and water lines had to catch up, and so did everything else. By about 1970 the LBJ Freeway, I-635, linked Mesquite with Garland and Balch Springs, and Town East Mall opened in 1971. The Mesquite Championship Rodeo, which began as a professional rodeo in 1958, gave the town its nickname as the Rodeo Capital of Texas.

A house framed in 1958 has lived through more than sixty North Texas summers. Its original roof is long gone, and it has almost certainly been re-roofed more than once. The questions a homeowner should ask on a roof that old are different from the questions on a 2010 build, and they start under the shingles: the condition of the decking, whether old layers were ever removed, and how the attic breathes.

Most of the city sits in Dallas County, with a small part reaching into Kaufman County. Both sides fall under the same City of Mesquite building rules.

Mesquite's license step comes before the permit

Mesquite handles contractors a little differently from many neighbors. The city's building FAQ puts it plainly: a professional license is required before a building permit can be issued.

The professional license

Contractors get it through the city's online Citizen Self Service portal. It is a city license, not a state one; Texas itself does not license roofers. Ask any company you are considering whether its Mesquite license is active.

Who can apply for permits

The portal accepts permit applications from licensed contractors, and from homeowners who own the house, live in it and carry a homestead exemption with the Dallas Central Appraisal District. For roof work, let the roofer apply.

What needs a permit

The city lists new construction, repairs, remodels, windows and exterior doors among permitted work and exempts cosmetic jobs like paint and carpet. Its rental inspection checklist names roofs, with inspections booked through the portal.

Who to ask

Mesquite Building Inspection answers at 972-216-6212, Monday through Friday, 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. Rules and fees change, so ask about your exact job before work begins.

The Saturday morning tornado of March 2019

At about 7:35 a.m. on Saturday, March 9, 2019, residents near Allen Drive heard rain, then hail, then a roar. The National Weather Service surveyed the damage and confirmed an EF-0 tornado, the weakest rating, with winds estimated at 75 to 85 mph. It tracked from the Allen Drive and Cedar Crest area east across I-635 and south Belt Line Road before lifting near Wilkinson and Paza.

Weak as it was, it took roofs. Homes on Allen Drive lost shingles and fences, one family's house was made unlivable, and part of the roof of a church blew off, with metal landing on the freeway. Police reported no injuries.

Most Mesquite roof damage comes from storms less dramatic than that one: spring wind, hail and long rain. The pattern after any of them is the same. Look from the yard, photograph what you see, call your insurer, then get a roofer to look up close.

Before you sign with a Mesquite roofer

  • Ask whether the company's City of Mesquite professional license is current, and whose name will be on the permit.
  • On a house from the 1950s or 1960s, ask how many old layers are up there and whether the price strips every one to bare wood.
  • Have the roofer explain how air enters and leaves the attic now, and what the new roof will change about it.
  • Ask what replacing a damaged sheet of decking will add to the bill, and ask to see photos of any sheet before it is swapped.
  • Request a certificate of liability insurance mailed or emailed to you by the insurer, not a photocopy.
  • Get the workmanship warranty and the shingle maker's warranty registration in writing.
