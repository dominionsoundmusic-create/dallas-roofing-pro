Tarrant County's oldest town, on the edge of a big lake

Roofing in Grapevine, TX covers more history than most suburbs can claim. Settlers founded Grape Vine Prairie in 1844, making it the oldest settlement in Tarrant County, and the 1888 arrival of the Cotton Belt rail line turned it into a farm trade center.

The city incorporated in 1907. Then, in 1952, the Army Corps of Engineers finished Grapevine Lake, built under the River and Harbors Act of 1945 to hold back Trinity River floods. Today the lake wraps the city's north side, DFW Airport sits to the south, and Grapevine's limits reach into three counties: Tarrant, Dallas and Denton.

That mix produces three different kinds of roof call: older homes near the historic downtown, decades of suburban houses across the rest of the city, and commercial roofs around the hotels, shops and offices. December 13, 2022: two tornadoes in one morning

The storm Grapevine residents remember best came before dawn in mid-December, during a North Texas outbreak in which the National Weather Service confirmed 16 tornadoes.

  1. The first track. NWS surveyors rated it EF-1 with winds near 110 mph. It ran from around the Sam's Club area to southwest of the Gaylord Texan and lifted at the lakeshore.
  2. The second track. A short EF-1, near 100 mph, touched down at Grapevine Mills, damaging the mall and nearby stores.
  3. Homes hit. Houses along Lucas Drive near Northwest Highway had roof and tree damage, and the city counted more than 40 structures affected.
  4. The toll. Mayor William D. Tate reported five minor injuries and no deaths.

Wind damage is not always obvious. Shingles can be lifted and creased without falling off, which breaks their seal and lets the next rain in. After a storm, look from the yard for shingles out of line or missing, and call your insurer before permanent repairs.

What Grapevine expects from a re-roof

The City of Grapevine spells out its residential re-roofing rules on a single sheet from Building Services, at 200 S. Main Street. A homeowner who knows them can spot a roofer who does not.

A permit comes first

Grapevine will not let a re-roof start without one. The work follows the 2021 International Residential Code, Section R908 for re-roofing.

No shingles over shingles

Asphalt shingles may not be installed over an existing roof covering. Every asphalt re-roof here is a tear-off, which exposes the decking for a look.

Vents done right

The sheet calls for roof vents to be properly connected and flashed as part of the permit. Ask the roofer how it handles each pipe and vent.

Registered roofers

Grapevine registers roofing contractors as general contractors, and registration is required before permitted work. Owner-occupants are the exemption.

Inspections are on you too

The city puts the duty to get inspections on the permit applicant and the property owner. If inspections are not passed, the permit expires.

Who to call

Inspections are requested at 817-410-3010. Permit and registration questions go to Building Services at 817-410-3165.

Roofs in Grapevine's historic districts

Grapevine protects its old downtown and several historic districts. For a house in one of them, or a designated landmark, a Certificate of Appropriateness must be approved before exterior alterations. City staff can approve simple, routine changes within a few days of the application; bigger projects go before the Historic Preservation Commission, which meets once a month.

The district design guidelines include a roof section. They favor repairing original features and replacing them only when they are past repair, with new material that matches the old in design and appearance. A Certificate of Appropriateness is not a building permit, so the roofer still pulls the regular re-roof permit. Before choosing shingles for a historic house, call the preservation office at 817-410-3197. Staff help costs nothing.
