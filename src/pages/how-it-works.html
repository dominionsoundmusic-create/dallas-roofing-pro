A call, from start to finish

Most calls take two or three minutes. Here is the order of things, using a typical spring evening in North Texas as the example: hail went through an hour ago, and there is now a brown ring on the upstairs ceiling.

  1. You dial {{ site.business.phone_display }}. The automated assistant picks up immediately, whatever the hour.
  2. It asks what kind of help you need. The number serves roofing, air conditioning and heating, and pressure washing, so the first question sorts your call. Say roofing.
  3. It asks about the roof. In a sentence or two: hail, a leak, missing shingles, a tree limb, a worn-out roof, a commercial building. It also asks your town and how urgent it is.
  4. It transfers you. The line rings through to a locally owned, independent roofer working in your part of DFW. Before it picks up, the company hears a short summary of what you said, so you do not have to start over.
  5. If the company cannot answer, your name, callback number, town, need and urgency are passed to it so it can call you back.
  6. From there you deal with the roofer. It sets a time to look at the roof, inspects it, gives you its own estimate, and if you hire it, does the work. You pay it directly, or your insurer pays as you arrange.

What the line does, and what it never does

The line does

  • Answer every call with an automated assistant, day and night
  • Ask a few questions so the roofer knows what to expect
  • Transfer you, or pass your details for a call back
  • Publish guides on hail, permits, costs and Texas claim law

The line never

  • Climbs on a roof, inspects, quotes, repairs, replaces or tarps
  • Sells shingles or any other product
  • Files, negotiates or manages insurance claims
  • Charges you, or promises anything about a company's work, price or schedule

Why it is set up this way

After a big hailstorm, homeowners across Dallas-Fort Worth face the same problem at once: hundreds of roofing companies, signs in every yard, crews knocking on doors, and no simple way to know who is local. A single number that reaches a locally owned company cuts down the searching.

The line is paid the way many referral services are: companies that receive calls may pay it a referral fee. That keeps the call free for you. It also means you should treat the connection as a starting point, not an endorsement. Ask the questions on the roofer checking guide no matter who you hire.

Good questions for the roofer who calls you

  • Do you charge to come out and look at the roof?
  • Will you send a certificate of liability insurance from your insurance agent?
  • Does my city require a permit or contractor registration, and who pulls the permit?
  • Can I have the estimate in writing, with the shingle brand and line, decking price and warranty?
  • Who will be on my roof: your own employees or a subcontracted crew?
  • What payment do you expect, and when?
