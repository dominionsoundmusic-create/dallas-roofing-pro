What a roof inspection in Dallas is for

A roof inspection in Dallas answers one practical question: what condition is this roof in right now, and what, if anything, should be done about it? The answer is only as good as the evidence behind it, which is why the photos and the written report matter more than the visit itself.

People book one for different reasons. Some just had golf ball hail on the patio. Some are buying an older brick ranch house and want to know if the roof has five years left or five months. Some are watching a stain spread across the ceiling. The same inspection covers all of these, but the report should speak to the reason you called.

Good times to have a North Texas roof inspected

After a storm

The National Weather Service treats hail an inch across and wind of 58 mph as severe. If a storm like that crossed your street, have the roof checked even if it looks fine. Insurance policies set their own deadlines to report damage, and the Texas Department of Insurance advises reporting as soon as you know.

Around a home sale

Sellers who know the roof's condition before listing avoid a surprise in the buyer's report. Buyers who hear "the roof has little life left" from a home inspector can bring in a roofer for a closer look and a price before the option period ends.

As the roof ages

Heat in a Texas summer wears hardest on sealants, rubber pipe boots and the shingles facing south and west. A periodic check on an older roof finds a failing boot or lifted ridge before it becomes a ceiling stain.

When something changes

Granules collecting at the downspouts, a musty attic, daylight through the decking, a drip after a heavy rain: each is a reason to call before the next storm.

Before a warranty ends

A workmanship warranty on a newer roof often runs a limited number of years. An inspection shortly before it expires can catch installation problems while the installer still owes the fix.

Before adding to the roof

Solar panels, a new skylight or a room addition all depend on the roof under or beside them. Knowing its remaining life first can save taking panels down to re-roof a few years later.

Home sale inspections and storm checks are not the same visit

The Texas Real Estate Commission licenses the professional inspectors who check houses for buyers and sellers. Under its standards of practice, the inspector reports on the roof covering along with the rest of the house, and may stay off a roof that cannot be reached safely or that walking could damage, as long as the report explains why. That report is a snapshot of the whole home, not a roofing diagnosis.

Roofers are a different case. Roofing contractors hold no state license in Texas, and a roofer's inspection follows no state standard. It tends to go deeper on the roof itself, and it often comes from a company that would like to do the work. Neither is better in every case; they answer different questions.

Your part before and during the visit

  • Note the dates of any storms you remember, and pull out photos of hail or debris you took at the time.
  • Find the roof's age, the last re-roof invoice or the warranty papers if you have them.
  • Clear a path to the attic access and move stored items away from it.
  • Point out every ceiling stain, even old ones, so the roofer can check above them.
  • Stay on the ground. Watch from the yard if you like, and ask to see the photos afterward.
  • Ask whether the report will separate storm damage from normal wear.

Questions to ask the roofer who inspects

  1. Is there a fee, and what will I receive? Many roofers inspect at no charge; others charge for a formal written report. Confirm it on the call.
  2. Will you walk every slope and go into the attic? A look from the ladder or the street misses most hail and wind damage.
  3. Can I keep the photos? They belong in your records whether or not you hire this company.
  4. Which findings are storm damage and which are age? The difference matters for any insurance claim.
  5. What would you repair rather than replace? A roofer who only ever recommends a full roof deserves a second opinion.
