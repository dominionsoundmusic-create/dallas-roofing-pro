From melon fields to a city of large homes

Roofing companies in Colleyville, TX work in a town that did not exist as a city until 1956. Its own history page says settlers arrived in the mid-1800s and that the name honors Dr. Lilburn Howard Colley, a doctor who came in the 1880s.

For decades this was farm country, known in the early 1900s for melons, squash and tomatoes. Colleyville incorporated in early 1956 with fewer than two square miles and roughly 250 residents. The big change came with DFW Airport: the city says being close to it fueled home building in the late 1970s and early 1980s, and Colleyville now counts more than 25,000 residents.

That history shows on its rooftops. Houses from the first building wave are now well past one roof cycle, while later homes tend to be larger, with complex roofs full of hips, valleys and dormers that take more skill to flash and more material to replace.

What Colleyville's building office lets you check

Colleyville Building Inspections, at 100 Main Street, keeps a closer eye on roof work than many homeowners realize. Each of these is a way to tell one roofing company from another.

Online-only registration

The city registers contractors and takes permits only through its online CSS portal. It does not take registrations by email or mail. A roofer working here regularly will already have an account.

Reroof permits on the record

Reroofs are among the permit types the city tracks and publishes on its Permits Issued page. Ask the roofer for your permit number, then watch for it.

Virtual inspections

Since October 1, 2023, reroofs have been eligible for the city's virtual inspection program, and there is a Final Roof Inspection Waiver procedure. Ask the roofer which route your job will take and what photos it will submit.

Rules and forms change; the city updated its submittal guidelines for projects filed on or after April 1, 2025. The permit technician line is 817-503-1030.

HOA approval and the shingle you choose

Many Colleyville neighborhoods have an association with an architectural review step. Section 202.011 of the Texas Property Code limits how far that review can go. An association may not prohibit or restrict shingles whose main purpose is standing up to hail and wind, shingles that give better heating and cooling efficiency than ordinary composition shingles, or solar shingles.

The condition is appearance: once installed, they must resemble shingles used or approved in the subdivision. So submit the product sheet and color before work is scheduled, and get the approval in writing. Whether a sturdier shingle changes your premium is a question for your insurance agent.

Storms and power lines in Colleyville

Colleyville shares the Mid-Cities' spring hail. On May 27, 2024, the Storm Prediction Center's report log lists ping-pong-ball hail in Colleyville, on a night when neighboring Bedford and Euless reported stones up to baseball size. After any storm like that, check from the ground and from inside, and call your insurer before permanent repairs.

Electric service here is not all one company. The city's utility page lists outage reporting for both Oncor and Tri-County Electric Cooperative, so know which one serves your street before a storm.
