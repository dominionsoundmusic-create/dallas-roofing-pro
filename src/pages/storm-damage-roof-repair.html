When a North Texas storm tears into a roof

Storm damage roof repair in Dallas usually begins with something you can see: a limb across the ridge, shingles scattered on the driveway, a patch of bare felt, or a ceiling stain that appeared overnight. Underneath that obvious damage there is usually more, and finding all of it is what separates a lasting repair from a patch that leaks in the next storm.

Thunderstorms here bring three threats at once. Straight-line wind lifts and rips shingles, hail bruises them, and the trees that shade so many DFW streets drop branches onto the roof. Tornadoes add a fourth, rarer threat that can lift whole sections of decking. Each leaves its own marks, and a good roofer checks for all of them instead of fixing only the one you pointed out.

DFW Expert Roofers is a phone line, not a contractor. It answers at any hour, takes down what happened, and transfers you to an independent roofing company that is locally owned and works in your part of the metroplex. That company handles the inspection, the estimate and the repair.

Storms that left their mark on DFW roofs

The National Weather Service calls a thunderstorm severe once it produces wind of 58 mph or hail an inch across. Storms in this region pass that bar every spring, and several have done damage on a citywide scale.

May 28, 2024: the morning windstorm

A line of storms tore across Dallas early on a Tuesday morning. Fox Weather reported a 75 mph gust at Dallas Love Field and 83 mph near Denton, and DFW Airport logged 77 mph. Trees, fences and roofs were damaged from downtown to North Dallas and Irving, Dallas County declared a disaster, and Oncor later called it the most impactful storm ever in its Dallas County service area.

October 20, 2019: North Dallas tornado

An EF3 tornado cut a path through North Dallas on a Sunday night, the National Weather Service in Fort Worth found, damaging homes, schools and shopping centers along its track. Roofs near the path lost decking as well as shingles, which means a full structural look before any new roofing goes on.

December 26, 2015: Garland and Rowlett

Twelve tornadoes touched down in North Texas the day after Christmas. Texas Public Radio reported that hundreds of homes were damaged in Rowlett and Garland, a reminder that storm season here does not end in June.

Hail has its own long record in the region; the hail damage page covers it, and the wind damage page goes deeper on shingles that lift and crease.

What storm repair on a roof usually includes

The word "repair" covers a wide range after a storm. On one house it means replacing a dozen shingles near the ridge; on the next it means new decking under the spot where a limb came through, new flashing, and a ceiling that has to dry out before anything is painted. The scope a roofer writes up should name each of these pieces separately.

The surface

Missing, torn or creased shingles; ridge caps blown off; loose drip edge; hail bruises across a slope. Replacement shingles should match as closely as the market allows, and the estimate should say what happens if an exact match is no longer made.

The structure

Punctured or cracked plywood decking, a broken rafter, or a sagging section where a heavy limb landed. Structural work is priced and permitted differently from shingle work, so ask whether any is included and how it will be measured.

The openings

Flashing at walls and chimneys, pipe boots, attic vents and skylights. Wind and debris bend and loosen these metal and rubber parts, and a leak at one of them often gets blamed on the shingles.

The inside

Wet insulation, stained drywall, a swollen ceiling. Many roofing companies stop at the roof deck and leave interior repairs to others, so ask early who handles what and get it in writing.

From the storm to the finished repair: a timeline

No two storms play out the same way, and how quickly a roofer can come depends on that company and on how many roofs the storm hit. The order of events, though, is fairly steady. Here is how a storm repair in Dallas-Fort Worth usually unfolds.

  1. While the storm is still overhead. Stay inside and away from windows. Nobody, homeowner or roofer, should be on a roof during lightning or high wind.
  2. Once it passes. Walk the yard if it is safe and there are no downed lines. Photograph the roof from several angles, any shingles or limbs on the ground, and every wet spot inside. Note the date and time.
  3. The first call. Ring the free line and say what you see. You are transferred to a local roofing company; if it cannot answer, it receives your name, number, town and how urgent things are so it can call back.
  4. Keeping the rain out. If the roof is open, the roofer you reach can talk with you about a tarp or a temporary patch. Ask what it costs, how it will be fastened, and that it be photographed before and after.
  5. The inspection. The roofer checks each slope, the flashing, the vents and the attic, then shows you photos of what it found. Many roofers look at storm damage at no charge; ask on the call.
  6. The claim, if you file one. You report the damage to your insurer. The Texas Department of Insurance advises telling your company as soon as you know about damage, since policies set their own reporting deadlines.
  7. The adjuster's visit. The insurer sends an adjuster. You can ask your roofer to be there to point out what it found, but the claim stays between you and your insurer.
  8. Permit and scheduling. If the job is large enough to need a permit in your city, the roofer applies for it. You get a written contract with the scope, materials, price and start date before work begins.
  9. The repair and the paperwork. After the work, ask for the final invoice, photos of the finished roof, the permit final if one was pulled, and the warranty terms.

Permits for storm repairs in Dallas

The City of Dallas spells out its storm rules on its building inspection pages. Small fixes, such as replacing a few shingles or a vent, do not need a permit. Replacing a large section of the roof, or the whole roof, does. When only shingles and decking are involved, the roofing permit can be issued over the counter or online, but work on the framing calls for a building permit.

Dallas also requires a registered contractor on the permit application. A homeowner doing the work personally can file an exemption instead, which is one more reason to be wary when a door knocker asks you to pull the permit in your own name. The city's building inspection line for these questions is 214-948-4480.

Other DFW cities write their own rules, and some require roofers to register before they work there. Each town page notes what that city requires.

Storm chasers, contracts and your deductible

After a storm makes the evening news, trucks from out of the area roll into the hardest-hit neighborhoods within days. Some do honest work. Others take a deposit, put on a roof that fails early, and are gone by the time the warranty matters. A few signs deserve a second look: pressure to sign the same afternoon, a contract with blank lines, a request for most of the money up front, and any talk of making your deductible "go away."

Texas law is clear on that last one. A contractor may not waive or absorb your insurance deductible, and a contractor may not negotiate your claim as if it were a public adjuster. Read the contract slowly, keep a copy, and do not sign an assignment of your insurance benefits without understanding what it hands over.

Questions to put to the roofer before you sign

  • Which parts of this damage came from the storm, and which are age or wear? Show me in the photos.
  • Is any decking or framing broken, and how will extra sheets of plywood be priced?
  • Does my city require a permit or contractor registration for this job, and who is getting it?
  • What shingle are you matching, and what happens if the exact color is discontinued?
  • Can I see a certificate of insurance sent straight from your insurance agent?
  • What warranty covers your labor, how long does it last, and is it in writing?
