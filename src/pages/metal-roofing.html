Why metal keeps coming up in DFW roof conversations

After a hail season, a lot of North Texas homeowners start asking whether the next roof should be something other than asphalt shingles. Metal roofing in Dallas is one of the most common answers, along with the questions that come with it: which panel, which color, what it does in hail, whether the HOA will allow it and what it costs.

Standing seam, exposed fastener and other metal profiles

The biggest difference between metal roofs is how the panels are held down. That choice affects the look, the price, the upkeep and where the roof is allowed.

Gauge is the panel's thickness: the lower the number, the thicker the steel. Some cities set a floor. Garland's re-roofing rules, for example, call for metal roofing of 26 gauge or heavier installed to the manufacturer's specifications, and a change of roofing material there needs a permit. Ask the bidder to name the gauge, the coating (galvanized or aluminum-zinc) and the paint system in writing.

Hail ratings and Texas insurance credits

What Class 4 means

UL 2218 tests a roofing product by dropping steel balls on it; to reach Class 4, the top rating, a sample has to survive a 2-inch steel ball dropped twice on the same spot without cracking through. The rating is about penetration, not looks. A Class 4 metal panel can still dent in a big storm, and dents show more on flat, glossy panels than on ribbed or textured ones.

The insurance side

The Texas Department of Insurance says insurers give credits for roofing rated under UL 2218, with Class 4 earning the largest, but each insurer sets its own amount and paperwork. The product label should show the class, maker, year and brand, and the roofer fills out TDI form PC068 after installation. Ask your insurer for the credit amount and whether your policy treats dents that do not leak as covered damage.

The insurer-funded research group IBHS has rated impact-resistant shingles with lab-made hailstones since 2019, which is a useful comparison if you are choosing between a metal roof and an impact-rated shingle. Ask any bidder for the product's test reports, not just a brochure.

Metal roof color ideas for North Texas houses

Color choice is partly taste and partly physics. Lighter colors, and darker pigments engineered as cool colors, bounce back more sunlight than ordinary dark paint, which can keep an attic cooler through a DFW summer. The way to compare is the product's tested solar reflectance and thermal emittance. The Cool Roof Rating Council publishes those values for rated products, and since ENERGY STAR stopped labeling roof products on June 1, 2022, the CRRC directory is the place to look rather than an ENERGY STAR sticker.

  • Charcoal, black and dark bronze suit modern farmhouse and contemporary homes; ask whether a cool-pigment version is offered.
  • Galvalume and light gray fit barns, shops and ranch homes and tend to be among the more reflective finishes.
  • Barn red, forest green and other classic colors appear on rural buildings across North Texas; check fade and chalk warranty terms for each color.
  • Brown and tan tones often blend best with brick and stone in suburban HOAs.

Before falling for a color, check the rules that apply. Fort Worth's design guidelines for the Fairmount historic district say metal roofs need a certificate of appropriateness, allow standing seam on secondary roof slopes and list R-panel and rib panel profiles as prohibited. Many HOAs keep an approved color list.

What a metal roof costs in Dallas-Fort Worth

Two published sources give very different numbers, and both are worth knowing. Angi's Dallas cost page reports an average metal roofing project of about $12,474, with a range from $4,118 to $20,829. The Journal of Light Construction's Cost vs. Value report for 2025 prices its model job, about 3,000 square feet of standing seam metal roofing on a hip roof, at $46,790 in Dallas, $44,505 for the West South Central region and $51,865 nationally, and estimates that about 49 percent of the Dallas cost is recouped at resale. The difference comes from what each one measures: Angi averages jobs of all sizes, including small buildings and partial roofs, while the Cost vs. Value job is a complete standing seam roof on a fairly large house.

Panel profile, gauge, roof complexity (valleys, dormers, chimneys), tear-off and decking repairs, underlayment, trim and the paint system all move the number. None of these figures is a price from the line or from any roofer, and real quotes vary. Get every bid itemized so you can compare one panel system with another.

Questions to ask a metal roofer before you sign

  1. Which panel, gauge and coating? Brand, profile, thickness, metallic coating and paint system, in writing.
  2. What is the impact rating? Ask for the UL 2218 class and the label or test report you will need for the TDI form.
  3. What goes under the panels? Tear-off or not, decking repairs, the underlayment and how ventilation will work.
  4. How are the details handled? Valleys, walls, chimneys, pipe penetrations and ridge closures are where metal roofs leak.
  5. Which warranties apply? The panel maker's paint and finish warranty, any weathertightness warranty, and the installer's own workmanship terms.
  6. Does the city or HOA need to approve it? Who pulls the permit, and is written HOA approval in hand before work starts?

If you own acreage in Parker, Hood, Johnson or Ellis County and want a house, barn and shop roofed at the same time, say so on the call; some roofers handle residential panels and agricultural buildings, and some do only one.
