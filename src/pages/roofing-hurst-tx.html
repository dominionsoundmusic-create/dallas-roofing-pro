A town that went up in a hurry

Roofing in Hurst, TX starts with the town's age. The city's own history says the Bell helicopter plant opened in 1951 and touched off a building boom, and Hurst incorporated on September 25, 1952 with about 2,700 people.

Growth did not slow. The city counts roughly 5,700 residents five years after incorporation and more than 20,000 by the mid-1960s. A 1953 Fort Worth Star-Telegram photo in the UTA archive shows rows of new houses going up and notes that land values had climbed 200 to 1,000 percent. A 1955 aerial view looking south from Pipeline Road already shows Highway 183, Brown Trail and the Bell plant. North East Mall followed in 1972.

What that means today: many Hurst houses are past their sixtieth birthday. Their roofs have been replaced before, sometimes more than once, and each time someone made choices about decking, layers and vents that the next roofer inherits. DFW Expert Roofers does not inspect any of it. Its job is narrower: answer the phone for free and hand your call to an independent, locally owned roofing company working in Hurst, which takes it from there.

How Hurst handles roofing permits now

Hurst changed how it tracks contractors. Instead of a yearly sign-up, the paperwork rides along with each permit. Here is what the Building Inspections pages describe.

ItemWhat the city saysWhat to ask your roofer
Contractor registrationNo annual registration. The contractor registers on each permit application.Will you pull the permit for my job, under your company name?
Documents with the permitA copy of a US-issued ID, a trade license if applicable, and a copy of general liability insurance.May I see the same insurance certificate you give the city?
Where it is filedThe online Citizen Self-Service portal, using a company name and company email, not a personal one.What is the permit number once it is issued?

One loose end: a Public Works fee page still lists a yearly contractor registration fee, which does not match the per-permit process. If anything is unclear about your job, Building Inspections answers at 817-788-7088.

What a careful roofer looks at on a mid-century Hurst roof

How many layers are up there

A house roofed in the 1950s or 1960s has had several roofs. If one was laid over another, the weight and the hidden decking both matter. Ask the roofer to count the layers and write the number down.

The decking under the shingles

Decking on an older house may be original. Only a tear-off shows whether it is sound. Ask what one replacement sheet costs before work starts, so a surprise does not become a fight.

Vents, pipes and chimneys

Decades of remodeling add vents and pipe boots in odd places, and older chimneys carry their own flashing. Leaks often start at those spots rather than in the open field of shingles.

Attic airflow

A roof that cannot breathe runs hot under a North Texas summer sun. Ask the roofer to check the attic's intake and exhaust vents and say whether the quote changes them.

Before you sign with a roofer in Hurst

  • Ask for the general liability certificate to be sent to you by the roofer's insurance agent, the same coverage the city asks for on the permit.
  • Confirm the company name on the contract matches the name on the Hurst permit.
  • Get the layer count, the decking price per sheet and the underlayment by product name in writing.
  • Ask who files the city's final roof certificate and when you will see it.
  • For an insurance job, read the deductible notice in the contract. Texas requires it.
