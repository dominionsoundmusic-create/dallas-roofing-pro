A courthouse town where the city keeps the paperwork

Weatherford is the county seat of Parker County, the place where the 1886 courthouse still anchors the square and where the railroad first arrived in 1880. It counted 30,854 people in 2020, in a county of 148,222 that keeps adding rooftops on former ranch land west of Fort Worth.

For anyone shopping for roofing in Weatherford, Texas, the useful detail is at City Hall. Weatherford requires every contractor working inside city limits to register first, and the sign-up packet wants a photo ID plus a liability policy with limits of $300,000 or more. That is a real record you can ask about, in a state that does not license roofers at all.

Inspection, pricing, labor and billing all belong to that company; you settle up with it, not with the line.

How a Weatherford roof job moves through City Hall

Taken from the City of Weatherford's residential permit pages, its contractor registration page and its monthly permit reports.

  1. The roofer registers with the city. All contractors working inside city limits must be registered. The application needs a general liability certificate showing at least $300,000 in coverage and a photo ID.
  2. The roofer applies for a Roof Repair or Replace permit. The city's residential page lists roof replacement and major roof repairs among work that generally needs a permit. Applications are filed online, and plan review usually takes two to three business days.
  3. Work is inspected. The city schedules inspections at set stages and warns against covering work before it is inspected.
  4. You keep the final inspection. The city notes that many lenders will not finance improvements or repairs without proof of a final inspection, which matters if you refinance or sell.

Questions go to the permit office at (817) 598-4282, extension 2. Small repairs usually do not need a permit, so ask before assuming either way. Homes on county land outside city limits fall outside this process; have the roofer confirm which rules reach your property.

The March 2023 storm that took copper off City Hall

Weatherford's own government building shows what a hard wind can do. During the storm of Thursday, March 2, 2023, covered by the Fort Worth Star-Telegram, copper paneling was torn from the roof of City Hall, large trees and power lines came down, billboards north of downtown were blown over, and residents of two apartment complexes had to leave their homes. Weatherford ISD canceled classes the next day, and about 10,000 city electric customers lost power.

City officials first suspected a tornado. A National Weather Service meteorologist said the damage looked like straight-line winds, and the Parker County fire marshal reported winds of 80 to 90 mph at five or six places in the county. CBS Texas has separately reported a suspected tornado that tore roofs, split trees and flattened fences in a Weatherford neighborhood.

Straight-line wind is easy to underrate because nobody names it. It lifts shingle tabs, peels ridge caps, bends metal trim and drops limbs, often on a whole street at once.

Shingles or metal on Parker County land

Out past the city limits, many properties carry more than one roof: a house, a barn, a shop. Owners often ask whether to stay with shingles or switch to metal. The honest answer depends on the building, so it helps to see the trade-offs side by side before a roofer prices both.

The line does not recommend one material over another. It connects you with a roofing company, and that company should explain its choice in writing.

Four reasons Weatherford calls come in

A windstorm hit the street

Wind strong enough to strip copper from City Hall can lift shingles across a neighborhood. People want the damage seen and written up before more rain falls.

A barn or shop roof is failing

Acreage owners call about outbuildings as often as houses, and ask whether metal makes sense for the whole property.

Refinancing or selling

Because lenders often want proof of a final inspection, owners prefer a roofer who pulls the city permit rather than one who skips it.

A storefront near the square

Older commercial buildings downtown have their own leaks and low-slope sections, and the owners need a company that handles commercial roofs.

Questions worth asking any Weatherford roofer

  • Are you registered with the City of Weatherford, and can I see the certificate of insurance you filed?
  • Will you pull the Roof Repair or Replace permit, and is the fee in your price?
  • Will I get a copy of the final inspection for my records and my lender?
  • Does the bid include a full tear-off, and how is rotten decking priced if it turns up?
  • What wind rating do the shingles or panels carry, and who registers the manufacturer warranty?
  • Can you give me addresses in Parker County where you have finished roofs?
