Plano checks more than shingles

Most Plano TX roofing companies already know the drill here: the city wants a permit for the re-roof, a contractor that is registered with Building Inspections, and a final inspection that does not stop at the shingles. The inspector goes into the attic.

That attic visit is the part homeowners rarely hear about. According to the City of Plano, the final roof inspection confirms that vent pipes through the roof are in place, that the rafters are in position, and that the water heater and furnace exhaust vents were reinstalled properly. A tear-off crew working fast can bump a gas flue out of its collar. If it is not reseated, exhaust can stay in the attic or the house instead of leaving through the roof.

So when you compare roofers in Plano, you are comparing more than shingle prices. You want a company that files for the permit under its own registration, shows up on the city's list, and treats the attic as part of the job.

DFW Expert Roofers is not one of those companies. It is a free phone line. An automated assistant answers, asks about your roof and your part of Plano, and transfers the call to an independent roofing company, owned locally, which handles the inspection, the estimate and the work.

Plano's re-roof rules, in the order they come up

These come from the City of Plano's roof permit page, its residential re-roof handout and its contractor registration requirements. Rules change, so confirm with Plano Building Inspections at 1520 K Avenue before work starts.

  1. The roofer registers with the city. Plano lists roof companies under its general contractor registration, with a $100 annual fee and a current driver's license on file. City guidance says contractors must be registered before doing any work.
  2. The roofer pulls a permit. The city's roof permit page says a permit is required on all re-roof projects. If yours is a small repair, ask the city whether it needs one.
  3. Packaged shingles only. Plano's re-roof handout bans unpackaged shingles, often sold as "seconds." Every bundle should arrive wrapped and labeled by the manufacturer.
  4. Check your deed restrictions. The same handout reminds owners that an HOA or deed restriction can add its own rules on color and material. Get the HOA's approval in writing if it asks for one.
  5. Final inspection, roof and attic. The inspector looks at vent piping, rafters, and the water heater and furnace exhaust vents. Keep the permit number and the passed inspection with your house records.

The city publishes a list of registered general contractors. Ask the roofer which name it registered under and look for it there, or call Building Inspections at 972-941-7140.

Two Plano hailstorms worth knowing

June 13, 2012. The National Weather Service office in Fort Worth reported that storms that evening pounded parts of Dallas, Collin and Fannin counties with hail as large as baseballs. Numerous cars and houses were severely damaged, and the agency said early estimates put the cost near $900 million, which would make it one of the costliest hailstorms ever in Texas.

Spring 2016. Four years later, NBC 5 reported that Plano took the brunt of a hailstorm that damaged hundreds of homes and thousands of cars. CoreLogic's review of that spring's Texas hail, published by Insurance Journal, estimated $35.8 million in losses from the March 23 Plano storm, with stones of 1.5 to 2 inches falling along a swath of nearly 50 miles from near Lewisville to east of Wylie.

Storms like these are why many Plano roofs have been replaced more than once, and why a ten-year-old roof here can carry hits from more than one season. A bruise from one storm may not leak until the next one.

Four reasons Plano owners pick up the phone

A permit and an inspection to line up

Because every re-roof in Plano runs through a permit and a final inspection, homeowners want a roofer that schedules both without being reminded. That is a fair question to ask on the first call.

Hail, then a flood of door knockers

After a storm like the ones in 2012 and 2016, out-of-town crews knock on doors. The city's registration list gives you a quick way to tell who has filed paperwork with Plano.

Gas appliances in the attic

Many Plano homes have a gas water heater or furnace venting through the roof. The city's attic check exists for that reason. A good roofer photographs each flue after the new roof goes on.

Older homes near downtown

Plano has locally designated historic districts, including Haggard Park and Downtown. If your house is in one, ask the city's Planning staff about the roof material before you sign.

What to ask a Plano roofer before you sign

  • Under what name are you registered with Plano Building Inspections, and will you pull the permit in that name?
  • Will you schedule the final inspection, and will you send me photos of the water heater and furnace vents where they leave the roof?
  • Which shingle are you installing, by brand and line, and will it arrive in labeled packages?
  • Will the old layers come off down to bare decking, and if rotten boards turn up, what will each replaced sheet cost?
  • Will your insurance carrier email me a current liability certificate, rather than a copy from your truck?
  • Who registers the manufacturer's shingle warranty, and how long does your workmanship coverage last on paper?

Insurance, deductibles and the Plano roof claim

After hail, the order matters. Take pictures from the lawn and from inside the rooms, and open the claim with your insurer before any permanent repair. A roofer can show you what it found and be there when the adjuster comes, but the claim is yours. Texas law keeps it that way by barring a contractor from serving as the public adjuster for a home where it does, or may do, the work.

Expect a roofer's contract paid with insurance money to include a notice that you owe the deductible. That notice is required. A Plano roofer who offers to "eat" or "work in" the deductible is offering to break the law, and the insurer can ask you for proof you paid it.
