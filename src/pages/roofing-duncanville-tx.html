A city that was built in a hurry

On August 2, 1947, residents of a small community southwest of Dallas incorporated a 225-acre city, largely to keep from being annexed by Dallas. The census counted 841 people in Duncanville in 1950. By 1980 the count was 27,781.

Most of that growth came in the 1950s, 1960s and 1970s, driven by urban development nearby. The state historical marker in town notes the rapid growth of those decades, and the old Army landing field nearby became Duncanville Air Force Station, a headquarters for Nike-Hercules missile sites that guarded the area.

What that history means for roofing in Duncanville, TX is simple. Many houses here were built during that boom and are now more than half a century old. The shingles may be fifteen years old, but the decking, the attic ventilation and the flashing around chimneys often date to the year the house was built, or to a re-roof that laid new shingles over old ones.

What an older Duncanville roof can hide

None of these is certain on any one house. They are the questions a careful roofer should answer after looking at yours, in person, from the attic as well as the roof.

Layers

On a house that has had several roofs, ask how many layers of shingles are up there now. A quote that leaves old layers in place is a different job from one that strips to the deck.

Decking

Older homes may have board decking or sheets that have softened around leaks. Ask the roofer to price replacement by the sheet in writing and to photograph any sheet it swaps.

Ventilation

Attic vents added piece by piece over decades do not always work together. Ask what the roofer sees for intake and exhaust, and how its plan changes them.

Flashing

Chimneys, walls and old vent pipes are where aging roofs often leak first. Ask whether the price includes new flashing or reuses the old metal.

Duncanville's permit rule, and whose name goes on it

Duncanville's building inspections guidance follows the building code: work that repairs or replaces a building system needs a permit before it starts, and one or more inspections before it counts as finished. Reroof permits are a routine permit type in the city's weekly reports.

The detail many homeowners miss is the city's warning about who applies. If you pull a permit for work a contractor does, Duncanville says you become responsible for that work. The city recommends that the contractor apply in its own name.

  • Contractors must register with the city before working in Duncanville, through the Citizen Access Portal.
  • Permit applications go through the same portal, or on paper at City Hall, 203 E. Wheatland Road.
  • A permit is valid for six months from the day it is issued.
  • The city lists the 2021 International Residential Code among its adopted codes.
  • Questions: Permit and Inspection Services, (972) 780-5000, open Monday through Friday during business hours.

Hail and wind on Duncanville roofs

Duncanville sits in the same spring storm belt as the rest of Dallas County, and hail and straight-line wind are what most often send people looking for a roofer here. No weather service damage survey of a tornado inside Duncanville turned up in the research for this page, so none is claimed here. If a storm has just passed, here is what to do, all from the ground:

  1. Inside first. Look at ceilings, especially near outside walls and under any chimney. Set out a bucket and move belongings away from a drip.
  2. Photos from the yard. Missing shingles, dents in gutters and vents, and limbs on the roof. Take dated pictures.
  3. Call your insurer. Ask how your policy handles wind and hail, what your deductible is, and how long you have to report damage.
  4. Then a roofer. Have the roofer document what it sees in photos, and ask about a temporary cover if the roof is open.

Five questions for a Duncanville roofer

  • Is your company on Duncanville's contractor register, and will the reroof permit carry your company name rather than mine?
  • How many shingle layers are on my house now, and does your price remove all of them?
  • After looking in my attic, would you change the vents, and how?
  • Is decking priced per sheet in the contract, with photos of each replaced sheet?
  • Will your insurance company send me a certificate of your liability coverage directly?
