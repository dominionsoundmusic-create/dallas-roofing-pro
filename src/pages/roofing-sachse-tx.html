The morning of May 28, 2024

For many people searching for roofing in Sachse, TX, the reason dates to a single Tuesday morning. The City of Sachse reported that a severe storm crossed the area with hail and winds above 70 mph, and that Sachse was hit particularly hard early that morning.

The mayor described significant damage to trees, homes, businesses and city infrastructure, along with widespread power outages. Three days later Oncor still showed 1,142 Sachse customers without power, and in June the city offered a community tool shed to help residents clear damaged trees and debris.

Wind does a different kind of damage than hail. It lifts shingle tabs and breaks the seal strip that holds them flat, sometimes without tearing them off. Seen from the curb, such a roof may seem untouched, then leak in the next sideways rain. Limbs that fell and rolled off can leave cracked shingles or punctured decking behind.

Sachse's re-roof rules, step by step

From the city's residential re-roofing procedures (July 2024) and its SmartGov guidance (2025). Call Development Services at 469-429-4781, 3815-B Sachse Road, to confirm what applies today.

  1. Registration in SmartGov. All contractors and subcontractors must be registered with the city. Since July 8, 2025, permits are filed through SmartGov, and a company must be registered there to pull one. New registrations stay pending until staff approve them.
  2. Permit above five squares. Replacing more than five squares of roof covering, about 500 square feet, needs a permit. The fee listed is $75.
  3. One overlay at most. No more than one layer of asphalt shingles may go over an existing layer.
  4. Code compliance. The work must meet the city's adopted building codes and Code of Ordinances.
  5. Building final. The required inspections end with a final. Keep the permit and the passed final with your house papers.

A suburb that grew fast

Sachse is old as a place and young as a suburb. The city's 2017 development report shows 359 people in 1960 and 20,329 by 2010, and the 2020 census counted 27,103.

That growth curve means a large share of Sachse homes went up in the 1990s and 2000s. Those roofs are at an age where storm damage and normal wear arrive together, so a roofer's first job is often sorting one from the other before anyone talks about a claim.

Why Sachse homeowners call

Shingles in the yard after wind

Storms like May 2024 leave tabs in flower beds and lifted edges up top. Owners want to know if it is a repair or a bigger job.

A limb came down on the house

Mature trees took the brunt of recent storms. A limb that bounced off a roof can crack decking that nobody can see from the ground.

An overlay already on the house

With the city's one-overlay limit, a home that already has two layers needs a full tear-off. Callers want that priced honestly.

Not sure what the insurer will ask

Wind and hail often carry their own deductible. Owners want a roofer's written findings before they call their agent back.

What to ask a Sachse roofer

  • Is your registration approved in Sachse's SmartGov portal, or still pending?
  • Is my house carrying one shingle layer or two, and does the city's one-overlay limit change your plan?
  • Which damage do you think came from the storm, and which is age? Can you show me photos of each?
  • Does my job cross the five-square permit line, and who requests the final?
  • What will you do about lifted shingles that did not blow off but lost their seal?
