Picking a roofer in a city that keeps a list

DeSoto voted itself a city in March 1949, 50 votes to 2, on land first settled in 1847. Today 56,145 people live there, according to the 2020 census, and it is one of the four cities that formed the Best Southwest Partnership in 1986 with Cedar Hill, Duncanville and Lancaster.

For anyone hunting for a roofing company in DeSoto, TX, the useful fact is this: the city does not let just anyone pull a roofing permit. A contractor registers, the city approves it, and only then can it apply. That gives you a simple first filter before you compare prices or shingle colors.

How DeSoto screens a roofing company

  1. It registers every year. The City of DeSoto requires all contractors to register annually. Roofing contractors pay $25. Since September 5, 2025, registration goes through the city's Customer Self-Service portal, and the city says approval normally takes one to two business days.
  2. It shows coverage. The city's registration application asks for a general liability policy naming the City of DeSoto as certificate holder. Ask for a copy of the certificate sent to you by the roofer's insurer.
  3. It waits for approval. DeSoto tells contractors to hold off on any permit application until staff approve the registration. A roofer that is "still waiting on the city" is not ready to start your job.
  4. It applies for the roofing permit online. The city counts roofing among jobs requiring a permit. Ask for the permit number before tear-off.
  5. The city inspects twice. DeSoto requires an inspection before any part of the work is concealed and another when the project is finished. Inspections are requested online by 4 p.m. for the next day.

Building Inspections takes registration questions at 972-230-9610. Rules and fees change, so confirm them with the city before you sign.

The 30-year shingle rule that no longer applies

Some long-time DeSoto homeowners remember being told a new roof had to carry 30-year shingles. They are not imagining it. The city had an ordinance requiring shingles with a 30-year limited warranty.

That changed on September 1, 2019. A state law, House Bill 2439, limits what cities can require for building materials, and DeSoto's own answer to the question says the state law supersedes the old ordinance. In practice, the shingle you choose is now a decision between you, your roofer and the building code, not a city mandate.

That does not make the lowest-priced shingle the right one. Ask any roofer you talk to how the shingles it recommends hold up to hail, whether an impact-rated option would change your insurance premium (ask your insurer that one), and what the manufacturer warranty covers once it is registered.

A reason to call the city: if a salesperson tells you DeSoto still requires a certain shingle, ask the Building Official at 972-230-9621. A wrong answer about local rules is worth knowing before you sign anything.

February 2018: a tornado two blocks long

Not every damaging storm is a big one. Early on the morning of Tuesday, February 20, 2018, a short-lived tornado crossed part of DeSoto. The National Weather Service rated it EF-0 and estimated its peak wind at 85 mph, and a meteorologist described the track as very narrow, only a couple of blocks long.

The damage was real anyway. The City of DeSoto reported about 30 homes with roof damage in the area of South Westmoreland Road and Eagle Drive, and a church and a car wash on Belt Line Road were also damaged. Nobody was hurt.

The lesson for DeSoto roofs is that wind does not have to make the evening news to lift shingles. After any storm with strong gusts, a walk around the house, looking up from the yard, is worth five minutes.

  • Shingle pieces, ridge caps or vent caps on the lawn or in the street.
  • Lighter or darker patches along the edges of the roof, where shingles lifted.
  • Gutters pulled loose or dented.
  • A fresh water mark on a ceiling or around a window.
