A planned town on the lake, now with a permit rule

Roofing in The Colony, TX got more formal on October 1, 2025. From that date the city requires a building permit for roof replacement, and each job ends with a final inspection that reaches into the attic.

The rule lands on a town with a clear starting point. The city's history says the Dallas developer Fox and Jacobs bought land on the eastern shore of Lake Lewisville for a housing development, and the first families moved in during October 1974. By January 1977 more than 3,500 people lived there, and they voted to incorporate. The name honors the Peters Colony settlers of the 1800s.

Houses from those early years have likely had more than one roof already, and the new rule covers the next one. DFW Expert Roofers is a free phone line, not a contractor. It connects your call to an independent, locally owned roofing company that serves The Colony, and that company deals with the city.

What The Colony's re-roof requirements ask for

The city publishes a Residential Re-Roof Requirements sheet. These are the parts a homeowner can watch for.

A permit for the job

Required for roof replacement and for sheathing replacement. The residential application lists a $75 re-roof fee for a single-family home.

The 2024 code

Work follows the 2024 International Residential Code, including its sections on underlayment, shingles, flashing, fasteners and re-roofing.

Underlayment photos

Photos of the installed underlayment, showing all sides and proper lapping, can be submitted online before the shingles cover it.

An attic check at final

The final inspection confirms vent pipes through the roof, rafters, and water heater and air conditioning exhaust vents are back in place.

A signed waiver

The sheet says a waiver stating the same is also required. Ask the roofer who signs it and when.

A registered roofer

All contractors must register with the city, $75 for one year. Permits are not issued while a registration has lapsed.

Roofing calls from The Colony

Storm repairs, smaller fixes on aging roofs and inspections before a sale or a claim are the usual reasons people here call.

Wind did the damage, not just the tornado

On March 8, 2016, a brief EF-0 tornado touched down in The Colony. The National Weather Service confirmed it the next day, and NBC DFW reported the agency's finding that most of the damage that day came from straight-line winds rather than the tornado itself. CBS DFW described roofs torn up, fences knocked down and sheds destroyed, while some homes nearby were barely touched, and the Red Cross helped house several families.

That pattern is common in North Texas. Straight-line gusts can lift a row of shingles or peel back a ridge cap on one street and leave the next alone. If a storm passes and you find shingle pieces in the yard, have the roof looked at even if the house next door seems fine.

Before you sign with a roofer in The Colony

  1. Check the registration. Ask for the company name on its city roofing contractor registration and confirm it is current.
  2. Read the scope. Look for the underlayment, shingle, flashing and vent details the city's sheet covers.
  3. Ask about the permit. Who applies, whether the fee is in the price, and when you will get a copy.
  4. Plan for the photos. Ask whether the roofer will upload underlayment photos or have it inspected in person.
  5. Clear the attic access. The final inspection includes the attic, so make room before the inspector arrives.
  6. Get proof of coverage. Have the roofer's insurance agent send you a current liability certificate.
