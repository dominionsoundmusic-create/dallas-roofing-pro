Dallas-Fort Worth is hail country

North Texas sits in one of the most hail-prone stretches of the country, and the Dallas-Fort Worth area has been hit by some of the costliest hailstorms on record. Most of it arrives between March and June, when spring storms roll across the metroplex in the late afternoon. A single storm can damage thousands of roofs in a few minutes, and the damage is not always obvious from the ground.

Bruised shingles

A hailstone can crack the fiberglass mat inside a shingle without breaking the surface. It looks fine for months, then starts to leak.

Granule loss

Dark round spots where the granules have been knocked off leave the asphalt exposed to the sun, and those shingles age fast.

Cracked or split shingles

Larger hail, or older and brittle shingles, can crack clean through. That is a leak waiting for the next rain.

Damaged soft metal

Dented roof vents, gutters, downspouts and flashing are often the easiest proof that hail large enough to damage the roof fell on your house.

What to check from the ground after a storm

  • Gutters and downspouts: fresh dents or dings in the metal.
  • The air conditioner: flattened fins on the side of the outdoor unit.
  • Cars, mailboxes, fences and window screens: dents, chipped paint or torn screens.
  • The ground below the downspouts: piles of shingle granules washed down from the roof.
  • Ceilings: any new stain, especially in upstairs rooms and closets.

If you see any of these, take dated photos and have the roof inspected. Stay off the roof yourself; a hail-damaged roof can be slick and fragile.

Filing a hail claim

Hail damage is usually covered by homeowners insurance, but you file the claim, not the roofer. The usual order is simple. Have the roof inspected so you know whether there is real damage. Call your insurance company to open the claim. The insurer sends an adjuster, and it helps to have the roofer on site for that visit to point out the damage.

Check your policy for three things before you start. The wind and hail deductible, which in Texas is often a percentage of the home's insured value rather than a flat amount. Whether the policy pays replacement cost or actual cash value for the roof. And the deadline to report damage, since many Texas policies require claims to be filed within a set time after the storm. Do not wait until next spring to have a roof looked at.

Watch out for storm chasers

Within days of a big DFW hailstorm, crews from out of state go door to door. Some are fine. Some do fast, poor work and are gone before the first leak. Be careful with anyone who pressures you to sign on the spot, asks for a large payment upfront, or offers to cover your deductible. Under Texas law, a contractor may not pay, waive or rebate your insurance deductible, and a roofer who offers to is telling you something about how they do business. Ask for proof of insurance and a local address, and get everything in writing.

Replacing after hail? Consider Class 4

If the roof is being replaced, ask about impact-resistant shingles rated Class 4. They hold up far better to hail, and many Texas insurers offer a premium discount for them, which can help pay back the extra cost over the years.

Common questions

How do I know if hail damaged my roof?

You often cannot tell from the ground. Dented gutters, vents or AC fins, granules piled at the downspouts and new ceiling stains are good clues. A roofer can inspect each slope up close and photograph what they find.

Does homeowners insurance cover hail damage in Texas?

Usually, yes, subject to your wind and hail deductible, which is often a percentage of your home's insured value. Your policy also says whether the roof is paid at replacement cost or actual cash value. Read those sections before you file.

How long do I have to file a hail claim?

It depends on your policy, and many Texas policies set a deadline after the storm. Have the roof inspected soon after a hailstorm and report damage promptly rather than waiting.

Can a roofer waive my deductible?

No. Texas law does not allow a contractor to pay, waive or rebate your insurance deductible. Treat that offer as a red flag.
