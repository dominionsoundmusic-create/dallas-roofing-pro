What goes wrong on Dallas roofs

North Texas is hard on a roof from three directions at once. Spring brings hail and straight-line wind. Summer puts the shingles in direct sun at well over a hundred and fifty degrees on the surface. And the clay soil under most Dallas homes shifts with every drought and downpour, which can move framing just enough to open up flashing. Most roofs that need repair here are not worn out. They have one or two weak spots that let water in.

Lifted or missing shingles

Wind gets under a tab, breaks the seal strip, and the next storm peels it off. Caught early, it is a small repair.

Flashing failures

The metal around chimneys, walls, skylights and valleys is where most leaks actually start, far more often than in the middle of a slope.

Cracked pipe boots

The rubber collars around plumbing vents bake in the Texas sun and split after several summers. It is one of the most common leak sources and one of the cheapest to fix.

Hail bruising

Hail knocks the protective granules off and can fracture the mat underneath. It is often invisible from the ground and shows up as a leak months later.

Popped nails

Heat cycling pushes nails back up through the shingle above them, leaving a small hole that lets water in along the nail.

Worn ridge and valleys

The ridge caps take the most sun and the valleys carry the most water, so both wear out ahead of the rest of the roof.

What to do before the roofer arrives

  • Stay off the roof. A wet or hail-damaged roof is slick, and falls from ladders are among the most common home injuries.
  • Catch the water. Put a bucket under the drip and move furniture and electronics out of the way. If a ceiling is bulging with water, keep people out from under it.
  • Take photos. Photograph the ceiling stain, the drip, and any shingles or debris in the yard, with dates. They help the roofer and any insurance claim.
  • Write down when it leaks. Only in heavy rain, only with wind from one direction, or all the time? That tells the roofer where to look.

If a storm has opened the roof, ask the roofer about an emergency tarp. A tarp is a temporary fix, and it should be followed by a proper repair within weeks, not months.

Repair or replace

Most calls end in a repair. A roof with a few lifted shingles, a failed pipe boot or a bad section of flashing is worth fixing, even if it is ten or twelve years old. Replacement starts to make sense when the damage is spread across many slopes, when the shingles are brittle and shedding granules into the gutters, when several leaks show up in different places, or when an asphalt roof is approaching twenty years in the Dallas sun.

If a roofer recommends replacement, ask which signs they are basing that on and what a repair would cost instead. A good roofer will show you photos from the roof so you can see it for yourself.

Protect yourself when hiring

Texas does not issue a state license for roofing contractors, so it is on you to check. Ask for proof of liability insurance, a local address, and references from jobs nearby. Get the scope and price in writing before work starts, along with the workmanship warranty.

After a big hailstorm, out-of-town crews knock on doors across Dallas. Be wary of anyone who asks for a large payment upfront or offers to cover your deductible. Under Texas law, a contractor may not pay, waive or rebate your insurance deductible.

Common questions

How much does roof repair cost in Dallas?

It depends on what failed and how much of the roof is involved. A cracked pipe boot or a few replaced shingles is a small job; flashing around a chimney or wall, or a damaged section of decking, costs more. The roofer sets the price after inspecting, and you should get it in writing before work starts.

Will my insurance pay for roof repair?

Homeowners insurance usually covers sudden damage such as hail and wind, not wear and tear. You file the claim with your insurer, and a roofer can document the damage and meet the adjuster on site. Check your policy for the wind and hail deductible, which in Texas is often a percentage of the home's insured value.

Can a roof be repaired in the rain?

Emergency tarping can be done in bad weather if it is safe. Permanent repairs need a dry roof, so shingles and flashing are usually fixed once the storm has passed.

How do I know if a roofer is legitimate?

Ask for proof of insurance, a local address, and references from nearby jobs. Get everything in writing, avoid large upfront payments, and walk away from anyone who offers to waive your deductible, which Texas law does not allow.
