How to Spot Hail Damage on Your Roof: A Dallas Homeowner's Guide

By Dallas Roofing Pro | DFW Expert Roofers | Roof Maintenance & Storm Damage

If you live in the Dallas-Fort Worth area, you already know that North Texas weather plays by its own rules. One afternoon it's sunny and 85 degrees — the next, a fast-moving storm is dropping golf ball-sized hail on your neighborhood. After a major hailstorm rolls through the DFW Metroplex, thousands of homeowners are left wondering the same thing: did my roof take a hit?

The tricky part? Hail damage isn't always obvious from the ground. You might walk outside after a storm, glance up at your roof, and think everything looks fine — only to discover months later that hidden damage has been slowly allowing water to seep into your home. That's why knowing how to spot hail damage early can save you thousands of dollars in repairs down the road.

In this guide, Dallas Roofing Pro walks you through exactly what to look for, where to look, and when to call a professional — all without having to be a roofing expert yourself.

Why Hail Damage in Dallas Is So Common (and So Costly)

The Dallas-Fort Worth region sits squarely in "Hail Alley," a corridor in the central United States that sees some of the most frequent and severe hailstorms in the world. According to the National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration (NOAA), Texas consistently ranks among the top states for hail-related property damage every single year.

Hailstones as small as one inch in diameter — about the size of a quarter — can cause significant damage to asphalt shingles, gutters, and other roofing materials. Larger stones cause even more dramatic destruction, cracking shingles, denting metal flashing, and compromising the structural integrity of your roof system. Because DFW homeowners deal with multiple storms per season, cumulative damage adds up fast.

The good news: most homeowner's insurance policies cover hail damage. But insurance claims typically require timely reporting and documentation — which is another reason to inspect your roof soon after any significant storm.

Start on the Ground: Signs You Can See Without Climbing

Before you grab a ladder, do a thorough walk-around of your property. You can often find strong early indicators of hail damage without ever leaving solid ground.

  • Dented gutters and downspouts: Gutters are one of the first places to show hail impact. Look for circular dents, dings, or cracks along the gutters and downspouts. If your gutters took a beating, your roof likely did too.
  • Damaged window screens or sills: Hail doesn't discriminate. Pockmarks, tears, or dents on window screens, sills, and trim are tell-tale signs that hailstones hit your property with force.
  • Dented or damaged AC unit: Your outdoor air conditioning unit sits exposed to the elements. Dents on the fins or housing are a reliable sign that hail was large enough to cause damage.
  • Debris and granules in gutters: After a hailstorm, walk over to your downspouts. If you notice a buildup of dark, sand-like granules washing out, those are coming from your asphalt shingles — a major red flag.
  • Dented deck furniture or fencing: Any soft metal surface in your yard can help you gauge hail size and impact intensity.

If you're noticing damage at ground level, there's a very strong chance your roof sustained damage as well. Document everything with photos before you proceed.

What Hail Damage Looks Like on Asphalt Shingles

Asphalt shingles are the most common roofing material in Dallas-area homes, and they show hail damage in a few specific ways. If you're comfortable safely accessing your roof or using binoculars from the ground, here's what to look for:

  • Random dark spots or bruising: Hail impacts leave soft, dark spots on shingles that may look like bruising. Unlike blistering (which is a manufacturing defect), hail bruises feel soft when pressed and are scattered randomly across the surface — not in a pattern.
  • Loss of granules: Asphalt shingles are coated with protective granules that shield the underlying material from UV rays and weathering. Hail knocks these granules loose, leaving bare, shiny patches that expose the asphalt below. This is one of the most reliable signs of hail damage.
  • Cracked or fractured shingles: Larger hailstones can crack shingles outright. Look for splits, fractures, or pieces of shingle that appear broken or displaced.
  • Exposed black substrate: When granules are stripped away and the asphalt mat beneath is visible, your shingle's waterproofing layer is compromised. This accelerates wear and opens the door to leaks.

Keep in mind that hail damage spots are typically random in location and roughly circular in shape. This helps distinguish them from other types of damage like foot traffic wear or tree limb impact.

Check These Other Roof Components Too

Your shingles aren't the only part of your roof at risk. A thorough hail damage inspection should include several other components:

  • Metal flashing: Look at the metal strips around your chimney, skylights, and roof valleys. Hail leaves clearly visible round dents in metal flashing — this is often the easiest way to confirm a significant hail event hit your specific property.
  • Roof vents and pipe boots: Metal vents, exhaust pipes, and rubber pipe boots are vulnerable to hail impact. Check for dents, cracks, or displaced rubber seals around pipe penetrations.
  • Ridge cap shingles: The shingles along the peak of your roof are fully exposed and often show damage first. Cracked or missing ridge cap shingles are a strong indicator of a problem.
  • Skylights: If you have skylights, check the frames and surrounding flashing for dents. The glass itself may also show impact marks or, in severe cases, actual cracking.

Inside Your Home: Interior Warning Signs After a Hailstorm

Sometimes hail damage reveals itself from the inside of your home — especially if the storm was severe or your roof had pre-existing vulnerabilities. Don't skip this interior check:

  • Water stains on ceilings or walls: Brown or yellow staining, especially in upstairs rooms or attic spaces, is a sign that water is penetrating your roof system.
