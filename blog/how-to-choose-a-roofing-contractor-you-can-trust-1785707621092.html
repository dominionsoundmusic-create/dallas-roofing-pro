How to Choose a Roofing Contractor You Can Trust in the Dallas-Fort Worth Area

Your roof is one of the most important — and most expensive — components of your home. Whether you're dealing with storm damage, an aging shingle system, or a mysterious leak that keeps coming back, hiring the right roofing contractor makes all the difference. Unfortunately, the Dallas-Fort Worth area sees its fair share of storm chasers, fly-by-night contractors, and low-quality installers who are more interested in a quick paycheck than a quality job. The good news? With a little know-how, you can spot a trustworthy roofing professional before you ever sign a contract. This guide walks you through exactly what to look for.

1. Verify Licensing, Insurance, and Local Credentials

The very first thing you should do before hiring any roofing contractor in Texas is verify that they're properly licensed and insured. In Texas, roofing contractors are required to register with the state, and any reputable company should be able to provide proof of both general liability insurance and workers' compensation coverage without hesitation.

Why does this matter to you as a homeowner? If an uninsured worker is injured on your roof, you could be held financially liable. If the contractor causes accidental damage to your property and lacks liability coverage, you're left paying out of pocket. Always ask for certificates of insurance and take the extra step of calling the insurance company to verify the policy is active.

Beyond state licensing, look for contractors who hold manufacturer certifications — such as GAF Master Elite or Owens Corning Preferred Contractor status. These designations mean the company has been vetted by the manufacturer and trained to install products correctly, which often unlocks better warranty options for you.

2. Choose a Contractor with a Strong Local Reputation

There's a big difference between a Dallas-based roofing company with years of roots in the community and an out-of-state crew that rolls into DFW after every hailstorm. Local roofing contractors have a reputation to protect. They rely on word-of-mouth referrals from neighbors, Nextdoor reviews, and repeat business from families who've trusted them for years.

Start your research online. Search for roofing contractors in your specific area — Dallas, Plano, Frisco, Arlington, or wherever you're located — and read through Google reviews carefully. Don't just look at the star rating. Read what people are actually saying about communication, timeliness, cleanup, and whether the roof held up over time.

You should also check the contractor's standing with the Better Business Bureau (BBB) and look them up on the Texas Secretary of State's website to confirm they're a legitimate registered business. A company that's been operating in DFW for five or more years with consistent positive reviews is a strong sign you're dealing with someone who stands behind their work.

3. Get Multiple Written Estimates — and Know What to Compare

Never accept a verbal quote or a ballpark number scribbled on a business card. Any professional roofing contractor should provide you with a detailed written estimate that includes the scope of work, materials to be used (brand, product line, and color), labor costs, timeline, and payment schedule.

We recommend getting at least three estimates for any roofing project. But here's the key: don't automatically go with the lowest bid. A suspiciously cheap quote often signals the use of inferior materials, cutting corners on installation, or hidden fees that surface later. Compare estimates line by line and ask questions about anything that seems vague or missing.

Pay close attention to what the estimate says about underlayment, flashing, ventilation, and decking. These are the components that make or break a roof system's long-term performance. A contractor who doesn't mention them in their estimate may be planning to skip them entirely.

4. Watch Out for These Major Red Flags

Knowing what not to accept is just as important as knowing what to look for. Here are some warning signs that should send you looking for a different contractor:

  • Demanding large upfront payments. A standard deposit is typically 10–30% of the project cost. Anyone asking for 50% or more before work begins is a red flag.
  • No physical address or local office. Storm chasers often work out of a P.O. box or a temporary address. If you can't find them after the job is done, you have no recourse.
  • Pressure tactics and "today only" discounts. Trustworthy contractors don't need to pressure you into a decision. Take your time.
  • Offering to waive your insurance deductible. This is actually insurance fraud in Texas and a contractor who suggests it is not someone you want on your roof.
  • No written contract or vague contract language. If it's not in writing, it doesn't exist. Period.

5. Ask the Right Questions Before You Sign Anything

A trustworthy roofing contractor will welcome your questions — not dodge them. Before you commit, here are some important things to ask:

  • How long have you been in business in the Dallas-Fort Worth area?
  • Who specifically will be doing the work — your own crew or subcontractors?
  • What warranties do you offer on both materials and workmanship?
  • How will you protect my landscaping and property during the project?
  • What does your cleanup process look like, and how do you handle nails and debris?
  • Can you provide references from recent jobs in my neighborhood?

Pay attention to how they answer. A contractor who is vague, dismissive, or can't provide references is telling you something important. The right company will answer every question clearly and confidently, because they have nothing to hide.

6. Understand Your Warranty — Before and After the Job

There are two types of warranties you should understand when getting a new roof: the manufacturer's material warranty and the contractor's workmanship warranty. Material warranties cover defects in the shingles or roofing products themselves, while workmanship warranties cover installation errors.

Most standard shingle warranties run 20–30 years, but many manufacturers offer enhanced or lifetime warranties when the product is installed by a certified contractor. Workmanship warranties vary widely — some contractors offer as little as one year, while others offer five years or more. The longer the workmanship warranty, the more confidence the contractor has in the quality of their installation.

Make sure all warranty details are spelled out in your written contract. Know what voids the warranty, who to contact if you have a problem, and how claims are handled. A warranty is only as good as the company standing behind it — which is one more reason to hire a contractor with an established local presence.

Conclusion: Take Your Time and Choose Wisely
