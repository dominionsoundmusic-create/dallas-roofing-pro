What to Do After a Storm Damages Your Roof: A Step-by-Step Guide for Dallas Homeowners

Severe weather is a fact of life in the Dallas-Fort Worth area. From powerful hailstorms and high winds to sudden heavy downpours, North Texas storms can leave your roof battered and your home vulnerable in a matter of minutes. Knowing exactly what to do after storm damage occurs can mean the difference between a quick, covered repair and a costly, drawn-out nightmare. This guide walks Dallas homeowners through every critical step — from the moment the storm passes to the day your roof is fully restored.

1. Wait for the Storm to Pass — Then Prioritize Safety First

As tempting as it may be to rush outside and assess the damage the moment the rain stops, safety must come first. Before you step outside or head up to inspect your roof, make sure of the following:

  • The storm has fully passed. Lightning and high winds can linger even after rainfall stops. Check a reliable weather app or local Dallas news before going outside.
  • There are no downed power lines near your home or touching your roof. If you see any, stay inside and call Oncor or 911 immediately.
  • You stay off the roof yourself. A storm-damaged roof can be slippery, structurally weakened, or have hidden soft spots. Leave the climbing to a licensed roofing professional.

Your safety — and your family's — is worth far more than a quick peek at some missing shingles. Once the area is clear, you can begin your initial ground-level inspection.

2. Perform a Ground-Level Damage Assessment

Before calling your insurance company or a roofing contractor, do a careful walk-around your property to document what you can see from the ground. In Dallas and the surrounding DFW area, the most common types of storm roof damage include:

  • Hail damage: Look for dented or cracked shingles, damaged gutters, and dented metal flashing. Hail damage isn't always obvious from the ground, but pock marks on gutters and downspouts are a reliable indicator.
  • Wind damage: Missing, curled, or lifted shingles are classic signs of high-wind damage. You may also find shingle debris scattered across your yard or driveway.
  • Fallen branches or debris: Tree limbs that have landed on your roof can cause impact damage, punctures, or structural issues.
  • Water damage signs: Check your attic and ceilings inside the home for water stains, dripping, or damp insulation — these are signs that your roof's waterproof barrier has been compromised.

Take photos and videos of everything you observe, both outside and inside your home. This documentation will be invaluable when you file your insurance claim.

3. Prevent Further Damage with Temporary Repairs

If you have visible openings in your roof — such as missing shingles, punctures from fallen branches, or exposed decking — it's important to prevent additional water intrusion while you wait for a professional inspection and permanent repair. This is known as emergency tarping or temporary weatherproofing.

You have two options here:

  • Hire a roofing company to tarp the roof. Many Dallas roofing contractors, including Dallas Roofing Pro, offer emergency tarping services after storms. This is the safest and most effective option.
  • Use interior buckets or towels to catch drips and protect your flooring and belongings from water damage inside the home.

Keep all receipts for any temporary repairs you make. Your homeowner's insurance policy typically covers the cost of emergency measures taken to prevent additional damage — but you'll need proof of what you spent.

4. File Your Homeowner's Insurance Claim Promptly

Once you've documented the damage and taken steps to prevent further water intrusion, contact your homeowner's insurance company to file a storm damage claim. Most major insurers — including State Farm, Allstate, Farmers, and USAA, which are common in the DFW area — have 24/7 claims lines or mobile apps for quick filing.

Here's what to have ready when you call:

  • Your policy number and contact information
  • The date and type of storm (hail, wind, etc.)
  • Photos and videos of all visible damage
  • Receipts for any emergency repairs you've already made

Your insurer will schedule a visit from an insurance adjuster to evaluate the damage. Important tip: You have the right to have a licensed roofing contractor present during the adjuster's inspection. Having a knowledgeable local roofer by your side helps ensure that no damage is overlooked and that you receive a fair settlement.

5. Get a Professional Roof Inspection from a Trusted Dallas Roofer

After a storm, your phone may ring with offers from out-of-town "storm chasers" — contractors who flood local neighborhoods after major weather events and often disappear before the work is properly finished. Avoid these fly-by-night operations. Instead, work with a local, licensed, and insured roofing company that has a proven track record in the Dallas-Fort Worth community.

A thorough professional roof inspection will assess:

  • The overall condition of your shingles, including granule loss from hail impacts
  • Damage to flashing around chimneys, vents, and skylights
  • The integrity of your roof's underlayment and decking
  • Gutter and downspout damage
  • Any pre-existing issues that may have been worsened by the storm

At Dallas Roofing Pro, our certified inspectors know exactly what Dallas-area storms can do to a roof and what insurance companies need to see in a proper damage report. We work directly with adjusters on your behalf to make the claims process as smooth and stress-free as possible.

6. Understand Your Repair or Replacement Options

Once your inspection is complete and your insurance claim is approved, you'll need to decide whether your roof requires a partial repair or a full replacement. This decision depends on several factors:

  • Extent of the damage: If damage is isolated to a specific section of the roof, targeted repairs may be sufficient. Widespread hail damage affecting the majority of shingles typically warrants a full replacement.
  • Age of your roof: If your roof is already 15–20 years old and has sustained significant storm damage, a full replacement is often the smarter long-term investment.
  • Insurance coverage: Depending on your policy, your insurer may cover the full replacement value (RCV) or the actual cash value (ACV) of your roof. A reputable contractor can help you understand what your policy covers.
