# Research notes: /roof-flashing-repair/ (where does flashing go on a roof), Oct 14 2026

- ARMA Residential Manual, Chapter 10 Flashing: step flashing under shingles at each course where a roof plane meets a wall, each piece about 10 inches long and at least 2 inches wider than the exposure, nailed to the deck not the wall; siding brought down at least 2 inches over the vertical leg as counterflashing; chimneys get apron flashing fastened to the deck and counterflashing fastened to the masonry so the chimney can move; ARMA strongly recommends a cricket on chimneys 24 inches or wider.
  https://www.asphaltroofing.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/Residential-Manual-Preview-pages-77-80.pdf ; https://www.asphaltroofing.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/06/ARMA-Residential-Manual-Final-2.14.14.pdf
- ARMA laminated shingle application: metal valley flashing at least 26 gauge G-90 galvanized steel, 16 oz copper or 0.025 inch aluminum; no shingle end joints in the valley.
  https://www.asphaltroofing.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/Laminated-Shingles-Preview.pdf
- IRC R903.2.2: cricket or saddle required on the ridge side of a chimney or penetration more than 30 inches wide (measured perpendicular to the slope), covered with sheet metal or the roof covering material; unit skylights installed per maker's instructions are excepted.
- IRC R903.2.1: metal flashing corrosion resistant, at least 0.019 inch (No. 26 galvanized); eave-to-sidewall diverter flashing (kickout) required in the model code chapter.
- IRC R905.2.8.3: sidewall base flashing continuous or step, at least 4 x 4 inches, directing water away from the wall. Front walls, vent pipes and chimney flashing per shingle maker's instructions.
- IRC R905.2.8.2: open metal valley lining at least 24 inches wide (2021 edition).
- IRC R905.2.8.5: drip edge at eaves and rakes.
  https://codes.iccsafe.org/content/IRC2024V2.0/chapter-9-roof-assemblies
- NWS Fort Worth 100 degree days (heat cycling context): https://www.weather.gov/fwd/d100info

Left out: exact kickout section number (sources disagree across editions; page names the requirement without a number); any claim on how much hail it takes to dent aluminum or copper (not verified).
